## 1.1 Arrival *(Testimony and survey · 850 w)*

**Purpose:** Bring the reader into present-day Udaypur along the road, the village fabric, its remembered names and its legend, and meet the temple last. Close on the distance between the village and its past importance.

**Paragraph plan:**
1. **P1 — The approach.** From about 10 km out on the Ganj Basoda road the hillocks rise on the horizon, and the temple is visible from 5 km. The place is located by road and rail, with the Gazetteer's and Tiwari's distances set side by side. Vijay Manohar Tiwari is named at this first mention. (C01-KB-GEOHER-004, C01-JAGTA-EN-013, C01-JAGTA-EN-011, C01-JAGTA-EN-010, C01-JAGTA-EN-015, C01-GAZ-007, C01-GAZ-008, C01-MADHYABHARAT-002, C01-JAGTA-EN-014)
2. **P2 — The setting.** A village panchayat in Ganj Basoda tehsil, on the eastern Vindhyan range: 1,966 ha, black cotton and sandstone-derived soils, the hill overlooking the low village precinct, and 6,383 people in 2011. (C01-GEOHER-002, C01-INTACH-004, C01-GEOHER-004, C01-KB-GEOHER-005, C01-KB-GEOHER-003, C01-GEOHER-003, C01-INTACH-005)
3. **P3 — The fabric underfoot.** Dry-masonry stone dwellings everywhere; great Paramāra-style gateways with houses pressed against them, turning broad roads into narrow lanes (Tiwari's walk); the fortified royal quarter with its Teen Darwaza. The author calls it a "ruined little town", and Geo-Heritage stresses its village character. (C01-KB-GEOHER-001, C01-KB-JAGTA-HI-001, C01-KB-GEOHER-002, C01-GEOHER-001, C01-JAGTA-EN-009, C01-JAGTA-EN-015)
4. **P4 — Names that remember.** The Ghadiyali (time-keeper) family's house; the mosque residents call the *kachahari*; the remembered formula of 56 step-wells, 52 wells and 12½ lakes; and the black-soil fields where Udayāditya's tank once lay, in Tiwari's telling. (C01-JAGTA-EN-024, C01-PANDE-006, C01-JAGTA-EN-032, C01-GEOHER-012, C01-JAGTA-HI-009)
5. **P5 — Berni Mata.** The vermilion image on the embankment, and the competing stories of a court dancer, a dancer on a thread, and Narayan Vyas's *yakṣa*. (C01-GEOHER-013, C01-GEOHER-014, C01-JAGTA-EN-033, C01-JAGTA-EN-044)
6. **P6 — The temple.** The temple stands in a compound with entrances at the cardinal points; the approach passes through the old town. Daily *darśan* and an annual fair; morning and evening *pūjā*; Beglar's note that Hindus and Muslims "equally reverenced" the *liṅga*. A village now, a place "of considerable importance" in the medieval period. (C01-GEOHER-005, C01-KB-MADHYABHARAT-001, C01-JAGTA-EN-025, C01-JAGTA-EN-030, C01-PANDE-010, C01-PANDE-004, C01-MADHYABHARAT-009, C01-GANGULY-024)

**Conflicts:**
- **Berni Mata:** the court dancer (Geo-Heritage) vs the dancer on a thread whose image is her *samādhi* (residents, via Tiwari) vs a *yakṣa* (Vyas).
- **Distances:** 13 vs 16 km to Basoda; 55 vs about 70 km to Vidisha.

**Transition:** The temple's survival in a village of this size raises the question of what the place remembers, and through which records.

## 1.2 Long memory *(Survey + epigraphy · 850 w)*

**Purpose:** Set out the successive political orders whose buildings and inscriptions stand together at Udaypur. Give each one only as far as the evidence goes, and develop none.

**Paragraph plan:**
1. **P1 — The Paramāra foundation and court.**
   - The central temple stood in a square court with a small temple at each corner. Construction began in V.S. 1116 / 1059 and the flagstaff was raised in V.S. 1137 / 1080.
   - Udaypur was a centre of government and the headquarters of the eastern division, and Devapāla was in residence there.
   - Lūṇapasāka's grant is dated 1171, 1172 or 1173, depending on the source.

   (C07-PANDE-009, C01-MADHYABHARAT-012, C01-HARDY-004, C01-PARTEMPLES-005, C01-GANGULY-005, C01-GANGULY-015, C01-GANGULY-016, C01-JAGTA-EN-018, C01-RAJABHOJ-EN-003, C01-GANGULY-010, C01-CII-010, C01-GAZ-010)
2. **P2 — Its place in architecture.**
   - The three permitted assessments: Hardy's "imperial monuments", Krishna Deva's judgement, and Adhikari's "model".
   - Comparative facts: Ramgarh, about half a century later, follows a similar plan; Nemawar attempted nine storeys and tapered abruptly.
   - The hill's 1,400 m wall enclosing 56 acres.

   (C01-HARDY-002, C01-TEMPLES-IND-001, C01-PARTEMPLES-004, C01-KB-TEMPLES-IND-002, C01-KB-TEMPLES-IND-003, C01-KB-HARDY-001, C07-GEOHER-003, C07-GEOHER-009)
3. **P3 — The Khaljī conquest and the Tughluq mosque.**
   - Khaljī's general overran Malwa's cities in 1305, and the region faced attack under Allauddin Khilji.
   - The tradition of Muhammad Tughluq's order in 1325.
   - The mosque behind the temple, with inscriptions of Hijra 737 and 739.
   - The Aurangzeb attribution, which Beglar reports and Pande corrects.

   (C10-PARTEMPLES-003, C10-GEOHER-001, C01-GANGULY-013, C01-PANDE-005, C01-MADHYABHARAT-005, C07-GANGULY-003, C01-PANDE-003, C01-PANDE-013)
4. **P4 — Mandu and the Mughals.**
   - Sher Khan's Mandu-style mosque, A.H. 894 / 1488.
   - The Shahi Masjid, begun under Jahangir and finished in A.H. 1041 / 1632, with the adjoining mahal, probably the residence of the Mughal governor.
   - The disputed founder of the palace.
   - The surveyed list of monuments.

   (C07-MADHYABHARAT-012, C07-MADHYABHARAT-011, C01-KB-MADHYABHARAT-002, C01-KB-MADHYABHARAT-004, C01-GEOHER-007, C01-JAGTA-EN-017, C01-JAGTA-EN-026)
5. **P5 — From the Scindias to the present.**
   - The brass cover of 1775, its donor disputed.
   - ASI protection of a living temple.
   - The sixth-century cave in Forest Department ownership, inside a protected forest with several departmental stakeholders.

   (C01-MADHYABHARAT-013, C01-PANDE-011, C01-INTACH-006, C01-GEOHER-005, C01-PANDE-010, C07-INTACH-005, C13-GEOHER-004)
6. **P6 — The written record that binds the layers.**
   - Inscriptions in three languages across eight centuries.
   - Pilgrims' records on the pillars and seats of the porches, and a pilgrim's inscription of Samvat 1696.
   - The serpentine slab, reset into the courtyard wall.
   - The disputed count of inscriptions.

   (C01-SERPENT-002, C01-KB-MADHYABHARAT-003, C01-KB-GEOHER-006, C01-KB-SERPENT-001, C01-GEOHER-010, C01-JAGTA-EN-021, C01-JAGTA-HI-002, C01-JAGTA-EN-023)

**Conflicts:**
- **Lūṇapasāka:** 1171 vs 1172 vs 1173.
- **Brass-cover donor:** Khande Rao Appaji vs Bappa Saheb.
- **Palace founder:** Udayāditya vs the Jahangir-era *farmān* and a claimant.
- **Who built the mosque:** Tughluq vs Aurangzeb.
- **Inscription count:** 11 vs 20+ vs about 100.

**Transition:** So many layers raise the question of how far back the written record reaches.

## 1.3 Epistemic discipline *(Records · 800 w)*

**Purpose:** Establish where Udaypur's written record begins and what lies beneath it. Set the case for a deliberate eleventh-century foundation beside the evidence for something older, and follow how the place entered modern scholarship.

**Paragraph plan:**
1. **P1 — The first dated words.**
   - The in-situ record of the 1080 flag-hoisting gives the earliest known date for Udayāditya.
   - The *praśasti* is undated and gives a genealogy, not a date.
   - Two short inscriptions accompany it, one of them dated 1080.

   (C01-PARTEMPLES-005, C01-CII-007, C01-CII-008, C01-KB-GANGULY-005, C01-GANGULY-008)
2. **P2 — Foundation or re-foundation.**
   - Tiwari, following Verma, argues that no inscription of the earlier Paramāras means the town began with Udayāditya, yet also conjectures an earlier military post.
   - Against this: the Bhujariya Talab, a natural reservoir whose bund steps are older than Udaysagar; the dry-stone fortification of an "ancient place"; and INTACH's span from the sixth century.

   (C01-JAGTA-EN-050, C01-JAGTA-EN-042, C01-GEOHER-021, C01-KB-GEOHER-007, C01-KB-GEOHER-008, C01-GAZ-017, C01-INTACH-006)
3. **P3 — Names for an earlier place.**
   - The legends of the chief Bhringraj and Bhrangarajpur, and of Gond territory.
   - The unknown earlier dynasty, and the call for further research.
   - Hand-off to Chapter 3 for the Daśārṇa and Udayagiri associations.

   (C01-GEOHER-008, C01-GEOHER-011, C01-GEOHER-009, C01-GEOHER-022, C01-GEOHER-024, C01-JAGTA-EN-007, C01-JAGTA-EN-008)
4. **P4 — Later texts that remember the place.**
   - A Hoysala record of a seized "Udhapuram".
   - A sixteenth-century inscription that praises the temple.
   - The fragmentary record of 1513, which looks back to the foundation.

   (C01-GANGULY-007, C01-GANGULY-018, C01-GANGULY-020)
5. **P5 — Into modern scholarship.**
   - Beglar's 1871–72 tour report and Cunningham's correction of it.
   - Cunningham's survey reports and Bühler's edition in *Epigraphia Indica* (1888).
   - Garde's 1925–26 find of the second slab, badly abraded and read from an impression.
   - How lightly the archive is still known.

   (C01-PANDE-012, C01-KB-PANDE-001, C01-KB-PANDE-002, C01-JAGTA-EN-020, C01-PANDE-002, C01-CII-009, C01-KB-CII-001, C01-KB-CII-002, C01-SERPENT-002)

**Conflicts:**
- **Pre-Udayāditya settlement:** a foundation from nothing (C01-JAGTA-EN-050) vs earlier works and occupation (C01-GEOHER-021, C01-KB-GEOHER-007, C01-GAZ-017, C01-JAGTA-EN-042, C01-INTACH-006).

**Transition:** The town's first secure appearance is a name, and the name is the next subject.

## 1.4 Naming the settlement (kasba) *(Epigraphy · 750 w)*

**Purpose:** Present the naming of town, temple and tank after Udayāditya as the inscriptions and their editors give it; set out who built it and when, read the *praśasti*'s imagery as praise, and separate this Udaypur from Udaipur in Rajasthan.

**Paragraph plan:**
1. **P1 — One name for three works.**
   - A town, a Śiva temple and a tank, named Udayapura, Udayeśvara and Udayasamudra.
   - Ganguly's "built by Udayādiya in Sam 1116 = 1059".
   - The city thirty miles north of Bhilsa.

   (C01-MADHYABHARAT-001, C01-GANGULY-002, C01-KB-GANGULY-001, C01-GANGULY-004, C01-PARTEMPLES-001, C01-INTACH-001)
2. **P2 — When.**
   - 1059 as the beginning and 1080 as the flagstaff (Patil); foundation and consecration (Hardy); 1080 as the founding (Singh); "latter half of the 11th century" (Gazetteer).
   - Ganguly's doubt about the 1513 source.
   - His hypothesis that Udayāditya was then ruling a paternal territory.

   (C01-MADHYABHARAT-012, C01-HARDY-004, C01-SERPENT-001, C01-GAZ-003, C01-GANGULY-020, C01-GANGULY-021)
3. **P3 — Who, and in what circumstances.**
   - The CII editor's reading of a Nēmaka dynasty, subordinate to Udayāditya, holding Udaipur; Tiwari's "Nemak" kings, and the son of the feudatory Shudrak.
   - The *praśasti*'s hint of Bhoja's end and Jayasiṃha's recovery.
   - The boar that rescued the earth, read as praise.

   (C01-CII-012, C01-CII-002, C01-KB-JAGTA-EN-002, C01-JAGTA-EN-051, C01-KB-CII-003, C01-CII-007)
4. **P4 — Two names for a temple, and a lost tank.**
   - The temple is "Udayeśvaradeva" in the inscriptions; when "Nīlakaṇṭheśvara" arose is unknown, and the CII editor finds the double name "rather curious".
   - The Udayasamudra: ruins a short distance away (Gazetteer), "cannot be identified … must have been a lake or a big tank in the vicinity" (Ganguly), Udaysagar Talab (Geo-Heritage), black-soil fields (Tiwari).

   (C01-CII-001, C01-JAGTA-EN-006, C01-CII-006, C01-KB-GANGULY-003, C01-GAZ-004, C01-GEOHER-021, C01-JAGTA-HI-009)
5. **P5 — Legend, and the other Udaipur.**
   - Ganguly's snake-and-fire founding story, which he calls "an absolute myth".
   - Not the Sisodia Udaipur of Rajasthan.
   - The spellings "Udepur" and "Astpur".

   (C01-GANGULY-011, C01-GANGULY-012, C01-GANGULY-024, C01-JAGTA-EN-001, C01-JAGTA-EN-039, C01-PANDE-002, C01-JAGTA-EN-003)

**Conflicts:**
- **The founding — who:** Udayāditya vs the Nēmaka feudatory reading.
- **The founding — when:** 1059 vs 1080 vs "latter half of the 11th century", with Ganguly's caveat.
- **The Udayasamudra:** identified vs unidentified.

**Transition:** A name in stone is a claim, and the *praśasti* is the largest of them.

## 1.5 Stance *(Evidence · 750 w)*

**Purpose:** Examine the kinds of evidence the rest of the book rests on, through their substance: the *praśasti* as an object and as praise; the dated markers and how sources word them; the accession as a working position; and legend as legend. Close on the land that produced the town. This section discusses evidence, not the book's own method.

**Paragraph plan:**
1. **P1 — The *praśasti* as an object.**
   - Its location: one slab in front of the temple in 1933, against Gwalior Museum in later accounts; the second half found in a Dhimar's house near the Chatua Darwaza, now in Gwalior.
   - Its date: undated, about 1072 (Ganguly), or 1059 (Geo-Heritage).

   (C01-GANGULY-009, C01-GEOHER-006, C01-JAGTA-EN-019, C01-CII-009, C01-JAGTA-HI-003, C01-CII-008, C01-GANGULY-006)
2. **P2 — The *praśasti* as praise.**
   - It is the only complete genealogy from Upendra to Bhoja, yet Vaidya prefers the grants to "a much later" eulogy.
   - Ray doubts the building claims.
   - Other records omit its first four names.
   - Tiwari, following Verma: the eulogy "has nothing to do with succession".

   (C01-GANGULY-008, C01-BHOJA-TIMES-009, C01-BHOJA-TIMES-004, C01-KB-CII-004, C01-KB-BHOJA-TIMES-001, C01-KB-JAGTA-EN-003)
3. **P3 — Two dates and their verbs.**
   - 1059 (V.S. 1116) for the start, and 1080 (V.S. 1137) for the flagstaff: erected, hoisted or repaired, depending on the source.
   - Tiwari's reading of the flag-hoisting line.
   - Kramrisch's plan caption, 1059–1080.

   (C01-MADHYABHARAT-012, C01-PARTEMPLES-005, C01-GANGULY-025, C01-HARDY-004, C01-KB-JAGTA-EN-001, C01-KRAM-1-001)
4. **P4 — The accession, a working position.**
   - Pande: "ascended the throne in 1070 CE".
   - Adhikari: c. 1070–c. 1093, after Jayasiṃha's end.
   - Beside these: 1058–1087 (Patil), 1059–1086 (Ganguly), c. 1059–1087 (Kramrisch), "probably before 1080" to 1086 (Gazetteer), and 1070–1093 (Tiwari).

   (C04-PANDE-005, C01-KB-PARTEMPLES-001, C01-MADHYABHARAT-011, C01-GANGULY-026, C01-KRAM-2-002, C01-GAZ-015, C01-JAGTA-EN-053)
5. **P5 — Legend and survival.**
   - Stories that circulate elsewhere too (Lalitpur), and the flour-grinding woman's temple, which its architecture contradicts.
   - The single-night belief and the hidden-treasure superstition.
   - Two traditions of the temple's survival, Tughluq's wetted gunpowder and Aurangzeb's change of heart, beside the question of local resistance.
   - A closing lead into the land: what country produced this town.

   (C01-GANGULY-024, C01-PARTEMPLES-002, C01-MADHYABHARAT-006, C01-JAGTA-EN-029, C01-JAGTA-EN-034, C01-GANGULY-013, C01-KB-GANGULY-002, C01-PANDE-003, C01-JAGTA-EN-043)

**Conflicts:**
- **The *praśasti*:** its location (in front of the temple vs Gwalior Museum), its date (undated vs about 1072 vs 1059) and its reliability (Vaidya vs Ganguly).
- **The accession:** 1070 vs 1058/1059 vs "before 1080".
- **Survival:** Tughluq vs Aurangzeb vs local resistance.

**Transition:** Chapter 2 turns to the country that produced the town.
