# Chapter 1 — Udaypur: A Small Town with a Long Memory *(Ancient Scriptural References)* — refined outline

**Evidence base:** `evidence_pack.json`, which holds the Chapter 1 section of both evidence maps (185 direct and 33 contextual items). It also holds 14 items borrowed from other chapters' maps, each tagged `borrowed`. IDs such as `C01-GANGULY-006` are the shared key.

**Chapter length:** 4,000 words, set by the drafting brief. Your outline gave 4,000–4,500, so each of its section targets is scaled down proportionally.

**Section names:** unchanged from your outline.

**Conventions:**
- **The town:** *Udaypur* throughout. *Udayapura* is used only for the name in the records. The town is kept distinct from Udaipur in Rajasthan.
- **Diacritics:** Udayāditya, Paramāra, Udayeśvara, Nīlakaṇṭheśvara, Udayasamudra, *praśasti*, Bhūmija, Lūṇapasāka, Devapāla.
- **The testimony source:** *Jagta Hua Kasba*, using the canon spelling. Its author is named once as Vijay Manohar Tiwari, and after that called "the author" or "Tiwari". This is the editor's own knowledge, so it needs no citation. No pronouns are used for the author.

**Routing rules, from the canon:**
- *Jagta Hua Kasba* is used for testimony only, and is always attributed. It is never the source of a medieval fact or a measurement.
- The Gazetteer is used for the modern setting only. Medieval facts come from Patil (MADHYABHARAT), Ganguly, CII, Hardy, Pande and the other monographs.
- *Temple Economics* never appears; there are no Chapter 1 items from it.
- The Chapter 1 section carries no ⚠ items.

## 1.1 Arrival *(Testimony, attributed · 850 w)*

**Purpose:** Put the reader on the road into present-day Udaypur, see the village before the monument, and meet the temple last. Everything comes through the attributed eyes of *Jagta Hua Kasba* and the modern surveys.

**Key points:**
1. **The approach.** *Jagta Hua Kasba* places the town "a hundred and fifty kilometres from Bhopal" (C01-JAGTA-EN-010, C01-JAGTA-HI-001). It also gives a tar road of 16 km to Ganj Basoda (C01-JAGTA-EN-011) and Baretha as the nearest small station (C01-JAGTA-EN-014). These are attributed impressions; the measured distances come from the modern sources (point 2).
2. **Where it sits officially.**
   - It is a village panchayat in Ganj Basoda tehsil, Vidisha district (C01-GEOHER-002, C01-INTACH-004), covering 1,966 ha on the eastern Vindhyas (C01-GEOHER-004).
   - Basoda is 13 km and Vidisha 55 km away (C01-GAZ-007); Bareth station is 6.5 km (C01-GAZ-008).
   - The 1952 survey gives 4 miles from Bareth and about 8 miles from Basoda (C01-MADHYABHARAT-002).
3. **The village as the author finds it.**
   - The author writes that it "cannot even be called a town (*kasba*) today" (C01-JAGTA-EN-015) and calls it a "ruined little town" (C01-JAGTA-EN-009). Geo-Heritage describes its character as a village (C01-GEOHER-001).
   - The Ghadiyali (time-keeper) family's house (C01-JAGTA-EN-024, C01-PANDE-006).
   - The mosque that residents call the *kachahari* (C01-JAGTA-EN-032).
   - Black-soil fields where Udayāditya's tank once lay, as the author describes it (C01-JAGTA-HI-009).
   - Residents' remembered formula of past abundance, "chappan baoli, bavan kuae and saadhe baarah talab" (56 step-wells, 52 wells and 12½ lakes), as reported by Geo-Heritage (C01-GEOHER-012).
4. **The vermilion image on the embankment.** The Bedni/Berni Mata legend is told in parallel versions (see Conflicts) (C01-JAGTA-EN-033, C01-GEOHER-013, C01-GEOHER-014, C01-JAGTA-EN-044). C01-GAZ-011, on the regional *bedni* dancers, is context only.
5. **The living temple.**
   - People come daily for *darśan* (C01-JAGTA-EN-025), and there is an annual fair (C01-JAGTA-EN-030).
   - Morning and evening *pūjā* continue (C01-PANDE-010).
   - Beglar (1870s, quoted by Pande) found the *liṅga* "equally reverenced" by Hindus and Muslims (C01-PANDE-004).
   - The temple is "visible from five kilometres away" (C01-JAGTA-EN-013). The approach passes through the old town (C01-GEOHER-005).
6. **The gap.**
   - The population is 6,383 by the 2011 census (C01-GEOHER-003, C01-INTACH-005).
   - Against that, the town was "once a place of considerable importance" (C01-GAZ-008), "of considerable importance" in the medieval period (C01-MADHYABHARAT-009), and is "now once again only a small village" (C01-GANGULY-024).

**Conflicts to present:**
- **Bedni/Berni Mata.** A court dancer of Udayāditya (Geo-Heritage), against a dancer on a thread across the reservoir whose image is her *samādhi* (*Jagta Hua Kasba*'s residents). Narayan Vyas, quoted by the same book, reads the image as a *yakṣa*.
- **Distances.** Kept light: 13 km vs 16 km to Basoda; 55 km vs "about seventy kilometres" to Vidisha (C01-JAGTA-EN-015). The divergence is noted in a clause, not argued.

**Hand-offs:**
- Livelihoods, services and the custodian dispute go to **C12** ("Udaypur Today"), whose evidence also draws on *Jagta Hua Kasba*.
- The mosque and the palace go to **C7**.

**Transition:** The temple seen from the road raises the question this chapter turns on. What does a village of this size remember, and how?

**Cut for lack of evidence:** "dry-stone houses and courtyards", "carved fragments lying in the streets", shops and scooters. No Chapter 1 item describes these. The scene is built only from the details above.

## 1.2 Long memory *(Survey + Epigraphy · 850 w)*

**Purpose:** Present Udaypur as a witness to successive political orders. Name and date each one only as far as the Chapter 1 evidence allows, and explain none.

**Key points (layers as the evidence supports them; borrowed items are marked):**
1. **Paramāra.**
   - Construction began in V.S. 1116 / 1059, and the flagstaff was raised in V.S. 1137 / 1080 (C01-MADHYABHARAT-012, C01-HARDY-004, C01-PARTEMPLES-005).
   - Udayapur was a centre of Paramāra government (C01-GANGULY-005) and the headquarters of the eastern division (C01-GANGULY-015).
   - Devapāla was "residing in Udayapur" (C01-GANGULY-016), and *Jagta Hua Kasba* gives the record's year as 1229 (C01-JAGTA-EN-018).
   - Lūṇapasāka's grant is dated variously (see Conflicts).
   - **Significance (at most 3 attributed assessments, each cited; the narrator uses no superlatives):** choose 3 of Hardy's "three … imperial monuments" (C01-HARDY-002), Krishna Deva's assessment (C01-TEMPLES-IND-001), Kramrisch's choice of it "as a model" (C01-PARTEMPLES-003), and "the model to be followed by the later architects of Mālava" (C01-PARTEMPLES-004). Krishna Deva's "started in 1059 and completed in 1080" (C01-TEMPLES-IND-001) may also support the dates.
   - The precinct as built: "a small temple at each of the four corners" around the central temple (C07-PANDE-009, *borrowed*). Your outline's "eight subsidiary shrines" has no evidence and is cut.
   - The hill fortification wall: "a long wall that measures 1400 meters" (C07-GEOHER-003, *borrowed*), enclosing "56 acres" (C07-GEOHER-009, *borrowed*). Geo-Heritage's view that the fort complex "existed prior to Udayaditya" is noted here and taken up in 1.3.
2. **Khaljī conquest of Malwa.** Allauddin Khalji's general "overran the cities of Ujjayinī, Mandu, Dhār and Chanderi in 1305" (C10-PARTEMPLES-003, *borrowed*, contextual). Geo-Heritage records that the region "faced an attack in the rule of Allauddin Khilji" (C10-GEOHER-001, *borrowed*). The canon's formulation, "the Khaljī conquest of Malwa, 1305 (AH 705)", is used *only as far as the evidence goes*: the AH 705 equivalence is not in the evidence, so it is dropped.
3. **Delhi Sultanate (Tughluq).**
   - Tradition holds that Muhammad Tughluq ordered the temple razed in 1325 (C01-GANGULY-013, C01-PANDE-005).
   - He "erected a mosque at the back of the temple" (C07-PANDE-006, *borrowed*), which "still contains two inscriptions, dated Hijra 737 and 739" (C07-GANGULY-003, *borrowed*). Also C01-PANDE-013, C01-MADHYABHARAT-005.
   - *The irony is stated once:* the mosque and the temple stand together.
4. **Malwa Sultanate.** A Mandu-style mosque built "by an agent of Sherkhan during the rule of Chiyas Shah Khilji, Sultan of Mandu, in A. H. 894 (A. D. 1488)" (C07-MADHYABHARAT-012, *borrowed*; the source's OCR spells "Chiyas"). *This layer is new to your outline.*
5. **Mughal.**
   - The Shahi Masjid was "commenced in the reign of Jehangir and completed in that of Shah-jahan in A. H. 1041 (A. D. 1632)" (C07-MADHYABHARAT-011, *borrowed*). Your "1616" is not in the evidence and is cut.
   - *Jagta Hua Kasba*'s *farmān* attributes the mosque and the *Mahal Qubba* to Auliya Ibn Abdul Samad under Jahangir (C01-JAGTA-EN-017, testimony).
   - This conflicts with the tradition that Udayāditya built the palace (C01-GEOHER-007) and with a claimant's assertion (C01-JAGTA-EN-026). See Conflicts.
6. **Maratha.** The brass cover of the *liṅga* was given in V.S. 1841 / 1775 by Khande Rao Appaji, general of Mahadaji Scindia (C01-MADHYABHARAT-013, C01-PANDE-011). The INTACH foreword names a "Bappa Saheb" instead (C01-INTACH-006). See Conflicts. "Scindia repairs" has no evidence and is cut.
7. **The present state.**
   - The temple is ASI-protected (C01-GEOHER-005, C01-GEOHER-016) and is still a living temple (C01-PANDE-010).
   - The hill's sixth-century cave is "under Forest Department ownership" (C07-INTACH-005, *borrowed*). The site sits "inside a protected forest" with several stakeholder departments (C13-GEOHER-004, *borrowed*).
   - *The irony is stated once:* two departments, one ground.
8. **The archive that holds the layers together.**
   - The inscriptions are "in Sanskrit, Persian, and Hindi spanning eight centuries" (C01-SERPENT-002).
   - Pilgrims came "from faraway places in Rajasthan", as recorded on the hillock (C01-GEOHER-010).
   - The inscription counts differ (see Conflicts).

*Keep 1.2 an overview:* one or two sentences per layer. The detail belongs to C4, C6, C7 and C10.

**Conflicts to present:**
- **Lūṇapasāka record:** 1171 (C01-RAJABHOJ-EN-003) vs 1172 (C01-GANGULY-010) vs 1173 (C01-CII-010, C01-GAZ-010). The Gazetteer also attributes the 1173 record to Udayāditya.
- **Brass cover donor:** Khande Rao Appaji (Patil, Pande) vs Bappa Saheb (INTACH).
- **Palace founder:** Udayāditya (Geo-Heritage, "it is said") vs a Jahangir-era foundation (*Jagta Hua Kasba*'s *farmān*, and a claimant).
- **Damage and the mosque:** Aurangzeb (popular legend; Beglar) vs Muhammad Tughluq (Patil, Pande) (C01-PANDE-003, C01-PANDE-013, C01-MADHYABHARAT-005).
- **Inscription count:** 11 (C01-JAGTA-EN-021) vs "more than twenty" on the doorways (C01-JAGTA-HI-002) vs "around a hundred" (C01-JAGTA-EN-023).

**Hand-offs:**
- **C4:** the Paramāra regime.
- **C6:** the inscriptions in detail.
- **C7:** the mosques, the palace and the wall.
- **C10:** the Sultanate, Mughal and Maratha transitions.
- **C11:** ASI and modern administration.

**Transition:** So many layers raise the question of how deep the record really goes, and what lies beneath the first one.

**Dropped, and listed as research to-dos:**
- the 1310 residual-Paramāra record;
- the 1645 Qanungo Baoli slab;
- "one square kilometre";
- the AH 705 equivalence.

## 1.3 Epistemic discipline *(Scripture · 850 w)*

**Purpose:** State what the written record actually contains and when it begins. Show that the earliest securely dated references are eleventh-century, and set the "deliberate foundation" reading beside the evidence for something earlier, without filling the gap.

**Key points:**
1. **The first dated words.**
   - The 1080 flagstaff record is the "earliest known date for the king Udayāditya" (C01-CII-007, C01-PARTEMPLES-005).
   - The *praśasti* gives a "complete and exhaustive genealogy" but "does not bear any date" (C01-CII-008, C01-GANGULY-008).
2. **The argument from absence.**
   - *Jagta Hua Kasba* (citing S. K. Verma) reasons that "no inscription of the earlier Paramara kings is found here", so the town "came into existence only in Udayadity's time" (C01-JAGTA-EN-050). This is attributed reasoning, not a finding.
   - "No one wrote its details" (C01-JAGTA-EN-040).
3. **The evidence pulling the other way** (see Conflicts):
   - a tank "which existed before his time" (C01-GEOHER-021);
   - a fort complex "which existed prior to Udayaditya" (C07-GEOHER-003, *borrowed*);
   - "an ancient place", on the evidence of an old dry-stone fortification (C01-GAZ-017; Gazetteer, used here as a survey observation);
   - the author's inference of an earlier military post (C01-JAGTA-EN-042);
   - INTACH's span "from 6th century CE" (C01-INTACH-006);
   - local legends of an earlier chief, Bhringraj or Bhrangarajpur, and of Gond territory (C01-GEOHER-008, C01-GEOHER-011, C01-GEOHER-009);
   - Geo-Heritage's call for "more research … before the Parmars" (C01-GEOHER-024).
4. **Later texts that remember the place.**
   - A Hoysala ("Poysala") record of a seized city "Udhapuram", which Ganguly says "may be the same as Udayapur" (C01-GANGULY-007).
   - A sixteenth-century inscription calls the temple "the most beautiful temple in India" (C01-GANGULY-018). This is quoted as that inscription's claim.
   - The fragmentary 1513 record (C01-GANGULY-020).
5. **How the modern literature found it.**
   - Beglar's tour of 1871–72 (C01-PANDE-012).
   - Cunningham's reports and Bühler's *Epigraphia Indica* edition of 1888 (C01-JAGTA-EN-020, C01-PANDE-002).
   - Garde's find of the second slab (C01-CII-009).
   - Where it thins out: the dynasty before the Paramāras is "lost in history?" (C01-GEOHER-022), and the archive is "Less well known" (C01-SERPENT-002).

**Conflicts to present:**
- **Pre-Udayāditya settlement:** foundation *ex nihilo* (C01-JAGTA-EN-050) vs earlier settlement or works (C01-GEOHER-021, C07-GEOHER-003, C01-GAZ-017, C01-JAGTA-EN-042, C01-INTACH-006).

**Hand-offs:**
- **C3:** the pre-Paramāra landscape. *Jagta Hua Kasba*'s Daśārṇa and Udayagiri associations (C01-JAGTA-EN-007, C01-JAGTA-EN-008) are signposted, not developed.
- **C6:** the editions of the inscriptions.

**Transition:** If the town's first secure appearance is a name, the name is where to look next.

**Flag:** "The Purāṇic register is regional (Avanti, Vetravatī, Ujjayinī)" has **no Chapter 1 evidence**. The section can only state that the evidence base holds no pre-eleventh-century text naming Udayapura. See U9.

## 1.4 Naming the settlement (kasba) *(Epigraphy · 700 w)*

**Purpose:** Show the naming of town, temple and tank after Udayāditya, as the inscriptions and their editors present it. Read the *praśasti*'s imagery as panegyric, and separate this Udaypur from Udaipur in Rajasthan.

**Key points:**
1. **The triad.**
   - "designated all the three works after his own name as Udayapura, Udayeshvara and Udayasamudra" (C01-MADHYABHARAT-001).
   - Another inscription "credits him with the foundation of Udayapur, Udayesvara, and Udayasamudra" (C01-GANGULY-002).
   - Supported also by C01-PARTEMPLES-001, C01-INTACH-001, and Ganguly: "founded the city of Udayapur, thirty miles to the north of Bhilsa. Tradition gives a legendary story" (C01-GANGULY-004).
2. **When.**
   - Construction began in V.S. 1116 / 1059 (C01-MADHYABHARAT-012, C01-HARDY-004).
   - Ganguly notes that the 1059 date rests on a fragmentary record of 1513 "abound[ing] in gross mistakes" (C01-GANGULY-020). He proposes that Udayāditya built the temple in 1059 "when he was ruling his paternal territory apparently from Udayapur" (C01-GANGULY-021). *This is the evidence behind your "territorial lord" framing. It is presented as Ganguly's hypothesis.*
   - Saarthak Singh gives 1080 for the founding of the temple and tank (C01-SERPENT-001). See Conflicts.
3. **The *praśasti*'s image.** The record says Udayāditya "rescued the earth … as the primeval boar" (C01-CII-007). This is quoted as the inscription's claim. *Your model prose's "Sun of Dawn" image has no Chapter 1 evidence; the boar image replaces it* (U10).
4. **Two names for one temple.**
   - The inscriptions say Udayeśvara (C01-CII-001, C01-JAGTA-EN-005).
   - "In the contemporary inscriptions the name Nilkantheshvar appears nowhere" (C01-JAGTA-EN-006); Ganguly has "perhaps the same" (C01-GANGULY-002); the CII editor finds the two names "rather curious" (C01-CII-006).
5. **The tank's afterlife.** See Conflicts.
6. **What memory adds to the naming.** Ganguly's snake-and-fire legend, which he calls "an absolute myth" (C01-GANGULY-011, C01-GANGULY-012, C01-GANGULY-024). The legend is told and the verdict is attributed.
7. **Not that Udaipur.**
   - *Jagta Hua Kasba*'s opening contrast (C01-JAGTA-EN-001).
   - "the glorious Udaipur of the Sisodia dynasty in Rajasthan" (C01-JAGTA-EN-039).
   - Other forms of the name: "Udepur" in Bühler's title (C01-PANDE-002); and the author's own coinage "Astpur" [Sunset-town] (C01-JAGTA-EN-003).

**Conflicts to present:**
- **The founding:**
  - *Who:* Udayāditya (most sources) vs the CII editor's reading of a record. That reading places Udaipur "in charge of a king" of the Nēmaka dynasty, who "constructed a temple" (C01-CII-012, C01-CII-002). *Jagta Hua Kasba* says "the son of this very feudatory … Shudrak" (C01-JAGTA-EN-051).
  - *When:* 1059 begun (Patil, Hardy) vs 1080 founded (Singh) vs "latter half of the 11th century" (C01-GAZ-003). Ganguly's caveat on the 1059 source is given with it.
- **The Udayasamudra:** "ruins of which are seen at a short distance" (C01-GAZ-004) vs "At present, Udayasamudra cannot be identified" (C01-GANGULY-002) vs Geo-Heritage's "Udaysagar Talab, built … in 1059 CE" (C01-GEOHER-021), "on the outskirts of Udaypur village in the north with an area of 71 acres" (C07-GEOHER-043, *borrowed*) vs black-soil fields (C01-JAGTA-HI-009).

**Hand-offs:**
- **C4:** Udayāditya's relation to Bhoja, including the *bandhu* debate (C01-JAGTA-HI-010, C01-JAGTA-HI-011).
- **C6:** the *praśasti* text.
- **C7:** how the endowment worked, and the tank.

**Transition:** A name in stone is a claim. The last section sets out how the book will handle claims.

**Constraints kept:**
- Udayāditya is not said to have ruled Malwa in 1059.
- The *praśasti* is presented as panegyric.
- "Five centuries later" for Udaipur in Rajasthan has no evidence, and is cut (U11).

## 1.5 Stance *(750 w)*

**Purpose:** Make the book's method explicit, using the disagreements the reader has just met. Fix the dated markers the book commits to, and say plainly which questions remain open.

**Key points:**
1. ***Praśasti* as panegyric.**
   - It is undated and a *praśasti* (C01-CII-008). Ganguly puts it "about 1072" (C01-GANGULY-006).
   - Vaidya prefers the grants to "the Udaipur-praSasti of a much later date" (C01-BHOJA-TIMES-009; OCR spelling kept in the quote).
   - H. C. Ray finds "little evidence available to corroborate" its building claims for Bhoja (C01-BHOJA-TIMES-004).
   - See Conflicts on its location and reliability.
2. **Legend is kept apart and labelled.**
   - Ganguly's "absolute myth" (C01-GANGULY-024).
   - "The currency of identical legends at Lalitpur and Udaypur" (C01-PARTEMPLES-002).
   - The flour-grinding woman's temple, "belied by the style of the architecture" (C01-MADHYABHARAT-006, C01-GAZ-012).
   - The single-night belief (C01-JAGTA-EN-029) and the hidden-treasure superstition (C01-JAGTA-EN-034), both as reported.
3. **Testimony is attributed.** *Jagta Hua Kasba* is the voice of the present; its inferences (C01-JAGTA-EN-042, C01-JAGTA-EN-050) are labelled as the author's own.
4. **Committed markers.**
   - **1059** (V.S. 1116), construction begun: C01-MADHYABHARAT-012, C01-HARDY-004, C01-KRAM-1-001.
   - **1080** (V.S. 1137), flagstaff: C01-PARTEMPLES-005, C01-GANGULY-025 ("repaired").
   - **c. 1070**, accession — *a working position, not a commitment.* Pande: "Udayāditya ascended the throne in 1070 CE" (C04-PANDE-005, *borrowed*). Put beside it, attributed:
     - 1058–1087 (Patil, C01-MADHYABHARAT-011);
     - 1059–1086 (Ganguly, C01-GANGULY-026);
     - c. 1059–1087 (Kramrisch, C01-KRAM-2-002);
     - came to the throne "probably before the year A.D. 1080" (Gazetteer, C01-GAZ-015);
     - 1070–1093 (*Jagta Hua Kasba*, C01-JAGTA-EN-053).
   - The draft does not commit to one date.
5. **Questions the book leaves open:**
   - who built the temple (the Nēmaka reading);
   - whether a settlement preceded the foundation;
   - where the tank lay;
   - how the temple survived (C01-JAGTA-EN-027, C01-JAGTA-EN-043);
   - when the name *Nīlakaṇṭheśvara* displaced *Udayeśvara* (C01-JAGTA-EN-006);
   - Udayāditya's relation to Bhoja (→ C4).

**Conflicts to present:**
- **The *praśasti*'s location:** "now lying in front of the Nīla-kantheśvara temple" (Ganguly, 1933; C01-GANGULY-009) vs kept in the Gwalior Museum (C01-JAGTA-EN-019, C01-GEOHER-006). The second half was found "in a Dhimar's house near the Cha[tua Darwaza]" (C01-CII-009, C01-JAGTA-HI-003).
- **Its date:** undated (C01-CII-008, C01-GANGULY-008, C01-JAGTA-EN-052) vs "The year mentioned is 1059 CE" (C01-GEOHER-006) vs "about 1072" (C01-GANGULY-006).
- **Its reliability:** "of a much later date" (Vaidya) vs "the only available record which supplies a complete genealogy" (Ganguly).
- **The temple's survival:** Tughluq gunpowder (C01-GANGULY-013), Aurangzeb's dream (C01-GANGULY-014, C01-PANDE-003), local resistance (C01-JAGTA-HI-005, C01-JAGTA-EN-043). Summarised here, developed in C10.

**Hand-off:** **C2** asks what country produced this town. This is the closing lead.

**Transition:** End of chapter.
