# Chapter 1 — Udaypur: A Small Town with a Long Memory — outline v4 (authored history)

**Thesis:** see `ch01_interpretive_brief.md`. Udaypur's memory is organised around Udayāditya's single act of naming a town, a temple and a tank. Whether he founded the place or renamed one already in use is the question the evidence leaves open, argued in 1.3 together with what each answer would mean.

## 1.1 Arrival *(The town today · 850 w)*

**Purpose:** Show that today's village lives inside the medieval town, and that its memories reach back to the founder.

**Paragraph plan:**
1. **P1 — Opening scene: the hill and temple seen from the road.**
   - **Point** *(tension)*: Every road and rail line to Udaypur passes through a busier neighbour (Ganj Basoda's market, Bareth on the Bina–Bhilsa line), yet the hillocks and the temple announce the place kilometres before arrival: the approach itself shows a village that was once a place of considerable importance and is now a dependant of its neighbours.
   - Role: open on the concrete object that carries the thesis. A village temple of royal scale can be seen from far off.
   - The approach and distances: 13–16 km from Ganj Basoda, 55–70 km from Vidisha.
   - Name Vijay Manohar Tiwari here once, as the eyewitness.

## 1.2 Long memory *(Accumulation around one centre · 850 w)*

**Purpose:** Show that Udaypur's history accumulated inside and beside the founder's precinct, and that the place stayed a seat of administration.

**Paragraph plan:**
1. **P1 — The founder's precinct and seat.**
   - **Point** *(consequence)*: With the eastern division's headquarters at Udayapur and a later king, Devapāla, residing there, the founder's temple court stood at the centre of a governed place, not beside an isolated shrine; it is the centre against which every later layer is measured.
   - Role: set the centre that later layers are measured against.
   - A temple court with corner shrines; work from 1059, flag in 1080.
   - Division headquarters and Devapāla's residence.
   - The Lūṇapasāka grant (1171–1173) with its date variants in a `{{note}}`.

   (C07-PANDE-009, C01-MADHYABHARAT-012, C01-HARDY-004, C01-PARTEMPLES-005, C01-GANGULY-005, C01-GANGULY-015, C01-GANGULY-016, C01-JAGTA-EN-018, C01-RAJABHOJ-EN-003, C01-GANGULY-010, C01-CII-010, C01-GAZ-010, C01-KB-JAGTA-EN-006)
2. **P2 — A model temple.**
   - **Point** *(consequence)*: Builders at Ramgarh followed the Udaypur temple's plan and those at Nemawar tried to surpass it, so the village's chief monument set a standard well beyond Udaypur, which is why its precinct, not the village, defines the place.
   - Role: why this centre drew later investment.
   - The three attributed assessments, brief and named. Ramgarh and Nemawar as followers.
   - The hill wall: 1.4 km, 56 acres.

   (C01-PARTEMPLES-004, C01-TEMPLES-IND-001, C01-HARDY-002, C01-KB-TEMPLES-IND-002, C01-KB-HARDY-001, C01-KB-TEMPLES-IND-003, C07-GEOHER-003, C07-GEOHER-009)
3. **P3 — Conquest inside the court.**
   - **Point** *(tension)*: The stone record shows a partial survival (the central temple kept; a corner shrine and the hall for reading the Vedas taken down for a mosque), while the legends told of the same conquest speak only of what was saved.
   - Role: claim 3, survival by partial appropriation.
   - 1305; a shrine and a hall demolished; the mosque of the 1330s.
   - Its builder remembered two ways, with the detail in a `{{note}}`.

   (C10-PARTEMPLES-003, C07-PANDE-009, C01-PANDE-005, C01-MADHYABHARAT-005, C01-PANDE-013, C01-GANGULY-013, C01-PANDE-003, C07-GANGULY-003, C10-GEOHER-001, C01-KB-PANDE-003)

   - Role: claim 1, accretion.
   - The walled town and its gates; Sher Khan's mosque (1488); the Shahi Masjid (1632); the governor's mansion.
   - The palace's founder is disputed; the dispute goes in a `{{note}}`.

   (C01-KB-MADHYABHARAT-006, C07-MADHYABHARAT-012, C07-MADHYABHARAT-011, C01-KB-MADHYABHARAT-002, C01-KB-MADHYABHARAT-004, C01-KB-GEOHER-011, C01-GEOHER-007, C01-JAGTA-EN-017, C01-JAGTA-EN-026, C01-KB-JAGTA-EN-010)

## 1.3 Epistemic discipline *(Founded or renamed? · 800 w)*

**Purpose:** Argue the thesis's open question, whether Udayāditya founded Udaypur or renamed a place already in use. Set out the firm boundary of the record (1080), the case for each answer, how much weight each can bear, and what each answer would mean. The question is left open.

**Paragraph plan:**
1. **P1 — The firm boundary.**
   - **Point** *(consequence)*: With the praśasti undated and the 1080 flag inscription the earliest secure date for the king, Udaypur before 1080 can be reached only through proposals and traditions, and both answers to the founding question have to be argued from that same thin ground.
   - Role: claim 1. Everything before 1080 is tradition or proposal.
   - The in-situ flag inscription, with the earliest known date for the king; the undated *praśasti*; the short dated inscriptions.

   (C01-PARTEMPLES-005, C01-CII-007, C01-CII-008, C01-KB-GANGULY-005, C01-GANGULY-008)
2. **P2 — The case for an older place.**
   - **Point** *(tension)*: Inscription, tank, reused columns, wall and layers all point towards an earlier place, but each is a proposal ('tentative', 'may belong', 'provisionally'); their agreement in direction gives the case for renaming its weight, and their separate uncertainty keeps it a case rather than a finding.
   - Role: claim 2, framed as the survey's proposals.
   - The hill inscription, tentatively read and assigned to the eighth century; Bhujariya Talab, which "may belong to the 8th or 9th century", its embankment older than Udaysagar; columns provisionally identified as seventh–eighth century, reused; layers read back to the sixth century; the uncemented wall.

   (C01-KB-GEOHER-013, C01-KB-GEOHER-014, C01-KB-GEOHER-012, C01-KB-GEOHER-007, C01-KB-GEOHER-008, C01-GEOHER-021, C01-INTACH-006, C01-GAZ-017, C01-KB-INTACH-002, C01-KB-CII-005)
3. **P3 — The case for a new town, and the remembered masters.**
   - **Point** *(tension)*: The case for a new town rests on an absence of earlier Paramāra inscriptions, which cannot rule out a place under other masters and which its own proponent qualifies with a frontier post; the traditions of Bhringraj and the Gonds remember those masters, but only as people the founder defeated, so memory favours renaming while telling it from the founder's side.
   - Role: claims 3 and 4.
   - The reading from the absence of earlier Paramāra inscriptions, which itself allows an earlier frontier post.
   - Bhringraj and Bhringrajpur, and Gond territory, remembered as conquered.
   - The Daśārṇa and Udayagiri associations are handed to Chapter 3.

   (C01-JAGTA-EN-050, C01-JAGTA-EN-042, C01-GEOHER-008, C01-GEOHER-011, C01-GEOHER-009, C01-GEOHER-022, C01-GEOHER-024, C01-JAGTA-EN-007, C01-JAGTA-EN-008)
4. **P4 — What each answer would mean.**
   - **Point** *(consequence)*: If Udayāditya founded Udaypur, its long memory is the memory of one eleventh-century royal project; if he renamed it, the naming was an act of appropriation that laid his name over an older place. The later echoes (Udhapuram, the sixteenth-century praise, the record of 1513) all come after the act and cannot decide between the two.
   - Role: claim 5 (`[INT]`-led, anchored in a cited recapitulation).
   - Founded: a royal creation of the 1050s. Renamed: an act of appropriation over an older place.
   - Later echoes that cannot settle it: Udhapuram, a possible identification; the sixteenth-century praise; the retrospective record of 1513.

   (C01-GANGULY-007, C01-GANGULY-018, C01-GANGULY-020, C01-PARTEMPLES-005)
5. **P5 — Why the question stays open.**
   - **Point** *(cause)*: The question stays open because the founding is known mainly through the founder's own praise, studied only since the 1870s, with its second half largely abraded: the record that could settle founded-or-renamed is the record least able to judge it.
   - Role: claim 6, and the close.
   - The 1871–72 tour; the 1888 edition; the second slab found in 1925–26, abraded.
   - `[INT]`: the founder is documented mainly through his own panegyric, so the question cannot be closed from it.

   (C01-PANDE-012, C01-KB-PANDE-001, C01-KB-PANDE-002, C01-JAGTA-EN-020, C01-PANDE-002, C01-CII-009, C01-KB-CII-001, C01-KB-CII-002, C01-SERPENT-002)

## 1.4 Naming the settlement (kasba) *(The name · 750 w)*

**Purpose:** Show that the single name is the foundation's most durable monument, and that its dates and holders show what kind of founder Udayāditya was.

**Paragraph plan:**
1. **P1 — One name, three works.**
   - **Point** *(thesis)*: What the inscriptions record as Udayāditya's foundation is an act of naming as much as of building: three works, a town, a temple and a tank, under one name, and the name is the one the village still answers to.
   - Role: claim 1.
   - Udayapura, Udayeśvara, Udayasamudra; Sam 1116 (1059); thirty miles north of Bhilsa; Udaypur today.

   (C01-MADHYABHARAT-001, C01-GANGULY-002, C01-KB-GANGULY-001, C01-GANGULY-004, C01-PARTEMPLES-001, C01-INTACH-001, C01-CII-002)
2. **P2 — What the dates decide.**
   - **Point** *(consequence)*: The dates bear on the founder's standing as much as on the building's age: on the paternal-territory hypothesis, Udaypur began as the seat of a lord ruling his own lands in 1059 and became a king's foundation only later.
   - Role: claim 2. A provincial seat before a royal city.
   - 1059 and 1080; the 1513 retrospective; the paternal-territory reading. The readings go in a `{{note}}`.

   (C01-MADHYABHARAT-012, C01-HARDY-004, C01-SERPENT-001, C01-GAZ-003, C01-GANGULY-020, C01-GANGULY-021)
3. **P3 — A local house under the royal name.**
   - **Point** *(tension)*: The town that carries the king's name appears in its own inscription as the capital of the Nēmaka house subordinate to him, so a royal name sat over a local line's seat; the praśasti's image of the king as the boar who lifted the earth belongs to praise, not to the record of who governed here.
   - Role: claim 2, continued.
   - The Nēmaka capital; the feudatory Shudrak's son.
   - The *praśasti*'s boar image, read as praise.

   (C01-CII-012, C01-CII-002, C01-KB-JAGTA-EN-002, C01-JAGTA-EN-051, C01-KB-CII-003, C01-CII-007, C01-KB-CII-006)
4. **P4 — Names that drifted.**
   - **Point** *(comparison)*: The temple's name drifted from Udayeśvara to Nīlakaṇṭheśvara and the tank can no longer be securely identified, yet Udayapura held as Udaypur: the founder's name survived where it named the whole place, not its parts.
   - Role: claim 3.
   - Udayeśvaradeva becomes Nīlakaṇṭheśvara.
   - The tank is located variously; the identifications go in a `{{note}}`.

   (C01-CII-001, C01-JAGTA-EN-006, C01-CII-006, C01-KB-GANGULY-003, C01-GAZ-004, C01-GEOHER-021, C01-JAGTA-HI-009, C01-KB-MADHYABHARAT-008)
5. **P5 — Pride and neglect in a name.**
   - **Point** *(tension)*: The name that keeps the foundation alive also obscures it: overshadowed by Rajasthan's Udaipur, spelled 'Udepur' in an early edition of its praśasti and punned by a local writer as 'Astpur', the sunset-town, the founder's most durable monument now stands for the place's eclipse.
   - Role: claim 4, and the close.
   - The snake-and-fire myth; the Udaipur of Rajasthan; "Udepur"; "Astpur".

   (C01-GANGULY-011, C01-GANGULY-012, C01-GANGULY-024, C01-JAGTA-EN-001, C01-JAGTA-EN-039, C01-PANDE-002, C01-JAGTA-EN-003)

## 1.5 Stance *(What carries the story · 750 w)*

**Purpose:** Show that the records carrying Udaypur's story are a founder's praise, a pair of dates and a body of legends, all organised around the founding act.

**Paragraph plan:**
1. **P1 — A scattered slab.**
   - **Point** *(consequence)*: The praśasti's two halves came to light apart, one by the temple and one in a house near the Chatua Darwaza, and both now lie in Gwalior; Udaypur's chief written witness to its founding is broken, kept outside the place it describes, and undated, so even its time is a reading.
   - Role: claim 1, the object.
   - Two slabs: one formerly in front of the temple, one from a Dhimar's house near the Chatua Darwaza; now in Gwalior.
   - Undated, assigned to about 1072 or 1059; the readings go in a `{{note}}`.

   (C01-GEOHER-006, C01-GANGULY-009, C01-JAGTA-EN-019, C01-CII-009, C01-JAGTA-HI-003, C01-CII-008, C01-GANGULY-006)
2. **P2 — The dynasty's self-portrait.**
   - **Point** *(tension)*: The praśasti is at once the dynasty's only complete genealogy and a panegyric whose earliest names are missing from other records and whose building claims no other source confirms, so the founder appears vivid in praise and thin in fact.
   - Role: claim 1, the reading.
   - The only complete genealogy; the mythical origin; earliest names absent elsewhere; uncorroborated building claims.
   - `[INT]`: vivid in praise, thin in fact. The doubts go in a `{{note}}`.

   (C01-GANGULY-008, C01-KB-CII-004, C01-GANGULY-006, C01-BHOJA-TIMES-009, C01-KB-BHOJA-TIMES-001, C01-BHOJA-TIMES-004, C01-KB-JAGTA-EN-003, C01-KB-JAGTA-EN-011)
3. **P3 — Two dates.**
   - **Point** *(tension)*: The two dates are the founding's only fixed points, and even the second is read three ways (a flag-staff erected, a flag hoisted, a flag-staff repaired), so the tidy caption '1059–1080' on the temple's plan claims more than the inscriptions settle.
   - Role: the documentary anchors.
   - V.S. 1116 and V.S. 1137; the flag erected, hoisted or repaired; the plan captioned 1059–1080.

   (C01-MADHYABHARAT-012, C01-PARTEMPLES-005, C01-GANGULY-025, C01-HARDY-004, C01-KB-JAGTA-EN-001, C01-KRAM-1-001, C01-KB-JAGTA-EN-005)

   - **Point** *(consequence)*: If Udayāditya came to the throne around 1070, the work of 1059 began a decade before his kingship and Udaypur belongs to his rise rather than his reign; an accession of 1058–59 would make it a new king's first work, and the choice between the two changes what kind of founding this was.
   - Role: claim 2.
   - c. 1070 as the working position against 1058/1059.
   - `[INT]`: a project of his rise. Who argues what goes in a `{{note}}`.

   (C04-PANDE-005, C01-KB-PARTEMPLES-001, C01-MADHYABHARAT-011, C01-GANGULY-026, C01-KRAM-2-002, C01-MADHYABHARAT-012, C01-GAZ-015, C01-JAGTA-EN-053, C01-KB-GANGULY-007, C01-KB-GANGULY-008, C01-KB-JAGTA-EN-012)
5. **P5 — Legends that explain survival.**
   - **Point** *(tension)*: The survival legends account for what still stands, and even the miracle of the wetted gunpowder concedes that a hall became a mosque; none is corroborated, so local memory has turned a partial survival into a deliverance.
   - Role: claim 3, and the close.
