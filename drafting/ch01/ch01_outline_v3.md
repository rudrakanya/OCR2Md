## 1.1 Arrival *(The town today · 850 w)*

**Purpose:** The reader arrives in present-day Udaypur, a village of stone houses, old gateways, remembered names and a living legend, and meets the temple last. The section closes on the distance between the village it is now and the place of importance it was.

**Paragraph plan:**
1. **P1 — Udaypur rises out of a plain of fields.**
   - Topic sentence: *Udaypur announces itself from far off: its hillocks show on the horizon about ten kilometres out on the Ganj Basoda road.*
   - The temple comes into view at about five kilometres. Vijay Manohar Tiwari, who walked in, is named here once, as the eyewitness.
   - The town lies some 13–16 km from Ganj Basoda and some 55–70 km from Vidisha. These are ranges: the footnote cites both items.
   - The nearest railway stations are Bareth (about 6.5 km) and Ganj Basoda.

   (C01-KB-GEOHER-004, C01-JAGTA-EN-013, C01-GAZ-007, C01-JAGTA-EN-011, C01-JAGTA-EN-015, C01-GAZ-008, C01-JAGTA-EN-014, C01-JAGTA-EN-010, C01-MADHYABHARAT-002)
2. **P2 — It is a village now.**
   - Topic sentence: *Today Udaypur is a village panchayat in Ganj Basoda tehsil, on the eastern Vindhyan range, home to 6,383 people at the 2011 census.*
   - 1,966 ha.
   - The low village precinct sits beneath the hill.
   - Black-soil and sandstone fields.
   - 6,383 people in 2011.

   (C01-GEOHER-002, C01-INTACH-004, C01-GEOHER-004, C01-KB-GEOHER-005, C01-KB-GEOHER-003, C01-GEOHER-003, C01-INTACH-005, C01-KB-GEOHER-009, C01-KB-GEOHER-010, C01-KB-GAZ-002) — v3 adds about 1,247 houses, the census split by sex and literacy, and the village's schools, dispensaries, post office and Monday market
3. **P3 — Its streets are built of its own stone.**
   - Topic sentence: *Udaypur's lanes run between dry-stone houses and the remains of great gateways.*
   - Houses press against Paramāra-style gates, so that broad roads have become narrow lanes.
   - The fortified royal quarter is entered by the Teen Darwaza, and its wall survives in stretches.
   - A "ruined little town": one short quotation at most.

   (C01-KB-GEOHER-001, C01-KB-JAGTA-HI-001, C01-KB-GEOHER-002, C01-GEOHER-001, C01-JAGTA-EN-009, C01-JAGTA-EN-015, C01-KB-JAGTA-HI-002, C01-KB-JAGTA-HI-003, C01-KB-INTACH-001) — v3 adds shop rows filled with centuries of rubble beside living shops, and a twelve-courtyard house still used by its family
4. **P4 — Its houses and mosques keep old names.**
   - Topic sentence: *Udaypur's names remember what its buildings once did.*
   - The time-keepers' house near the temple, whose family recall the bells and gongs of the daily rites.
   - The mosque residents still call the *kachahari* (court).
   - The remembered tally of 56 step-wells, 52 wells and 12½ lakes.
   - The black-soil fields where Udayāditya's tank is said to have lain.

   (C01-PANDE-006, C01-JAGTA-EN-024, C01-JAGTA-EN-032, C01-GEOHER-012, C01-JAGTA-HI-009)
5. **P5 — On the old embankment sits Berni Mata.**
   - Topic sentence: *On an embankment of the vanished reservoir sits a vermilion-smeared image the village calls Berni Mata.*
   - Three stories compete, and all are given as stories:
     - a dancer at Udayāditya's court;
     - a dancer who fell crossing the water on a thread, the image being her *samādhi*;
     - a *yakṣa*, as one scholar identified it on close inspection.

   (C01-GEOHER-013, C01-GEOHER-014, C01-JAGTA-EN-033, C01-JAGTA-EN-044)
6. **P6 — The temple is still a working temple.**
   - Topic sentence: *Through the village one reaches the temple, which is still a place of daily worship.*
   - It stands in a walled compound with entrances at the cardinal points.
   - Morning and evening *pūjā*, daily *darśan* and an annual fair.
   - In the nineteenth century Hindus and Muslims alike reverenced its *liṅga* (Beglar, named because his date matters).
   - It is a village now, but a place "of considerable importance" in the medieval period.

   (C01-GEOHER-005, C01-KB-MADHYABHARAT-001, C01-JAGTA-EN-025, C01-JAGTA-EN-030, C01-PANDE-010, C01-PANDE-004, C01-MADHYABHARAT-009, C01-GANGULY-024, C01-KB-JAGTA-HI-004) — v3 adds the daily visitors who come for darśan

**Conflicts:**
- **Berni Mata:** court dancer vs dancer on a thread vs *yakṣa*. These are traditions and one identification, framed around the image.
- **Distances:** 13–16 km to Basoda and 55–70 km to Vidisha, given as ranges, not a debate.

**Transition:** A village of this size keeps a temple of this scale, so the next question is what successive orders left in it.

## 1.2 Long memory *(The layers in the town · 850 w)*

**Purpose:** Udaypur holds the buildings and inscriptions of several successive orders within a small compass:
- a Paramāra foundation and seat of government;
- the Khaljī conquest and a Tughluq mosque;
- Malwa-sultanate and Mughal building;
- Scindia-era patronage;
- protection by the ASI.

Each layer is given only as far as the evidence goes.

**Paragraph plan:**
1. **P1 — It began as a Paramāra temple-town and seat of government.**
   - Topic sentence: *Udaypur's oldest standing layer is Paramāra.*
   - A central temple in a square court with corner shrines.
   - Construction begun in V.S. 1116 (1059), and the flagstaff raised in V.S. 1137 (1080).
   - The headquarters of an eastern division, where Devapāla resided.
   - Lūṇapasāka's grant, dated variously to 1171–1173.

   (C07-PANDE-009, C01-MADHYABHARAT-012, C01-HARDY-004, C01-PARTEMPLES-005, C01-GANGULY-005, C01-GANGULY-015, C01-GANGULY-016, C01-JAGTA-EN-018, C01-RAJABHOJ-EN-003, C01-GANGULY-010, C01-CII-010, C01-GAZ-010)
2. **P2 — Its temple set a standard for others.**
   - Topic sentence: *The temple Udaypur received was among the dynasty's most ambitious works.*
   - The chapter's three attributed assessments (Hardy, Krishna Deva, Adhikari), named because they are judgements.
   - Ramgarh follows a similar plan about half a century later, and Nemawar attempted nine storeys.
   - The hill's 1,400 m wall encloses 56 acres.

   (C01-HARDY-002, C01-TEMPLES-IND-001, C01-PARTEMPLES-004, C01-KB-TEMPLES-IND-002, C01-KB-TEMPLES-IND-003, C01-KB-HARDY-001, C07-GEOHER-003, C07-GEOHER-009)
3. **P3 — Conquest brought a mosque into the temple's precinct.**
   - Topic sentence: *In 1305 Malwa's cities fell to Khaljī's general, and Udaypur gained a mosque behind its temple.*
   - The mosque's inscriptions of Hijra 737 and 739.
   - Who built it is told two ways: tradition credits Muhammad Tughluq's order of 1325, and another account names Aurangzeb. Frame this around the mosque.

   (C10-PARTEMPLES-003, C10-GEOHER-001, C01-GANGULY-013, C01-PANDE-005, C01-MADHYABHARAT-005, C07-GANGULY-003, C01-PANDE-003, C01-PANDE-013)
4. **P4 — Sultanate and Mughal building followed.**
   - Topic sentence: *Udaypur went on building under Mandu and the Mughals.*
   - A Mandu-style mosque of A.H. 894 (1488).
   - The Shahi Masjid, finished in A.H. 1041 (1632), with the adjoining mahal, probably the Mughal governor's residence. Who founded the palace is disputed.

   (C07-MADHYABHARAT-012, C07-MADHYABHARAT-011, C01-KB-MADHYABHARAT-002, C01-KB-MADHYABHARAT-004, C01-GEOHER-007, C01-JAGTA-EN-017, C01-JAGTA-EN-026, C01-KB-MADHYABHARAT-006, C01-KB-GEOHER-011) — v3 adds the walled town with its gates and Sher Khan's mosque outside the eastern gate, and the ruined fortified palace, seat of successive rulers
5. **P5 — Later patrons and the state cared for it.**
   - Topic sentence: *In the last two and a half centuries Udaypur's temple passed from princely patronage to state protection.*
   - A brass cover of 1775, its donor disputed (Khande Rao Appaji or Bappa Saheb).
   - ASI protection of a living temple.
   - The sixth-century cave on Forest Department land.

   (C01-MADHYABHARAT-013, C01-PANDE-011, C01-INTACH-006, C01-GEOHER-005, C01-PANDE-010, C07-INTACH-005, C13-GEOHER-004, C01-KB-MADHYABHARAT-007, C01-KB-JAGTA-EN-004) — v3 adds the liṅga's brass sheet with a face in relief, and the Scindia-era maintenance under Garde
6. **P6 — The town's walls carry eight centuries of writing.**
   - Topic sentence: *Udaypur's stones carry writing in three languages across some eight centuries.*
   - Pilgrims' records on the porch pillars and seats, including one of Samvat 1696.
   - The serpentine slab, reset into the courtyard wall.
   - The number of inscriptions is put at 11, at more than 20, or at about 100.

   (C01-SERPENT-002, C01-KB-MADHYABHARAT-003, C01-KB-GEOHER-006, C01-KB-SERPENT-001, C01-GEOHER-010, C01-JAGTA-EN-021, C01-JAGTA-HI-002, C01-JAGTA-EN-023)

**Conflicts:**
- **The Lūṇapasāka grant:** 1171–1173.
- **The brass cover's donor:** Appaji or Bappa Saheb.
- **The palace's founder:** disputed.
- **The mosque:** Tughluq or Aurangzeb.
- **The number of inscriptions:** 11, 20+ or about 100.

Each is framed around the object.

**Transition:** So many layers lead back to the question of how far back the written record of the place reaches.

## 1.3 Epistemic discipline *(How far back the record goes · 800 w)*

**Purpose:** Udaypur's written record begins in the eleventh century. Beneath it lie signs of something older: a natural reservoir, a dry-stone fortification, a cave, and remembered older names. Later centuries and modern scholarship kept the place in view.

**Paragraph plan:**
1. **P1 — The earliest dated words.**
   - Topic sentence: *Udaypur's earliest securely dated words belong to 1080, though a short hill inscription has been tentatively assigned to the eighth century.*
   - The in-situ record of the flag-hoisting.
   - The undated *praśasti*, which gives a genealogy rather than a date.
   - Two short inscriptions beside it, one of them dated 1080.

   (C01-PARTEMPLES-005, C01-CII-007, C01-CII-008, C01-KB-GANGULY-005, C01-GANGULY-008)
2. **P2 — There are signs of an older place.**
   - Topic sentence: *The ground at Udaypur suggests a place older than its inscriptions.*
   - Bhujariya Talab, a natural reservoir whose embankment steps are older than Udaysagar.
   - The dry-stone fortification of an "ancient place".
   - Occupation reaching back to the sixth century.
   - Against this, a view that the absence of earlier Paramāra inscriptions means the town began with Udayāditya, a view that still allows an earlier military post. Frame both around the town's beginning.

   (C01-JAGTA-EN-050, C01-JAGTA-EN-042, C01-GEOHER-021, C01-KB-GEOHER-007, C01-KB-GEOHER-008, C01-GAZ-017, C01-INTACH-006, C01-KB-GEOHER-013, C01-KB-GEOHER-014, C01-KB-GEOHER-012) — v3 adds the two-line hill inscription tentatively read and assigned to the eighth century, a proposed 8th–9th-century date for Bhujariya Talab, and temple-column fragments reused in a hill stepwell's embankment
3. **P3 — Older names are remembered.**
   - Topic sentence: *Local memory gives Udaypur older names and older masters.*
   - The chief Bhringraj and Bhrangarajpur.
   - Gond territory.
   - An unknown earlier dynasty.
   - The Daśārṇa and Udayagiri associations are handed to Chapter 3.

   (C01-GEOHER-008, C01-GEOHER-011, C01-GEOHER-009, C01-GEOHER-022, C01-GEOHER-024, C01-JAGTA-EN-007, C01-JAGTA-EN-008)
4. **P4 — Later centuries remembered the place.**
   - Topic sentence: *Udaypur stayed in the record after its founders.*
   - An "Udhapuram" seized in a Hoysala record.
   - A sixteenth-century inscription praising the temple.
   - A fragmentary record of 1513 that looks back to the foundation.

   (C01-GANGULY-007, C01-GANGULY-018, C01-GANGULY-020)
5. **P5 — Modern scholarship found it.**
   - Topic sentence: *Udaypur entered modern scholarship in the 1870s and is still lightly known.*
   - The 1871–72 tour report and its correction.
   - The survey reports and the edition of the *praśasti* in 1888.
   - The second slab, found in 1925–26 and badly abraded.
   - How thin the archive remains.

   (C01-PANDE-012, C01-KB-PANDE-001, C01-KB-PANDE-002, C01-JAGTA-EN-020, C01-PANDE-002, C01-CII-009, C01-KB-CII-001, C01-KB-CII-002, C01-SERPENT-002)

**Conflicts:**
- **The town's beginning:** a foundation from nothing vs older works and occupation, framed around the place. The eighth-century reading and the dating of the Talab are tentative; present them as proposals by the survey, the one place in 1.3 where naming the source matters.

**Transition:** The town's first secure appearance is a name, and the name is the next subject.

## 1.4 Naming the settlement (kasba) *(The name · 750 w)*

**Purpose:** In the eleventh century a town, a Śiva temple and a tank received one founder's name: Udayapura, Udayeśvara and Udayasamudra. This covers when and by whom they were founded, what became of the names, and how this Udaypur differs from Udaipur in Rajasthan.

**Paragraph plan:**
1. **P1 — One name for three works.**
   - Topic sentence: *A town, a temple and a tank took one name.*
   - Udayapura, Udayeśvara, Udayasamudra.
   - The temple was built in Sam 1116 (1059).
   - The city lies some thirty miles north of Bhilsa.

   (C01-MADHYABHARAT-001, C01-GANGULY-002, C01-KB-GANGULY-001, C01-GANGULY-004, C01-PARTEMPLES-001, C01-INTACH-001)
2. **P2 — When.**
   - Topic sentence: *The foundation is dated between 1059 and 1080.*
   - 1059 marks the start and 1080 the flagstaff (foundation and consecration). One account takes 1080 as the founding; another puts the founding generally in "the latter half of the 11th century".
   - The late source of 1513 is doubted.
   - Udayāditya may then have held only a paternal territory. Name the historian here, because this is his hypothesis.

   (C01-MADHYABHARAT-012, C01-HARDY-004, C01-SERPENT-001, C01-GAZ-003, C01-GANGULY-020, C01-GANGULY-021)
3. **P3 — Who.**
   - Topic sentence: *Udaypur's founding is tied to Udayāditya, though one reading places a feudatory house there.*
   - The Nēmaka dynasty, subordinate to Udayāditya; local "Nemak" kings; the son of the feudatory Shudrak.
   - The *praśasti*'s hint of Bhoja's end and Jayasiṃha's recovery.
   - The boar that rescued the earth, read as praise.

   (C01-CII-012, C01-CII-002, C01-KB-JAGTA-EN-002, C01-JAGTA-EN-051, C01-KB-CII-003, C01-CII-007)
4. **P4 — Two names for the temple, and a tank that is lost.**
   - Topic sentence: *The temple has had two names, and its tank has vanished.*
   - Udayeśvaradeva in the inscriptions; when Nīlakaṇṭheśvara arose is unknown, and the double name is "rather curious".
   - The Udayasamudra is located variously: as ruins a short distance away, as a tank that cannot now be identified, as Udaysagar Talab, or as black-soil fields.

   (C01-CII-001, C01-JAGTA-EN-006, C01-CII-006, C01-KB-GANGULY-003, C01-GAZ-004, C01-GEOHER-021, C01-JAGTA-HI-009)
5. **P5 — A legend, and the other Udaipur.**
   - Topic sentence: *Udaypur has a founding legend and an unrelated namesake.*
   - The snake-and-fire story, which a historian called "an absolute myth".
   - It is not the Sisodia Udaipur of Rajasthan.
   - The older spellings "Udepur" and "Astpur".

   (C01-GANGULY-011, C01-GANGULY-012, C01-GANGULY-024, C01-JAGTA-EN-001, C01-JAGTA-EN-039, C01-PANDE-002, C01-JAGTA-EN-003)

**Conflicts:**
- **The founding — when:** 1059 vs 1080 vs "latter half of the 11th century".
- **The founding — who:** Udayāditya vs a Nēmaka feudatory.
- **The Udayasamudra:** identified vs unidentified.

**Transition:** A name in stone is a claim, and the *praśasti* is the largest of them.

## 1.5 Stance *(What carries the story · 750 w)*

**Purpose:** Udaypur's story rests on a few kinds of record:
- a broken panegyric slab;
- two dated markers;
- a contested accession;
- many legends.

This section presents those things themselves: where they are, what they say and how they disagree. It closes on the land that produced the town.

**Paragraph plan:**
1. **P1 — Udaypur's great inscription is broken and scattered.**
   - Topic sentence: *Udaypur's great inscription survives as two slabs that have moved.*
   - One slab stood in front of the temple in 1933 and is in Gwalior Museum in later accounts.
   - The second half was found in a Dhimar's house near the Chatua Darwaza and is now in Gwalior.
   - It is undated: assigned to about 1072, or to 1059.

   (C01-GANGULY-009, C01-GEOHER-006, C01-JAGTA-EN-019, C01-CII-009, C01-JAGTA-HI-003, C01-CII-008, C01-GANGULY-006)
2. **P2 — The *praśasti* is praise.**
   - Topic sentence: *The *praśasti* gives the dynasty's only complete genealogy, and it is panegyric.*
   - Upendra to Bhoja.
   - Its later date and building claims are doubted, and other records omit its first four names.
   - It "has nothing to do with succession".

   (C01-GANGULY-008, C01-BHOJA-TIMES-009, C01-BHOJA-TIMES-004, C01-KB-CII-004, C01-KB-BHOJA-TIMES-001, C01-KB-JAGTA-EN-003)
3. **P3 — Two dates anchor the town.**
   - Topic sentence: *Two dates anchor Udaypur's foundation: V.S. 1116 and V.S. 1137.*
   - 1059 is the start and 1080 the flagstaff, which is variously said to have been erected, hoisted or repaired.
   - A plan dated 1059–1080.

   (C01-MADHYABHARAT-012, C01-PARTEMPLES-005, C01-GANGULY-025, C01-HARDY-004, C01-KB-JAGTA-EN-001, C01-KRAM-1-001)
4. **P4 — Udayāditya's accession.**
   - Topic sentence: *Udayāditya's accession is dated variously to 1058, 1059 and c. 1070.*
   - c. 1070 is the working position, after Jayasiṃha's end, with a reign to c. 1093.
   - Beside it: 1058–1087, 1059–1086, c. 1059–1087, and "probably before 1080" to 1086.

   (C04-PANDE-005, C01-KB-PARTEMPLES-001, C01-MADHYABHARAT-011, C01-GANGULY-026, C01-KRAM-2-002, C01-GAZ-015, C01-JAGTA-EN-053)
5. **P5 — Udaypur's legends.**
   - Topic sentence: *Around the temple, Udaypur keeps legends of its building and its survival.*
   - Stories shared with Lalitpur; the flour-grinding woman's temple, which its architecture contradicts.
   - The temple built in a single night, and the hidden treasure.
   - Two traditions of survival, Tughluq's wetted gunpowder and Aurangzeb's change of heart, beside the question of local resistance.
   - A closing lead into the land that produced the town.

   (C01-GANGULY-024, C01-PARTEMPLES-002, C01-MADHYABHARAT-006, C01-JAGTA-EN-029, C01-JAGTA-EN-034, C01-GANGULY-013, C01-KB-GANGULY-002, C01-PANDE-003, C01-JAGTA-EN-043)

**Conflicts:**
- **The *praśasti*:** its date (undated vs about 1072 vs 1059) and where it stands.
- **The accession:** 1058/1059 vs c. 1070.
- **Survival:** Tughluq vs Aurangzeb vs local resistance.

Each is framed around the object or event.

**Transition:** Chapter 2 turns to the country that produced the town.
