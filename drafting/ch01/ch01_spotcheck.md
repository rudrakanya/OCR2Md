## 7. Section 1.3 — verdict: SUPPORTED

> Its account of the hill fortification is less definite: the fort complex might have preceded Udayāditya, or might have been constructed during the king’s time as a military cantonment. [C07-GEOHER-003]

- **C07-GEOHER-003** — Geo-Heritage of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh: The Third Eye, p. 19 *(borrowed)*
  > At about 100 meters from this Natraj sculpture, we see a long wall that measures 1400 meters. It is a very thick wall encircling the hillock from all side, a close examination of this 1400 m wall show that this is a fortification wall, meaning that the encircled land is part of a fort complex, which existed prior to Udayaditya, or could have been constructed during the time of Udayaditya as a military Cantonment, because this was the most secured hillock, with very steep slopes. Walking along this hill fortification wall, we see bastions of different dimensions, the semicircular bastion, the rectangular bastion, and gateways on both sides of the entry gate. This entire fort complex is a mystery because historical records are silent about it. It is also an architectural marvel that is standing undisturbed over a span of 1400 meters, maybe the longest one anywhere in Madhya Pradesh.
