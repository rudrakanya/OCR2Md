# Chapter 3 — Before Udaypur: Prehistory, Early Vidisha and the Historical Landscape of Central India — outline

**Argument:** Udaypur was founded in a country that was already old. It sits in a Betwa valley used since the Stone Age. It lay in the hinterland of ancient Vidisha, capital of Ākara/Daśārṇa and a crossroads of the north–south roads. By the eighth to tenth centuries it was a contested frontier, with temples and towns a few kilometres from Udaypur's own hill. The eleventh-century town inherited its roads, its sacred geography and its rivals rather than creating them.

**Evidence:** `evidence_pack_v2.json`, made up of:
- 156 mapped items: S1 3, S2 80, S3 10, S4 40, S5 23;
- 24 `kb-new` items, verified verbatim and listed in `ch03_kb_additions_v1.md`.

The knowledge base found no prehistoric site on or near Udaypur's own hill. The Gazetteer is the only source for the region's prehistory.

**Boundaries:**
- The chapter stops before the Paramāra foundation (Chapter 4).
- Temple architecture, the Bhūmija style and the Udayasamudra tank are left to Chapters 5 and 7.
- The inscriptions are discussed in detail in Chapter 6.
- Geology and the river system belong to Chapter 2, which is referred back to in clauses.
- The map's conflicts on Bhūmija origin, the Udayasamudra, Udayāditya's relation to Bhoja and Udaypur's distance are handed off, not debated.

**Open questions, stated rather than padded:**

## 3.1 An old country: first traces and first names *(Archaeology + ancient geography · 800 w)*

**Purpose:** Udaypur lies in a valley worked since the Stone Age, inside the ancient country of Daśārṇa or Ākara (eastern Malwa), whose capital was Vidisha.

**Key points:**
1. Palaeolithic tools come from the valleys of the Betwa and its tributaries, the Bina, Dhasan and Keothan: Udaypur's own river system (Chapter 2). (C03-GAZ-002)
2. There is an ancient mound with chalcolithic tools and wares of 1000–700 BC, and microliths. Around Vidisha, a sequence runs from the Chalcolithic to the early medieval. (C03-GAZ-001, C03-GAZ-003)
3. The region was called Daśārṇa from the Buddha's day to Kālidāsa's, with Vidisha as its capital. The name lives on in the Dhasan, which joins the Betwa. (C03-MADHYABHARAT-018, C03-GAZ-041, C03-JAGTA-EN-005)
4. Ākara (eastern Malwa) is distinct from Avanti (western Malwa) in the Nasik *praśasti*. Vidisha was the capital of the Ākara-janapada. (C03-CII-004, C03-MADHYABHARAT-019, C03-CII-002, C03-KB-MADHYABHARAT-001)
5. In Purāṇic tradition, a Haihaya branch and then the Pradyotas held the region. (C03-GAZ-042, C03-MADHYABHARAT-001, C03-KB-MADHYABHARAT-001)

## 3.2 Vidisha: the city at the fork of two rivers *(Archaeology + literature · 950 w)*

**Purpose:** Ancient Vidisha (Besnagar) was the great city of Udaypur's country: Maurya, Śuṅga, Vaiṣṇava and Buddhist. Its temples, sculptors and merchants made eastern Malwa a centre long before the Paramāras.

**Key points:**
1. **The site:** Besnagar lies in the fork of the Betwa and the Bes, about two miles from Bhilsa. Its name is held to come from Vaidisha-nagara; the Pali texts call it Vessanagar. (C03-MADHYABHARAT-015, C03-GAZ-025, C03-RAJABHOJ-EN-003, C03-KB-GAZ-006)
2. **Ashoka and Sanchi:**
   - Prince Ashoka married a Vidisha banker's daughter, the mother of Mahendra and Sanghamitra.
   - Sanchi was raised largely by the donations of Vidisha's merchants, though general belief credits Ashoka.

   (C03-MADHYABHARAT-002, C03-GAZ-011, C03-GAZ-009, C03-HARDY-001)
3. **Śuṅga capital:** Vidisha was the capital of Ākara under the Śuṅgas, and Agnimitra was viceroy there in Kālidāsa's *Mālavikāgnimitra*. (C03-GAZ-015, C03-MADHYABHARAT-003, C03-RAJABHOJ-EN-002)
4. **Heliodorus:**
   - Heliodorus, envoy of a Greek king, set up a Garuḍa-dhvaja to Vāsudeva.
   - The pillar is of pinkish Vindhyan sandstone.
   - It is dated variously, to the first or second century BC, or by the latest date of the Indo-Bactrian king, 140 BC.

   (C03-GAZ-027, C03-MADHYABHARAT-013, C03-GAZ-029, C03-GAZ-028, C03-MADHYABHARAT-007)
5. **Early temple and sculpture:**
   - The elliptical Bhāgavata temple, possibly of timber.
   - The earliest historical lime mortar.
   - Colossal Yakṣiṇīs, Kubera, Durgā and the Seven Mothers.
   - The earliest Śaiva images in Madhya Bharat.

   (C03-TEMPLES-IND-001, C03-KRAM-1-002, C03-KRAM-1-001, C03-MADHYABHARAT-008, C03-MADHYABHARAT-009, C03-MADHYABHARAT-012, C03-MADHYABHARAT-006)
6. **A walled city:**
   - A fortification wall, probably of the second century BC.
   - Six periods of habitation at the confluence.
   - Northern Black Polished ware and punch-marked coins from the excavations.

   (C03-GAZ-039, C03-GAZ-037, C03-KB-GAZ-007)

## 3.3 Roads, markets and holy places *(Routes + sacred geography · 650 w)*

**Purpose:** Udaypur's country lay on the roads joining north India to the Deccan and on a map of holy places. Those roads and that sacred geography outlasted ancient Vidisha and were still in use when Udaypur was founded.

**Key points:**
1. Vidisha stood at the crossing of two trade routes. One ran from Pratiṣṭhāna through Ujjayinī and Vidisha to Kauśāmbī. A branch ran down the Betwa valley to Kauśāmbī and Pāṭaliputra. (C03-GAZ-061, C03-GAZ-060, C03-RAJABHOJ-EN-008, C03-MADHYABHARAT-027)
2. Jivaka's journey linked Vidisha, Gonaddha, Ujjain and Mahishmati, and Gonarda is identified with Godarmau near Bhopal. A traveller's road ran from Vidisha to Mānyakheṭa. (C03-KB-GAZ-010, C03-RAJABHOJ-EN-006, C03-CII-006)
3. The later Deccan road ran through Sironj and Bhilsa to Nemawar, and Nemawar was on the north–Deccan route in the early eleventh century. (C03-KB-MADHYABHARAT-002, C03-MADHYABHARAT-029, C03-GAZ-063)
4. Vidisha is a *tīrtha* in the Skanda-Purāṇa, and a Vaiśya of Vidiśā appears in its Khaṇḍeśvara legend. (C03-GAZ-064, C03-SKANDA-001, C03-SKANDA-002, C03-GAZ-040)
5. The road as a path of devotion: Tiwari's reflection (testimony), attributed, which ends at Udaypur's Udayeśvara. (C03-JAGTA-HI-013)

## 3.4 From Vidisha to Bhilsa: kings and shrines of the first millennium *(Dynasties + archaeology · 850 w)*

**Purpose:** Through the first millennium AD, Udaypur's country passed between Sātavāhanas, Kṣatrapas, Nāgas, Guptas and Kalachuris. Gupta rule left Brahmanical shrines at Udayagiri and perhaps near Udaypur itself. Old Vidisha gave way to Bhilsa, and Udaypur's own hill acquired its first datable cave.

**Key points:**
1. Sātavāhanas, Kṣatrapas and Nāgas held the country in turn, as their coins at Vidisha show. (C03-GAZ-043, C03-GAZ-045, C03-MADHYABHARAT-020, C03-MADHYABHARAT-021, C03-GAZ-047)
2. **The Guptas:**
   - Samudragupta defeated the Nāgas.
   - Chandragupta II's epigraphs are in the Udayagiri caves (Gupta Era 82).
   - Udayagiri is the earliest Brahmanical rock-cut temple, and its Varāha panel survives.

   (C03-MADHYABHARAT-022, C03-GAZ-050, C03-MADHYABHARAT-026, C03-KRAM-1-005, C03-HARDY-002, C03-JAGTA-HI-008)
3. **Kalachuris:** Buddharāja's grant of 608 was issued from Vaidiśa. (C03-GAZ-052, C03-GAZ-053)
4. **Besnagar to Bhilsa:**
   - Old Besnagar was deserted, by deluge or by some political event, and Bhilsa rose on the east bank.
   - The change is dated variously: after the seventh century; the 8th–9th; a branch-town from the 9th; by the 10th.
   - Bhailasvāmin's bazaars were already let in 878, and the name comes from a Sun temple.

   (C03-GAZ-019, C03-KB-GAZ-001, C03-KB-RAJABHOJ-EN-002, C03-MADHYABHARAT-017, C03-KB-RAJABHOJ-EN-001, C03-CII-001, C03-CII-008, C03-KB-GAZ-002)
5. **Near Udaypur:**
   - A sixth-century cave on the hill south of the village, with a two-line inscription not yet deciphered.
   - Gupta-period Viṣṇu images from Muradpur to Sonari, in Tiwari's account (testimony).

   (C03-KB-INTACH-001, C03-KB-INTACH-002, C03-JAGTA-EN-007)

**Paragraph plan:**
1. **P1 — Coins mark the changes of master.**
   - Topic sentence: *For three centuries after the Śuṅgas, Udaypur's country changed masters, and their coins mark each change.*

   (C03-GAZ-043, C03-GAZ-045, C03-MADHYABHARAT-020, C03-MADHYABHARAT-021, C03-GAZ-047)
2. **P2 — The Guptas carve into the rock.**

## 3.5 A crowded frontier: Udaypur's neighbours, eighth to tenth centuries *(Dynasties + monuments · 750 w)*

**Purpose:** Before the Paramāras, the country within a few kilometres of Udaypur was already a frontier. Pratihāras, Rāṣṭrakūṭas and Chandellas contested it, and it was full of temples and towns, from Badoh–Pathari to Udaypur's own hill. These are the rivals and the building traditions the Paramāras would inherit.

**Key points:**
1. **Rāṣṭrakūṭas at Pathari:**
   - Rāṣṭrakūṭa princes ruled at Pathari.
   - A minister of the Rāṣṭrakūṭa Parabala raised the Pathari pillar in 861.
   - Malwa passed from the Gurjara-Pratihāras to a Rāṣṭrakūṭa vassal between 808 and 812; hand the identification of that vassal to Chapter 4.

   (C03-KB-GANGULY-001, C03-MADHYABHARAT-024, C03-KB-GANGULY-002)
2. **The town at Badoh–Pathari:**
   - Temples round a great tank.
   - Gadarmal, of about the ninth century.
   - The Daśāvatāra group, eighth to tenth centuries.
   - At Pathari, a fifth-century Mātṛkā panel and a pillar, Varāha and Śiva temple of the ninth to eleventh centuries.
   - Patari's ruins, about 15 km from Udaypur.

   (C03-KB-MADHYABHARAT-003, C03-KB-MADHYABHARAT-004, C03-KB-MADHYABHARAT-005, C03-KB-MADHYABHARAT-006, C03-TEMPLES-IND-006, C03-JAGTA-EN-008)
3. **Udaypur's eighth-century record:** a two-line hill inscription, tentatively read, beginning with the *siddham* and naming a *sūtradhāra*. (C03-KB-GEOHER-002)
4. **The Chandellas reach Bhilsa:** by the Khajuraho inscription of 954, under Dhaṅga. (C03-GANGULY-001, C03-GAZ-055, C03-CII-005)
5. **The three powers:** Pratihāra power waned, and Kalachuris, Chandellas and Paramāras rose together. Central India was "thickly studded" with temples. (C03-JAGTA-HI-010, C03-TEMPLES-IND-008, C03-JAGTA-HI-009, C03-TEMPLES-IND-005)
