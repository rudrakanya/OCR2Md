**Historical question.** How was Udaypur made known, protected and administered, from the first survey report to 2021?

**Through-line.** Made known and protected in pieces: a report that turned a legend into a statement and was corrected by reading the stones; readings that became fixtures and a temple that became a textbook model; a princely department that found, removed and reset; a new state's list dividing the town between authorities, with money spent and records unshared; one remembered clearance; a voluntary survey counting the whole town for the first time, its list without legal force; a revenue order over the palace; and the record of loss, by theft, by quarry and by herd, which meets the people who live there (Chapter 12).

**Arc.** The reports (11.1) → the princely department (11.2) → protection after 1947 (11.3) → the one clearance (11.4) → the count of 2020–21 (11.5) → the palace and the order (11.6) → loss: sculpture (11.7) → loss: the hill (11.8, into Ch 12).

**Opening.** From Chapter 10's close (rank lost in steps; decline undated): the town re-enters writing as an object of survey, on Beglar's tour of 1871–72.

**Closing.** The herds, the quarries and the climbers on the hill are the people the record meets at its edges. No recommendation (Afterword).
