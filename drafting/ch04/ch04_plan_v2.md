# Chapter 4 — The Paramāras and the Foundation of Udaypur: Kingship, Patronage and Urban Formation — chapter plan v2

**Historical question.** How did a frontier ridge on Malwa's eastern edge come to carry a king's name? What can the record actually say about whether Udayāditya made Udaypur before he was king, as king, or by putting his name on a place that others held?

**Through-line.** At the start the reader knows Udaypur as a king's town, said to have been begun in 1059. By the end, the reader understands four things:
- the Paramāras reached this frontier as Rāṣṭrakūṭa feudatories who made themselves kings;
- they held it as their eastern limit under Bhoja and nearly lost the kingdom after him;
- the town carries the name of Bhoja's brother, who recovered it, and a contemporary record ties a temple there to him by 1080;
- the familiar beginning in 1059, before he was king, is Ganguly's reading of late records, which the dated record cannot confirm.

**Arc.**
1. The frontier and the house that took it (4.1).
2. Bhoja's frontier, and Bhoja's name on Udaypur's stone (4.2).
3. The broken succession and the three-sided war (4.3).
4. Who Udayāditya was: a bridge (4.4).
5. The dates tested: 1059 against 1080, then the dated reign (4.5).
6. What Udayapura was in his kingdom, and what the record ties to him there (4.6, into Ch 5).

Chronology runs through 4.1–4.3, is suspended in 4.4–4.5, and returns at the end of 4.6 (his death).

**Opening move (Part I.5; master outline).** Ch 3 ends: "Who held the hill itself in those years is not known." Ch 4 opens on the next claim about the same country. The district Gazetteer credits Sīyaka II with ousting the Candellas and with a kingdom bounded on the east by Vidisha (user's wording rule, 16 Oct: tier-7 claims are phrased as the Gazetteer's own). The paragraph keeps the gap open: a kingdom's eastern edge, not a holder of the hill.

**Closing move.** The last paragraph ends on the Udayeśvara as the single work at Udaypur that a contemporary record ties to the reign, named after its constructor. It does not describe the building (Ch 5), quote the 1080 record as a text (Ch 6), or detail the tanks and walls (Ch 7).

## 4.1 From feudatories to kings of Malwa *(c. 900–1010 · 650 w)*
- **Statement:** By the end, the reader knows three things:
  - the house first appears as Rāṣṭrakūṭa feudatories in Gujarat;
  - a sovereign title brought war, and in 972–73 the Rāṣṭrakūṭa capital was plundered;
  - the first charter issued from Malwa dates from 975, and Udaypur's praśasti claims more for these kings than their grants and their enemies' records allow (R7).
- **Purpose:** answer Ch 3's open line with the house that took the frontier, and set up the praśasti as a source of claims.
- **Paragraphs:**
  1. **The claim on the east.** *Point:* the eastern limit at Vidisha is the edge of a kingdom, not the holder of the hill.
     - E: C04-GAZ-001 (T7), C04-PARTEMPLES-002 (T5) · C: C04-CII-012 (T1) · Note: C04-GANGULY-035 (T5)
  2. **Feudatories with a king's title.** *Point:* the 949 grants set a sovereign's style beside a vassal's rank; the line goes back to c. 900.
     - E: C04-CII-002 (T1), C04-CII-003 (T1), C04-CII-006 (T1) · C: C04-CII-007 (T1) · Note: C04-GANGULY-015 (T5), C04-GANGULY-027 (T5)
  3. **Independence by war.** *Point:* the title provoked the Rāṣṭrakūṭa expedition. The war ended with Mānyakheṭa plundered (972–73), and the first charter from Malwa followed in 975.
     - E: C04-CII-004 (T1), C04-CII-042 (T1), C04-GANGULY-032 (T5), C04-CII-012 (T1) · C: C04-CII-009 (T1) · Note: C04-GANGULY-033 (T5)
  4. **What Udaypur's stone claims for them.** *Point:* Muñja died a captive of Tailapa II, yet the praśasti sets the Karṇāṭas at his feet. The claim is judged inadmissible, while the praise of his learning is "not undeserved" (C04-CII-022). Sindhurāja has half a verse.
     - E: C04-CII-024 (T1), C04-CII-129 (T1), C04-CII-022 (T1), C04-CII-026 (T1) · C: C04-GANGULY-045 (T5) · Note: C04-GANGULY-039 (T5)
  5. **A genealogy kept at Udaypur.** *Point:* the praśasti is the only complete early line; its portraits are conventional; Upendra is the Kṛṣṇarāja of the grants.
     - E: C04-CII-021 (T1), C04-CII-023 (T1) · C: C04-GANGULY-023 (T5), C04-GANGULY-030 (T5) · Note: C04-GANGULY-019 (T5)
- **Classification:** documented (T1); the praśasti as claim: interpretive (R7).
- **Transition:** within a generation the eastern limit belonged to a king whose name would outlast his records.
