# Chapter 10. Political Change and Economic Decline: Udaypur under Successive Regimes

**Target:** 4,200 words · **Sections:** 6 · **Evidence:** `evidence_index/ch10.jsonl` (61 direct, 115 contextual), with the later-Paramāra items homed here by `chapters.md` · **Rules:** R2, R4–R9, U1, U7.

## 1. Chapter function

- **Central question:** When, and how, did Udaypur stop being a seat of power? What can "decline" mean for it?
- **Argument:** Udaypur was not destroyed by one conquest. It was lowered in rank, step by step over six centuries, while each new power still invested in it:
  - an administrative seat under several masters, including Caulukya governors 1163–early 1170s (U1);
  - a royal residence in the 1230s;
  - a *qasba* of Sarkar Chanderi;
  - a large village.

  Its economic decline, as distinct from its political demotion, is an inference the evidence cannot yet date.
- **Builds on:** §1.3, the sequence in outline, as corrected by U1. Every recalled event gets one clause; each paragraph's Point is new.
- **Establishes for Ch 11:** the Gazetteer's "once a place of considerable importance" as an official's description.

## 2. Arc

A seat that changed hands (10.1) → a king's residence on a shrinking frontier (10.2) → an end that took decades (10.3) → a *qasba* of Sarkar Chanderi (10.4) → revenue, brass and the railway (10.5) → what "decline" means (10.6, into Ch 11).

Chronology is the spine. Each period is asked the same four questions: rank, patronage, settlement and institutions. Chronology is suspended only in 10.6.

**Opening:** the V.S. 1220 record naming a Gujarat king at Udaypur (Part I, I.5).

**Closing:** the Gazetteer's sentence.
