# Chapter 11. Udaypur in the Modern Period: Archaeology, Heritage Administration and Conservation

**Target:** 4,250 words · **Sections:** 6 · **Evidence:** `evidence_index/ch11.jsonl` (92 direct, 17 contextual), with the conservation history moved here from the Afterword's map · **Rules:** R5–R9, U7.

## 1. Chapter function

- **Central question:** How was Udaypur made known and protected, from the first survey report to the documentation of 2021?
- **Argument:** Made known and protected in pieces. Each intervention covered one object, never the town:
  - a report;
  - a museum removal;
  - a list entry;
  - one clearance;
  - a survey.

  The record of loss to 2021 is told by kind of threat. Remedies are the Afterword's.
- **Builds on:** §1.3 (the brass cover, "Garde Sahib", custody today) and §1.4 (Beglar, Cunningham, Bühler; Garde's find; the reset slab). Each is recalled in a clause and adds only its effects.
- **Establishes for Ch 12:** the people the record meets at its edges.

## 2. Arc

Into the reports, 1871–1888 (11.1) → Gwalior State's department (11.2) → protected in parts after 1947 (11.3) → counting the town, 2020–21 (11.4) → the palace and the order, 2021 (11.5) → the record of loss by threat (11.6, into Ch 12).

**Opening:** Beglar's tour of 1871–72.

**Closing:** the households and occupants at the record's edges. No recommendation.
