# Chapter 7. The Wider Built Heritage of Udaypur: Fortifications, Shrines, Mosques and Water Structures

**Target:** 4,250 words · **Sections:** 7 · **Evidence:** `evidence_index/ch07.jsonl` (239 direct, 107 contextual) · **Rules:** R5–R8.

## 1. Chapter function

- **Central question:** What do Udaypur's buildings other than the great temple record about who held the town, when, and for what? How much of that can be dated?
- **Argument:** The built fabric is a sequence of layers whose order is clearer than its dates. Only the mosques (1336–38, 1488, 1632) and one tank carry dates of their own. Survey periods are proposals (R7), and the survey's blanket "11th century" hides differences of build.
- **What changes:** from §1.1's walled town and remembered water tally, to a reader who knows which buildings are dated by inscription, which by fabric, and which only by proposal or memory.
- **Builds on:**
  - §1.1 (gates, houses, the tally);
  - §1.3 (the mosques' dates, the mansion);
  - §1.5 (the Bhujariya Talab, reused columns, the uncemented wall);
  - §2.4 (water at landscape level);
  - Ch 3 (the cave, the Mothers panel).

  Recalled in clauses only.
- **Establishes for Ch 8:** the water built and used for drinking, fields and rites, and the tank as a work of merit.

## 2. Arc

The undated wall on the hill (7.1) → what was carved on the hill (7.2) → the walled town below (7.3) → shrines after the great temple (7.4) → the three dated mosques (7.5) → water on the hill and in the town (7.6) → the tanks, ending on the one tank with a contemporary record (7.7, into Ch 8).

The order runs from the least to the most closely dated. Chronology is suspended in 7.1–7.3 and runs inside 7.4–7.7.

**Opening:** a colonial observer's nameless wall (C07-GEOHER-002). The observer is to be named only after the ingest (R9).

**Closing:** Vikrama's tank and its stated uses.

### 7.1 A wall without a name *(the fortified hill · 750 w)*
- **Statement:** By the end, the reader knows four things:
  - the wall's fabric: dry sandstone, about 1,400 m, bastions, two gates, about 56 acres (the area conflict goes in a note);
  - what lay inside it, a lived and watered hill;
  - that its date is open: before Udayāditya or his, the survey's "tenth century" being a proposal;
  - that a lichen study has been proposed but not made.
- **Paragraphs:**
  1. **The unnamed wall.** *Point:* the object and its silence.

{{7.1:P2}}
  3. **What the wall enclosed.** *Point:* the middle and upper levels: cells, reservoir, a Śiva temple and its *liṅga*.
{{7.1:P3}}

{{7.1:P5}}
- **Transition:** the hill was carved as well as walled.

## 4. Conclusion

- **Demonstrated:** a sequence of layers, with dated mosques and one recorded tank.
- **Unresolved:** the wall's date; the colossus; the Moti Masjid; the Udayasamudra; most stepwell dates.
- **Carried forward:** what was done in and around these buildings (Ch 8).
