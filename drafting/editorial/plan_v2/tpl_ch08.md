# Chapter 8. Religion and the Material Life of Udaypur: Ritual, Agriculture, Trade and Craft

**Target:** 3,800 words · **Sections:** 7 · **Evidence:** `evidence_index/ch08.jsonl` (44 direct, 225 contextual) · **Rules:** R4–R9, U1.

## 1. Chapter function

- **Central question:** What kept Udaypur's temple and town going, in land, water, craft, trade and service? What can be said of it for Udaypur itself, as distinct from the kingdom?
- **Argument:** Gifts, not a budget. Three dated endowments are recorded at Udaypur:
  - 1163, under the Caulukya overlordship (U1);
  - the early 1170s, Lūṇapasāka under Ajayapāla (U1);
  - 1229, under the Paramāra Devapāla.

  No founding endowment is known. Everything wider (tolls, shops, levies, workshops, trade) is the regional or normative pattern, labelled as such, and each section says once what it cannot show for Udaypur.
- **Builds on:** §1.1 (the time-keepers, the fair, living worship); Ch 6 (the porch texts); Ch 7 (Vikrama's tank and its uses).
- **Establishes for Ch 9:** service and memory held by a family; the people around the temple.

## 2. Arc

The gift of land in 1229 and the earlier gifts (8.1) → the regional pattern of endowment (8.2) → land, measure and water in the grants (8.3) → stone, cloth and the market in the Paramāra centuries (8.4) → the later trades (8.5, a bridge across centuries) → what worship required, and which Śaivism (8.6) → service at Udaypur, then and now (8.7, into Ch 9).

**Opening:** Dhāmadeva's four *biswa*, "taking water in hand", in 1229 (Part I, I.5).

**Closing:** the time-keepers' family and its hereditary service.

## 3. Sections

### 8.1 Four *biswa* at the eastern gate *(the Udaypur endowments under changing masters, 1163–1232 · 800 w)*
- **Statement:** By the end, the reader knows four things:
  - the gifts recorded at Udaypur: 1163 (Vasantapāla, when Kumārapāla's governor Rājyapāla held the eastern division, U1), the early 1170s (Lūṇapasāka under Ajayapāla, U1) and 1229 (Dhāmadeva, treasury officer of the Paramāra Devapāla);
  - that two of them drew on the Bhṛṅgārikā villages, attested from the 1170s (R4);
  - that Udaleśvara, Vaidyanātha and Udayeśvara are kept apart (R6);
  - that no founding endowment is known.
- **Paragraphs:**
  1. **The pillar record.** *Point:* the 1229 gift as an act; its date is from Ch 6 (§6.6).
{{8.1:P1}}
  2. **How small, and where.** *Point:* the *biswa* is glossed here, hedged once; Bhṛṅgāri is probably Bhiraṅgāvalī, about 6 km north-east (CII's identification).
{{8.1:P2}}
  3. **The earlier gifts, under the Caulukyas.** *Point:* 1163 and the early 1170s, framed as Caulukya overlordship (U1). The same group of villages gave sixty years apart.
{{8.1:P3}}
  4. **What is missing.** *Point:* the founder's endowment is unrecorded, and Ganguly's general report draws on praśasti language (R7).
{{8.1:P4}}
- **Transition:** how other Paramāra temples were kept shows what Udaypur's few records leave out.

## 4. Conclusion

- **Demonstrated:** three dated gifts under changing masters; the pattern of endowment, labelled.
- **Unresolved:** the founding endowment; the 1163 record (Ganguly only); the *kundies*' source.
- **Carried forward:** a family's hereditary service; the lane beside the temple.
