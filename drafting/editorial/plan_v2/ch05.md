# Chapter 5. The Udayeśvara Temple: Architecture, Sculpture and Religious Iconography

**Target:** 4,000 words · **Sections:** 7 · **Evidence:** `evidence_index/ch05.jsonl` (284 direct, 698 contextual) · **Rules:** R5–R9.

## 1. Chapter function

- **Central question:** What is the Udayeśvara by its own fabric, and what has its image programme been made to mean?
- **Argument:** The temple is the fullest statement of the Bhūmija system: a rotated-square star of seven tiers whose wall projections rise into the tower. Its walls carry a programme set by rule (dancers, guardians, Mothers) that Pande reads as Śaiva Siddhānta. The building is described from its fabric. Its meanings are attributed and bounded (R7). Its losses, the court's shrines and the western hall, are part of the fabric's evidence.
- **What changes:** from "the temple" as a name and a date (Ch 4) to a building read part by part, and then to the readings laid on it.
- **Builds on:** §1.3 (the court described as 4 + 4, the mosque, Adhikari's verdict), §1.4 (the dates), §2.3 (the stone). Recalled in clauses only.
- **Establishes for Ch 6:** the east doorway with the flag-bearer and the god's name, where Ch 6 opens on the written record.

## 2. Arc

The court and the building as now met (5.1) → the geometry that makes the tower (5.2) → its place among the Bhūmija temples (5.3) → the surfaces as craft (5.4) → the gods on the walls by position (5.5) → the remaining images and the broken ones (5.6) → what the programme has been made to mean, ending at the east door (5.7).

Description comes before interpretation. Chronology is suspended: the building is one moment, its losses are later.

**Opening:** the temple faces east, but is entered today from the west (C05-PANDE-002, -011).

**Closing:** the flag-bearer and the inscribed name at the east doorway, not read (Ch 6 reads them).

## 4. Conclusion

- **Demonstrated:** the building as the fullest Bhūmija statement; its programme, by position; the readings, attributed.
- **Unresolved:**
  - the origin of the Bhūmija;
  - the shrine count;
  - the identifications of broken images;
  - measured dimensions: none exist in the corpus (Research Register RR-8).
- **Carried forward:** the temple names itself in writing; Ch 6 reads the writing.
