# Chapter 4. The Paramāras and the Foundation of Udaypur: Kingship, Patronage and Urban Formation

**Target:** 5,000 words (U9) · **Sections:** 6 · **Evidence:** `evidence_index/ch04.jsonl` (221 direct, 461 contextual) · **Rules:** R1–R9, U1, U6, U8.

## 1. Chapter function

- **Central question:** How did a frontier ridge on Malwa's eastern edge come to carry a king's name? What can the record say about whether Udayāditya made Udaypur before he was king, as king, or by putting his name on a place others held?
- **Argument:** The Paramāras reached this frontier as Rāṣṭrakūṭa feudatories who made themselves kings, and held it as their eastern limit under Bhoja. The town carries the name of the kinsman who recovered the kingdom after Bhoja, and a temple there bore his name by 1080. A beginning in 1059 rests on late or faulty records (R1, R3, U6). The town's physical making is not dated by this chapter's evidence.
- **What changes:**
  - from the dynasty as its praśasti presents it, to the dynasty read against grants and rivals' records;
  - from "founded in 1059", to "named and held by 1080; begun earlier only on a reading".
- **Builds on:**
  - §1.2 (the polity, the crisis, the kinship note, the triad, the province, now dated per U1);
  - §1.4 (the praśasti, the 1080 readings);
  - §1.5 (founded or taken over; Record A);
  - §3.5 (the Chandellas at Bhilsa).

  These are recalled in clauses only.
- **Establishes for Ch 5:** the Udayeśvara as the one securely dated royal work at Udaypur (the V.S. 1137 record), named after its constructor; its start date as a reading; and what the 1080 record does and does not show.

## 2. Arc

The frontier and the house that took it (4.1) → Bhoja's frontier and name (4.2) → the broken succession and the three-sided war (4.3) → who Udayāditya was (4.4, a bridge) → the dates tested and the dated reign (4.5) → the town that bears his name (4.6, into Ch 5).

Chronology runs through 4.1–4.3. It is suspended in 4.4–4.5, which weigh evidence, and returns at the end of 4.6, the king's death. The scale moves from Gujarat and Malwa (4.1–4.3) to Udaypur (4.5–4.6).

**Opening:** takes up 3.5's last line ("Who held the hill itself in those years is not known") with Sīyaka II's eastern limit at Vidisha, keeping the gap open.

**Closing:** the temple as the reign's one dated work at Udaypur. It gives no description of the building (Ch 5), no quotation of the 1080 record (Ch 6), and no tanks or walls (Ch 7).

- **Statement:** By the end, the reader knows three things:

## 4. Conclusion

- **Demonstrated:**
  - the house's rise and the frontier;
  - the omission of Jayasiṃha;
  - the three-sided war and the recovery;
  - the 1080 temple record as the firm date;
  - the Nēmaka seat;
  - the later province.
- **Unresolved:**
  - whether anything was built before the reign;
  - the kinship (D2);
  - the enemies' identities (R2);
  - the builder of the Nēmaka temple;
  - founded or renamed;
  - when the walled town took shape.
- **Carried forward:** a king's name and a king's temple are certain, a king's town is not; the building is now read for what it says of its own making.
