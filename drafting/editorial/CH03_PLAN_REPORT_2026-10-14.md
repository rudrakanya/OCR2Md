## 1. Historical question and arc

**Question.** What was already in this country when a king put his name on Udaypur's hill, and how much of that long past can be found at the hill itself?

| Section | Words | What the reader understands at the end |
|---|---|---|
| 3.1 Old ground, older names | 550 | The oldest traces are district finds, and nothing prehistoric is recorded on Udaypur's hill. The country was already named, Daśārṇa in literary tradition and Ākara in inscriptions, and its capital lay elsewhere. |
| 3.2 The old centre and its roads | 600 | Vidisha was a walled city for a thousand years and the Śuṅga capital of Ākara. It grew rich on crossing roads that still ran in the eleventh century. The city is the measure of what Udaypur was not. |
| 3.3 Carved rock and early shrines | 900 | Worship in stone in this country runs from Besnagar's Vāsudeva shrine and the Greek envoy's pillar, through early Śaiva images and the Gupta shrines at Udayagiri, to a fifth-century inscribed panel on the far slope of Udaypur's own range. The hill joins that landscape only through the survey's proposed sixth-century cave and Tiwari's reading. |
| 3.4 From Vidisha to Bhilsa | 650 | The old capital emptied between the seventh and tenth centuries. Bhilsa took its name from a Sun image, and that name passed to the province around Udaypur. |
| 3.5 A frontier on Udaypur's range | 850 | By the eighth to tenth centuries the range was a frontier crowded with temple towns: a Rāṣṭrakūṭa pillar at Pathari in 861, Gyaraspur and Badoh, and the hill's own tentatively read inscription. Three dynasties competed for it. Who held the hill is unknown. |
| **Total** | **3,550** | Accepted range 3,200–4,400; sections at 70–120% of target. |

**How it differs from v1.**
- **v1 was a survey of Vidisha.** It had six paragraphs on Besnagar alone, Ashoka's marriage, Jīvaka's journey and the Skanda-Purāṇa legend. In v2, Vidisha takes one section of 600 words, and only what changes Udaypur's meaning survives.
- **Udaypur now has a stake in every section:**
  - the country it stood in;
  - the roads and stone traditions near it;
  - its range's fifth-century panel and its hill's proposals;
  - its province's name;
  - its range as a frontier.

## 3. The five hand-off fixes, and the opening

1. **The caves opening sentence.**
   - **Was:** Ch3 v1 opened "Udaypur's Shiva Pindi hill contains both natural and man-made caves".
   - **Now:** 3.1 opens on the oldest traces in the valleys. The caves appear only as "already familiar from the hill's geology", with their limit: no recorded early use.
   - **Config:** OPEN_IN says the caves are already known to the reader.
2. **The "outlined in Chapter 2" clause.** Gone. 3.2 P1 refers to "the city in the fork" without re-explaining the fork, and the config forbids "as Chapter 2 showed" phrasing.
3. **Al-Bīrūnī c. 1030.** Ch3 uses him only for the origin of Bhilsa's name (3.4 P2). Ch2 keeps the political position. This is written into the chapter brief.
4. **The proposal stated as fact.**
   - **Was:** v1's "local evidence of a pre-Paramāra structure".
   - **Now:** the sixth-century cave (3.3 P3) and the eighth-century inscription (3.5 P3) are stated as the survey's proposals. Each refers to Chapter 1 in a clause and does not re-argue the founding question. The two inscriptions are never connected.
5. **The close.** 3.5 P4 ends on the contested frontier by the late tenth century. It may not re-introduce the Paramāras as a polity, Dhārā, or Bhilsa as the eastern limit (CLOSE_OUT).

**Opening.** The new Chapter 2 ends on "the setting a founder found… this country was used long before Udaypur had a name". Chapter 3 picks that up directly with the oldest human traces, and says at once how little of them reaches the hill.

**Chapter 4.** Ch4 has an evidence map but no plan yet. When its plan is written, its opening must take up "the Paramāras' arrival in this frontier".
