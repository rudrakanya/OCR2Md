# Chapter 4. The Paramāras and the Foundation of Udaypur: Kingship, Patronage and Urban Formation
## Jagta Hua Kasba / कथा उदयपुर — English translation (JAGTA-EN, 16)
When the Pratiharas' power weakened, three powers emerged. Among these, the Chedis of Kalachuri, the Chandelas of Jaijakabhukti, and the Paramaras of Malwa rise upon the horizon together. In their long span of five centuries, the ambitious Paramara kings extended their boundaries over a large territory. Their kingdom was spread — along with Malwa — up to Vidisha and Udaipur beyond Bhopal, and in the west up to areas like Hansol, Modhasa and Mahudi. In the south, the Paramaras were ruling over regions from the Narmada's coastal tracts, such as Hoshangabad, up to Kota in the north. Some branches of the Paramara dynasty of Malwa had succeeded in establishing their kingdoms in the Arbund Mandal — the Paramaras of Chandravati, and [those of] Bhinmal, Kiradu, Jalore and Bagar.
C04-JAGTA-EN-001   ≈ p. 139 (Hindi original)   also Ch 3
Ujjain was the first capital of the Paramaras, which Raja Bhoj took [and shifted] to Dhar — not Dhar, [but] Raja Bhoj's beloved city of Dhara. These splendid buildings, made in Raja Bhoj's time, were Sanskrit schools, where the images of Saraswati too were installed.
C04-JAGTA-EN-014   ≈ p. 64 (Hindi original)   also Ch 6
Bhoj was not only a king. He was also a deep knower and scholar of many subjects. There was a big, famous council (*parishad*) of scholars of all subjects — grammar, language, literature, drama, dance, music, water management, architecture, town planning, temple construction, vastu — to join which there was competition among scholars from as far as Kashmir.
C04-JAGTA-EN-015   ≈ p. 64 (Hindi original)   also Ch 6, Ch 5
Bhoj, like an ideal ruler, developed every kind of art in his kingdom. He gave respectful place, around himself, to virtuous people. He had splendid constructions made. He wrote books. He established knowledge-centres like the Saraswati-kanthabharana to shape the new generation.
He lived a small life, and in it too a very small opportunity of rule, in such a way that all the splendour of his time came to an end, but he is even now remembered with respect.
C04-JAGTA-EN-016   ≈ p. 66 (Hindi original)
There is quite a bit of proof that Bhopal was founded by Raja Bhoj. The Bada Talab (great lake) is still, today, spread over 36 square kilometres on the very man-made dam he built.
C04-JAGTA-EN-017   ≈ p. 80 (Hindi original)
Apart from inscriptions, their proofs and their testimony lie filled also in documents — such as the documents of that era: Padmagupta's *Navasahasanka Charita* and Merutunga's *Prabandha Chintamani*.
C04-JAGTA-EN-018   ≈ p. 138 (Hindi original)
The Saraswati Kanthabharan[s] built in the Paramaras' time were nothing less than the finest knowledge-centres of their day, where there was a tradition of teaching subjects of every kind. For centuries this knowledge-tradition endured. Even today we learn from documents that in the hostel connected with the Saraswati Kanthabharan of Mandap Durg (present-day Mandu), there was an in-charge, one Govind Bhatt by name.
C04-JAGTA-EN-022   ≈ p. 144 (Hindi original)   also Ch 6
He also drew the attention of history-lovers toward the remains of an 80-kilometre-long vast wall in the forests near Raisen, which was possibly the state boundary between the Paramaras and the Kalachuris.
C04-JAGTA-EN-047   ≈ p. 98 (Hindi original)
From within Maharashtra, Gujarat and Rajasthan, the northern boundary of the Paramaras' kingdom extended up to here, Udaipur. From here the territory of the Kalachuris began. Because of being on the border, Udaipur must have been very important for the Paramaras from a strategic point of view — which is evident too from its thousand-year-old development and expansion.
C04-JAGTA-EN-054   ≈ p. 11 (Hindi original)
The period from the ninth to the fourteenth century, during which the Paramaras existed in this region, was itself such a time that the royal dynasties present all over India were carrying out the finest construction work in their kingdoms. From the Cholas far in the south to the Chandelas of Bundelkhand, from the Chauhans in Rajasthan to the Pala kings in Bengal-Bihar, the priceless heritage built by the contribution of generations was shining at its peak. Just as, in a match, runs are scored rapidly in the last over, so, on a war-footing, this construction work was done. After the eleventh century, this "over" was really about to end — as though destiny were constantly inspiring India's countless dynasties in every corner to gather all their energy and all the talent of their craftsmen, and to depart only after fashioning some unmatched specimens of craft for the coming generations. Wherever stone was available, no corner was left [untouched] for India's great creation.
C04-JAGTA-EN-055   ≈ p. 16 (Hindi original)
The abundant availability of stone gave the Paramara dynasty the opportunity to erect splendid specimens of architecture. In this work the Paramara kings, as it were, competed with the Chola kings of Tamil Nadu. Everywhere in their kingdom they built matchless temples, palaces, tanks, stepwells, and Sanskrit education-centres. Raja Bhoj's work *Samarangana Sutradhara* is testimony to what high-grade creativity they were scholars who gave patronage.
C04-JAGTA-EN-059   ≈ p. 97 (Hindi original)   also Ch 5
Dam- and reservoir-construction was the Paramaras' expertise. Wherever vast temples were built, there even vaster reservoirs were built. In the eight months of the dry season, for the load-carriers, businessmen, sadhus, pilgrims and soldier-groups travelling in caravans, and for the thousands of cattle of their chariots and bullock-carts, these very reservoirs came in use at the time of the halt.
C04-JAGTA-EN-060   ≈ p. 110 (Hindi original)   also Ch 8
The last king of the Paramaras is Mahlakdeva, in whose time Delhi's invader-plunderer Shamsuddin Iltutmish had attacked Malwa. Then all the temples of this region were razed to the ground.
C04-JAGTA-EN-064   ≈ p. 65 (Hindi original)   also Ch 10
The remnants of the Paramaras he found in the form of kings of small, scattered principalities. At one place he has written — "The present ruler of the small principality of Saunti claims that he is a descendant of the ancient Panwars of Dhar. At the time of the Muslims' conquest, this family took refuge in the forests of Saunti. The only noteworthy Panwar family who consider Vikramaditya, the ancient chief of the Panwars of Central India, as their origin are the petty kings of Saunti." *[Malcolm's words, re-translated from the author's Hindi rendering.]*
C04-JAGTA-EN-068   ≈ p. 118 (Hindi original)
But about the Paramaras he mentioned everywhere with respect. He says that in the history of Central India, the Panwar Rajputs are considered more honourable than the other dynasties. But they were defeated by the Muslims, and long ago their kingdom ended. Later, a chieftain of this line got established in his ancestors' capital, Dhar; but he came as an attendant of a Maratha king.
C04-JAGTA-EN-069   ≈ p. 119 (Hindi original)
But Malcolm saw the Paramaras living and residing with much respect even after centuries. He is quite careful in writing about them. He knows that they are descendants of those great Paramara kings, whose golden memories of their splendid reign are spread far and wide in Central India.

*Gloss [my translation]:* King Bhoja's treatise 'Samarangana Sutradhara' is evidence of what high level of creativity he patronised as a scholar. Among Paramara kings Bhoja's stature is like the sun shining among stars. He had Sarasvati Kanthabharana built in Ujjain, Mandu and Dhar, which were temples of Sarasvati and centres of learning in all branches of knowledge of the time including the Sanskrit language. Mandu was then known as Mandapadurga, and the name of the head of one hostel of the Sarasvati Kanthabharana there, Govinda Bhatta, is recorded in history.

उस दौर में राजपरिवार के अन्य महत्वपूर्ण सदस्यों को राजधानी से दूर के महत्वपूर्ण नगरों और सैन्य ठिकानों की कमान सौंपी जाती थी। एक तरह से राज्य शासन चलाने के पारिवारिक प्रशिक्षण का यह हिस्सा था और उत्तराधिकारियों को राज्यकार्य में दक्ष ऐसे ही बनाया जाता होगा। जैसे मध्य में सम्राट बनने के पहले अशोक के पिता बिंदुसार ने उन्हें अवंति का राज्यपाल बनाकर उज्जैन भेजा था।
*Gloss [my translation]:* In that period other important members of the royal family were entrusted with command of important towns and military bases far from the capital. In a way this was part of family training in running the state. Just as before becoming emperor Ashoka was sent to Ujjain by his father Bindusara as governor of Avanti.

*Gloss [my translation]:* Earlier historians of the Gupta period believed that 'tatpadanudhyata' speaks directly of a successor. Hence the literal meaning 'meditating on the feet' was considered more apt; it is praise with no relation to succession. So the claim of direct succession from Bhoja to Udayaditya weakens here.

## King Bhoj and Paramara-period Town Architecture (Raja Bhoj aur Parmarkaleen Nagar Sthapatya) — English translation — Bhagwatilal Rajpurohit (RAJABHOJ-EN, 37)
From the literary and epigraphic sources contemporary with the Paramaras, it is learnt that the founding ancestor of this line was born from the sacrificial fire-pit (अग्निकुण्ड) of the sage Vasishtha (वसिष्ठ). No sooner was he born than he drove away the foe (*par*, i.e. enemies) by slaying them; hence the true name of that hero became "Paramar" (परमार).
C04-RAJABHOJ-EN-001   p. 2

Among them, Siyak (सीयक) or Shriharsha (श्रीहर्ष) is the first Paramara king whose tenth-century copper-plate grants are found. He had two sons: Vakpatiraja Munj (वाक्पतिराज मुंज) and Sindhuraja (सिन्धुराज) or Sindhul (सिन्धुल).
C04-RAJABHOJ-EN-002   p. 2
The event of King Bhoj's ancestors obtaining the kingdom of Malwa is described in the *Prabandha-chintamani*. According to it, the ancestors of Mularaja ruled at Kalyanakataka in Kanyakubja. Then Bhumada or Bhumadeva became ruler. That famous king donated the whole of Malwa to Mahakaleshvara, the lord of Ujjayini, and appointed the Paramara family as its foster-protector.
C04-RAJABHOJ-EN-003   p. 164
According to this story it is clearly evident that the Paramaras were once the protection-officers of Mahakal's Malava region, who later, getting the opportunity, became its masters. Full sovereignty over the kingdom was attained by King Munj, and it was conferred with the supreme glory by Maharaja Bhojadeva.
C04-RAJABHOJ-EN-004   p. 165
For reasons of security, King Bhoj shifted his capital from Ujjain to Dhar. Before this, he had duly settled and developed the city of Dhara to befit a capital, making it attractive with a rampart (प्राकार / outer fortification wall), a moat (परिखा / outer ditch), and — in the manner of the age — up-to-date gardens, lakes and the like. Then, following that same plan, he also re-founded Ujjain, Vidisha (विदिशा) and others afresh. Near Bhopal and near Patna he founded new cities named Bhojpur (भोजपुर) after himself.

Abundant references to Bhoj are found also in the poetry, treatises and so forth of the Bhoj period. In later inscriptions, texts, traditions, folk-tales, proverbs and poems, countless references to and accounts of King Bhoj are found. Our knowledge of King Bhoj from history proper is limited, but from traditional literature, oral traditions and the like we obtain much more. Thus, in gaining knowledge of King Bhoj's personality and achievements, history and tradition become mutually complementary.
C04-RAJABHOJ-EN-006   p. 3   also Ch 6

Subtracting 90 from 1055 gives about 965 as the apparent time of King Bhoj's birth. ## The Hindu Temple, Volume I — Stella Kramrisch (KRAM-1, 2)
In the ‘Samarāṅganasūtradhāra’, a treatise on architecture, the author, King Bhoja of Dhārā, who ruled over Mālava in the first part of the eleventh century, says: “He, who begins to work as an architect (sthapati) without knowing the science of architecture (vāstuśāstra) and proud with false knowledge must be put to death by the king as one who ruins the kingdom (rājahiṃsaka); dead before his time, his ghost will wander on this wide earth. He, who though well versed in the traditional science is not skilled in the work will faint at the time of action like a timid man on the battle-field. He, who is expert only in his workmanship, but unable to understand the meaning of the traditional science, will like a blind man be misled by anyone’ (S.S. XLIV. 6-10).
