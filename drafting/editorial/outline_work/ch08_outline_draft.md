## 1. Chapter Function

- **Central question:** What did the god of Udaypur's temple live on, and who served him? That is: what can actually be shown of the endowments, the land, the crafts and the trade that kept a royal Śaiva temple going at Udaypur, and of the people who performed its worship?
- **Principal argument / intervention:** The surviving record of Udaypur's material religious life is a record of **gifts, not of a budget**. Three endowments made in the century and a half after Udayāditya (by a donor in 1163, by the provincial governor Lūṇapasāka in the early 1170s, by a king's treasury officer in 1229) show the temple being provisioned from villages and plots in the surrounding countryside, one of them about 6 km away. How the temple was first endowed is not known. The wider Paramāra record shows the menu of means a temple of this rank could draw on (villages, plots, shops, oil-mills, tolls on bull-loads, market levies, cash), and one of these, a toll on loads, is attested at Vidisha itself; which of them Udaypur used is not recorded. The temple's servants appear only in two forms: a family of time-keepers whose claim runs back "for centuries", and regional norms for daily worship. The chapter refuses the inference, common in the popular sources, that a large temple proves a rich economy (C08-JAGTA-EN-017): it treats the monument as evidence of what was spent, not of how it was earned.
- **What changes over the chapter:** It moves from the stone record at the temple gate (what was given) outward to the region (how temples were usually kept), down to the ground (what the grants say about land, measure and water), across to craft and trade (the one craft named for Udaypur is stone), and back inside the temple (who served, in what doctrine, and what of that service survives today). Intellectually, the reader moves from "a great temple must have had great revenues" to a precise sense of three gifts, a regional pattern, and a gap.
- **How it develops Chapters 1–7:**
  - Builds on 1.2 (Rājyapāla at Udayapura; the *maṇḍala*, *bhoga* and *pāṭhaka* hierarchy), 1.3 (Lūṇapasāka's grant of Umaratha to Vaidyanātha, early 1170s; Devapāla's two records inside the temple entrance, three years apart; Devapāla residing at Udayapura), 1.1 (the time-keepers' house; the living worship; the fair), 2.2 (soils, *kharif*/*rabi*, wells and tanks for the winter crop), 2.5 (stone quarried, dressed and exported today).
  - **Must not repeat:** the land itself (Ch 2): no soils, rainfall, crops by season or tank landscape re-described; the time-keepers' house and the gong story as told in 1.1 (recall in a clause, add only Pande's drum-stone and "retinue" observation); the fair as scene (1.1); the Lūṇapasāka grant's facts (1.3) beyond what the endowment argument needs; the 1080 flag-staff (1.2, 1.4); the brass cover (1.3; V.S. 1841 only, ROADMAP 14).
  - Coordinates with **Ch 5** (the image programme, including Pande's reading of the tower-front as the Śaiva Siddhānta "pure path", the Lakulīśa panel, Nakulīśa and Harihara images: all Ch 5); **Ch 6** (the Dhāmadeva and Lūṇapasāka records as texts: their script, language, readings, the *panchakula* line as epigraphy); **Ch 7** (wells, stepwells, tanks and the town's bazaar fabric in detail; the Jain precinct as a building).
- **What it must establish for Chapter 9:** that the temple's continuity is carried by people (a hereditary time-keeping family, priests, the crowds of the Śivarātri) whose memory of their own role reaches further than any record of it. Ch 9 takes up community, festival and memory as subjects in their own right.

## 2. Chapter Arc

Opening on the Dhāmadeva pillar record at the temple's eastern gate → 8.1 the three Udaypur endowments and the missing foundation endowment → 8.2 the regional menu of means (scale shift out to the Paramāra kingdom and its branches, with Vidisha and Bhilsa as the nearest instances) → 8.3 what the grants' language says about land, measure and water (scale down to the field, still regional, with the Udaypur *biswa* as the anchor) → 8.4 stone, cloth and markets (Udaypur's one named craft; regional trade; the modern district as baseline) → 8.5 the god's servants and the god's doctrine (back inside the temple; from the eleventh century to today) → close on the time-keepers' family, which hands to Ch 9.

**Why this order:** opening on a gift binds the chapter's two halves (religion and material life) in one object, so neither half is a separate essay. Endowments come before ritual because they are the only Udaypur-specific medieval evidence with dates; ritual and doctrine come last because their Udaypur evidence is modern (Pande's observation, a family's claim) and the chapter should end where its evidence is living, which hands naturally to Ch 9. The alternative (old canon order: doctrine C12, then economy C13) would open on a section with no direct evidence (S1: 0 direct) and leave the chapter's strongest material until halfway.

**Scale shifts:** 8.2 goes out to the Paramāra realm (Ujjain, Kalvan, Jhalrapatan, Pānāhedā, Arthūnā, Bhinmāl) and must return to Vidisha and Bhilsa, the nearest attested instances, before closing. 8.4 shifts from the eleventh century to the 1969 industries list and today's stone trade and must mark the jump.

**Chronology:** runs in 8.1 (1163 → early 1170s → 1229) and loosely in 8.5 (eleventh-century norms → 1870s → today). Suspended in 8.2–8.4, which are thematic; each still keeps its layer visible (Paramāra period vs. 1979 Gazetteer vs. today).

**Through-line (one sentence):** By the end, the reader knows that Udaypur's temple is known to have been provisioned by gifts of village and plot from the countryside around it rather than by any recorded foundation estate, that the means and the people a Paramāra temple needed are visible only regionally, and that the one service still traceable at Udaypur is carried by a family's memory.

**Opening move:** The pillar to the right of the temple's eastern gate, with its record of V.S. 1286 (1229): a treasury officer of King Devapāla giving four *biswa* of land, "taking water in hand", to the god of the temple (C08-JAGTA-EN-009, C08-JAGTA-EN-010, C08-CII-020, C06-CII-037). Ch 7 (drafted in parallel) is expected to end on the town's built fabric outside the temple; this opening walks back through the gate. **Editor: align with Ch 7's last line.** Do not open on the size of the temple or a generalisation about temple economies.

## 3. Sections

### 8.1 Four *biswa* at the eastern gate *(the Udaypur endowments, 1163–1229 · ~850 w)*

- **Section statement:** By the end, the reader knows the three endowments recorded for Udaypur's temples, what each gave and from where, that two of them drew on the same group of villages to the north-east, and that no record of the temple's original endowment has been found.
- **Purpose:** Put the chapter's only dated, Udaypur-specific material evidence first, and fix the scale of what it shows.
- **Core claim / narrative movement:** The temple at Udaypur is seen being provisioned in small, dated gifts over the century and a half after its founding: donations for its maintenance in 1163, a whole village to the god Vaidyanātha at Udayapura in the early 1170s, and four *biswa* to Udayeśvara in 1229, the last two from the Bhṛṅgārikā group of villages about 6 km north-east. The founder's endowment, if there was one, is unrecorded.
- **Paragraph plan:**
  1. **P1 — The pillar record.** **Point:** Establish the 1229 gift as an act: in Devapāla's reign, on a Friday in the bright half of Kārttika, V.S. 1286, Dhāmadeva, the king's treasury officer, gave plots of land, four *biswa*, before the god of the temple, pouring water as the rite of gift; since no donee is named, the editor takes the god as the recipient. The record names the god "Udalmēśvara", which CII reads as Udayeśvara. The record is on the pillar right of the eastern gate. One clause recalls that this is the earlier of Devapāla's two records already met in 1.3.

### 8.2 What kept a Paramāra temple *(the regional pattern of endowment · ~750 w)*

- **Section statement:** By the end, the reader knows the kinds of income Paramāra-period temples in Malwa and its branches are recorded as receiving (villages, plots, flower gardens, shops, oil-mills, cash, tolls on bull-loads, levies on market goods and houses), that a toll of this kind was laid at Vidisha in the twelfth century and that Bhilsa's bazaars paid rent to a Sun temple in 878, and that temple land was lightly taxed rather than wholly exempt. The reader also knows that none of this is recorded at Udaypur.
- **Purpose:** Supply the frame that Udaypur's three gifts need, at the nearest scale the evidence allows, and say plainly what it can and cannot show for Udaypur.
- **Core claim / narrative movement:** A temple of the Paramāra world lived on a mixed portfolio, granted by kings, governors, officials and merchants, often on eclipse days and festivals; the closest instances (Vidisha and Bhilsa) show tolls and rents in Udaypur's own province. Udaypur's records show only the land element of that portfolio.
- **Paragraph plan:**
  1. **P1 — Nearest first: Vidisha and Bhilsa.** **Point:** In Udaypur's own province, a king Trailokyavarman laid a tax of one *viṃśopaka* on every load carried on a bull, "evidently in favour of the temple" (the Vidisha record; he is called king but his house is not named); and in 878 two bazaars of Bhāillasvāmin (Bhilsa) were let out and their rent of 130 *dramma*s given to the Sun temple. A temple could be fed by traffic and trade, not only by fields. Keep Trailokyavarman's dynastic identity for Ch 10 (C01/C10 items); do not resolve it here.
     - E: C08-CII-017, C08-CII-018, C08-RAJABHOJ-EN-007 · C: C08-CII-043 (*āvāsanikā*, "stall or shop", in a Bhilsa record of V.S. 935) · Note: C08-PARTEMPLES-001 (Vidisha V.S. 1216 and Gyaraspur records: temples built, images installed and villages granted for worship, in a regency period)
  2. **P2 — The range of a gift.** **Point:** Across the kingdom and its branches the same kinds of income recur: whole villages to temples and Brāhmaṇas (with the king ordering villagers to render cash taxes, the crop share and other dues to the donee); land for flower gardens with fourteen merchants' shops, oil-mills and fourteen *dramma*s for worship, *abhiṣeka*, offerings and festivals at a Jain temple in Bhoja's time; a Śiva temple at Pānāhedā assigned a *viṃśopaka* on every bull passing on the road, land, a garden and rice fields; and, at Arthūnā in the Vāgaḍa branch, levies on traders' houses at festivals, braziers' shops, distillers, a gambling house and loads of sugar, cotton, salt, oil and cloth, collected monthly. In Udayāditya's own reign at Jhalrapatan a Śiva temple was promised four *pala*s of oil and a ball of sweets yearly. Compress: one sentence per kind, not a catalogue; the Jain example shows the same means served all faiths.

Whether the town's own bazaar (Ch 7) paid the god anything is unrecorded. A later echo of the practice: until the early twentieth century, a village rebate on assessed revenue (*gaonti kharch*) still covered "charitable grants to temples" and festival expenses in this district: continuity of the habit, not of any medieval arrangement.

- **Drafting guidance:** Never "the priests of Udaypur performed …" for the medieval period: no item names them. Introduce the *Tattvaprakāśa* once (Ch 4 or Ch 5 may introduce it first: check the ledger after those chapters; if so, recall). Doctrine gets one paragraph; no exposition of *pati–paśu–pāśa*. Never quote SAMAR. Pande's drum-stone "may also have held"; the *vedī* "tradition holds"; the family's "centuries" is their claim. End on content (the family), not on an aphorism about memory.
- **Classification:** regional liturgy and doctrine: documented (tier 5, partly tier 1). Udaypur sect or monastery: gap. Time-keepers: tradition (family claim) reported at tier 5. *Vedī* purpose: tradition plus interpretation. Living worship: documented (observation).
- **Transition (into Ch 9):** The time-keepers are a family with a memory of its place in the town; Chapter 9 asks who else lives in Udaypur and what each remembers.

## 4. Chapter Conclusion

- **What has been demonstrated:** Udaypur's temple is recorded receiving three endowments between 1163 and 1229, two from the Bhṛṅgārikā group of villages about 6 km north-east; its foundation endowment is unrecorded; the Paramāra realm shows a repertoire of temple income (land, shops, cash, tolls, market levies), attested at Vidisha and Bhilsa in Udaypur's own province; grants measured land by plough and rod and treated wells and wheels as gifts; stone is the only craft named for Udaypur; the liturgy and doctrine are known for the kingdom; at Udaypur the service survives in a family's memory, in objects of inferred use and in daily worship.
- **What remains unresolved:** how the temple was first endowed; whether tolls or market levies ever went to Udayeśvara; whether any sect or monastery served it; who carved it; what the four-*biswa* plot was; the 1163 donation's source record (Ganguly only).
- **The tension carried forward:** a temple whose material base is visible only in small additions, and a town whose own account of its past (the family's centuries, Tiwari's "busiest commercial centre", the "budget" that "must have been considerable") is larger than the record. Ch 9 makes that tension its subject.
- **How the last paragraph leads into Chapter 9:** by content: it ends on the time-keepers' family as people who remember a hereditary service. It must not announce Ch 9, summarise Ch 8, or retell the fair or the gong story from 1.1.

### Research / Verification Flags

1. **Vasantapāla, 1163 (C08-GANGULY-016)** rests on Ganguly alone in the map. Find the record in CII VII.2 (not among the C08 CII items); confirm the god's name "Udaleśvara" and whether the donor's gift was to Udayeśvara. Ganguly frames 1163 under Kumārapāla's governor Rājyapāla; 1.2 presents Rājyapāla as holding the eastern division from Udayapura. **Check that Ch 1 and Ch 10 agree on whose officer Rājyapāla was in 1163.** Suggested wording: "In 1163, a donor named Vasantapāla is reported to have given …".
2. **Dhāmadeva's date.** CII gives V.S. 1286 = 1229 (C06-CII-037) with an alternative Kārttikādi reckoning to October 1230 (C06-CII-036). Text: "1229"; note the alternative only if Ch 6 does.
3. **The *biswa* reading** is conditional in CII ("If the reading is correct", C08-CII-039). Wording: "four *biswa*, if the word is read rightly …" once; do not repeat the hedge.
4. **Bhṛṅgāri = Bhiraṅgāvalī, ~6 km NE** (C06-CII-038) is the CII editor's identification. Wording: "probably the modern Bhiraṅgāvalī". The 6 km is CII's, not Tiwari's: safe as a distance.
5. **Construction span.** C08-JAGTA-HI-017 carries Tiwari's "two decades"; the map's conflict table ("temple foundation/consecration date") sets this against "three years" (C01-JAGTA-EN-045) and the 1059 reading. **Do not carry any construction span**; only 1080 is firm (ROADMAP 15).
6. **Rhetoric of prosperity.** C08-JAGTA-EN-017 / C08-JAGTA-HI-007 ("only an economically robust society could do such a thing") and C08-JAGTA-EN-007 ("budget … must have been quite considerable") are inference. Do not adopt them; at most a note giving them as Tiwari's view.
7. **"Undapura" (C08-CII-001).** The Harsaudā record's merchant Keśava is "a resident of Undapura"; C06-CII-035 shows a reading "[Uda?]pura" in another record. **No item says Undapura is Udaypur. Compatibility is not identity:** never call Keśava an Udaypur merchant. Ch 6/Ch 10 to rule.
8. **Bhilsa 878 rent (C08-RAJABHOJ-EN-007)** is a popular account (tier 9) of an inscription; confirm against CII (C08-CII-043 confirms only the word *āvāsanikā* in a Bhilsa record of V.S. 935). Wording until confirmed: "a record from Bhilsa of 878 is reported to …".
9. **Trailokyavarman (C08-CII-017/-018):** king of unstated house; the Vidisha record is V.S. 1216 (1158–59) per C06 items. Do not assign him a dynasty here.
10. **Mewar leaks:** C08-TEMPLES-IND-001 (Ekalingaji, "District Udaipur") and every TEMPLE-ECON item are excluded. C08-CII-024/-025/-040 and C08-GANGULY-006/-009/-019/-020/-021 are Abu/Chandrāvatī-branch records in today's Rajasthan: legitimate Paramāra evidence, but label them "the dynasty's western branches" and never let "Rajasthan" stand near "Udaypur".
11. **GAZ "kundies" (C08-GAZ-014)** cites an older source (footnote 1) not in the item. Find the source (likely a Gwalior State gazetteer) and its date: the reputation may be nineteenth-century.
12. **Tiwari as measurement:** do not use Tiwari for the police count (C08-JAGTA-EN-004), the "hundred millionaire businessmen" (C08-JAGTA-EN-015) or any figure.
13. **SAMAR items** (C08-SAMAR-1-001/-002/-003): ⚠ old OCR; re-verify against the re-OCR before any paraphrase; attribution to Bhoja doubted (say so where used).
14. **Mahesh Singh locators** are "no page; Chapter …". Cite exactly as given; never supply a page.
15. **Staged sources** (to be added after ingest, not evidence): the Gwalior Archaeological Report may list Udaypur's pillar records (C08-JAGTA-EN-009 cites the Gwalior Annual Report, Samvat 1974, no. 121); *Indian Antiquary* vols 18 and 20 for the Dhāmadeva record.
