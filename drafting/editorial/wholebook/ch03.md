# Chapter 3. Before Udaypur: Prehistory, Early Vidisha and the Historical Landscape of Central India

**Target:** 3,550 words (as written: 2,557 in 5 sections; below the 3,200 floor, evidence-limited, open issue RR-35) · **Evidence:** `evidence_index/ch03.jsonl` (the Gazetteer at most 40% of any section, R8) · **Rules:** R5–R8.

## 1. Chapter function

- **Central question:** What was already in this country when a king put his name on Udaypur's hill, and how much of that long past can be found at the hill itself?
- **Argument:** Udaypur's hill stood in an old, named country whose centre, roads and carved shrines lay elsewhere. They reached the hill only through proposals. By the tenth century its own range had become a crowded frontier. Udaypur is the subject; Vidisha and Central India enter only where they change what Udaypur means.
- **Establishes for later chapters:**
  - Daśārṇa and Ākara;
  - Vidisha/Besnagar, the roads, Sanchi;
  - Heliodoros and early Vaiṣṇava and Śaiva images;
  - Udayagiri and the Gupta shrines;
  - Badoh, Pathari and the Seven Mothers panel;
  - the sixth-century cave on Udaypur's hill (proposal);
  - Bhilsa and Bhāillasvāmin;
  - the Rāṣṭrakūṭas at Pathari, 861;
  - Gyaraspur;
  - the Candellas at Bhāsvat, 954.
