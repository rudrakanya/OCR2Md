## function
- **Central question:** How did a frontier ridge on Malwa's eastern edge come to carry a king's name? What can the record say about whether Udayāditya made Udaypur before he was king, as king, or by putting his name on a place others held?
- **Argument:** The Paramāras reached this frontier as Rāṣṭrakūṭa feudatories who made themselves kings, and held it as their eastern limit under Bhoja. The town carries the name of Bhoja's brother (U11), who recovered the kingdom after a three-sided war, and a temple there bore his name by 1080. A beginning in 1059 rests on late or faulty records (R1, R3, U6). The town's physical making is not dated by this chapter's evidence.
- **Builds on:** §1.2 (the polity, the crisis, the kinship note, the triad, the province, U1); §1.4 (the praśasti, the 1080 readings); §1.5 (founded or taken over; Record A); §3.5 (the Candellas at Bhāsvat). Recalled in clauses only.
- **Establishes for Ch 5:** the Udayeśvara as the one securely dated royal work at Udaypur (the V.S. 1137 record), named after its constructor; its start date as a reading.

## sec 4.1
scope: c. 900–1010
The house first appears as Rāṣṭrakūṭa feudatories in Gujarat; a sovereign title brought war; the first charter from Malwa dates from 975; Udaypur's praśasti claims more for these kings than their grants and their enemies' records allow (R7).
- P1: The claim on the east | the Gazetteer credits Sīyaka II with ousting the Candellas and a kingdom bounded by Vidisha (U8); the limit is a kingdom's edge, not the hill's holder.
- P2: Feudatories with a king's title | the 949 grants; the line back to c. 900; Ganguly's Rāṣṭrakūṭa vassalage (note).
- P3: Independence by war | the Rāṣṭrakūṭa expedition; Khalighaṭṭa; Mānyakheṭa plundered, 972–73; the first Malwa charter, 975.
- P4: What Udaypur's stone claims | Muñja's death as Tailapa II's captive against the praśasti's Karṇāṭas at his feet and Tripurī, judged inadmissible; Sindhurāja's half verse.
- P5: A genealogy kept at Udaypur | the only complete early line; conventional portraits; Upendra as the grants' Kṛṣṇarāja; the fire-pit birth set aside (note).
transition: Within a generation the eastern limit belonged to a king whose name would outlast his records.

## sec 4.2
scope: 1011–c. 1055
Bhoja's dated reign and the Bhopal country in his kingdom by 1018; the model of royal building and learning his name carried; his fame at Udaypur as praise cut by his successors; the praśasti's turn from his death to Udayāditya.
- P1: The east under Bhoja | the dated records; the 1018 grant; Al-Bīrūnī's limit at Bhilsa; nothing found at Udaypur.
- P2: A king who built and wrote | the royal temple as *dāna*; Bhojpur; the Vāgdevī image; the *Tattvaprakāśa*; authorship doubted (note).
- P3: Bhoja on Udaypur's stone | verses 16–20; the temples of the four quarters (the editor's reading); few remains.
- P4: From his death to Udayāditya | verse 21's "another Sun"; the enemies unnamed.
- P5: Bhoja remembered | Tiwari's conjecture and a popular account; the praśasti states no relation; Bhoja dead before 1055.
transition: The stone that praises Bhoja passes over the king who reigned next.

## sec 4.3
scope: c. 1055–1077
The praśastis omit Jayasiṃha; the Pānāheḍā record raises a possible rivalry; a coalition from three sides overwhelmed him (R2); Udayāditya turned it back, and Udaypur's slabs carry the dynasty's version. Udayāditya was Bhoja's brother (U11), and the rival pedigree that gives him eastern ground is the one its editor rejects.
- P1: The missing king | Jayasiṃha's grant, the gap in the dynasty's lists; Kielhorn on the short reign (note).
- P2: A rival for the throne? | Pānāheḍā, V.S. 1116; the editor's presumption of a struggle with "Udayāditya or some other person".
- P3: Three enemies | the Cālukya quarrel, Someśvara II's general and feudatory and the Kalacuri Karṇa (R2); Jayasiṃha routed; the editor's and Ganguly's variants in a note (RR-4, RR-K); the realm recovered took in part of Vidisha district.
- P4: Udaypur's slabs on the victory | the "total destruction" of the Kalacuri king; Bühler's doubt (note); the Boar-temple hint (the editor's inference); Vikramāditya VI's war of 1077.
- P5: Who Udayāditya was | Bhoja's brother by two inscriptions of his son's time (U11); why it matters at Udaypur: the Udayapura pedigree (Record B, R1) gives him the "eastern territory", Ganguly's junior line follows from it, and its editor rejects it; Tiwari's *pādānudhyāta* and silence arguments; "ruling in V.S. 1116".
transition: That eastern territory comes with a date, which 4.4 tests.

## sec 4.4

- P2: Where the year comes from | Records A, B and C kept apart; each late, faulty or both; the long inscription awaits an edition (RR-1).
- P3: The firmer year | the V.S. 1137 record "supplies the earliest known date"; Pānāheḍā and the Caulukya Karṇa's dates against a sovereign in 1059 (R2); 1080 dates a stage, not a beginning.
- P4: What rests on the choice | a prince's seat begun before the throne, or a recovered kingdom's eastern country; said once.
- P5: The dated reign | Jasahara 1081; the lamp for Lakṣmadeva; Jhālrapāṭan 1086–87; Kōśavardhana; to 1093; accession c. 1070 (datings in a note).
transition: With the king first, the question becomes what Udaypur was to him.

## sec 4.5
scope: Udaypur in focus
What the name proves (a claim made in stone) and what it does not (a founding); the village's tradition of a conquered Bhringrajpur, as the survey places it; the Nēmaka seat (one abraded slab); the province of the 1170s (R4, U1); how later writers picture the town; how the reign ended. The one work a contemporary record ties to him at Udaypur is the temple that bears his name.
- P1: What the name proves | the name on the temple; the triad's inscription; the Gazetteer's and later writers' founding (U8); the gift that keeps merit in the giver's name; name is not foundation.
- P2: The village's answer | Bhringrajpur as memory of a conquest; its place on the ground a survey proposal (R7).
- P3: A seat for a subordinate house | Udayapura as the Nēmaka capital under Udayāditya; Śūdraka's victory over a king of Gujarat (the Caulukya Karṇa or Bhīma, R2); which temple, unclear (RR-6).
- P4: A province described eighty years later | the 1173 record, the sixty-four villages, the *daṇḍanāyaka*; the Caulukya governors (R4, U1).
- P5: The town as it has been pictured | Tiwari's planned town and "mini-capital" (R5); Ganguly's list of towns; walls dated by fabric only (Ch 7).
- P6: The king's end, and what Udaypur keeps | Jagaddēva's refusal; death in 1093; Naravarman's succession and memorial gift; the temple that bears his name.
transition (out): The temple that bears his name, into Ch 5.
