# Chapter 9. Society and Cultural Life: Communities, Languages, Festivals and Oral Traditions

**Target:** 2,800 words (U2, approved exception) · **Sections:** 6 · **Evidence:** `evidence_index/ch09.jsonl` (32 direct, 43 contextual) · **Rules:** R5–R8.

## 1. Chapter function

- **Central question:** Who lived at Udaypur, how did they speak and keep festival, and how does the town narrate itself?
- **Argument:** The evidence for communities and language is district-level or one writer's testimony, and the chapter says so. Its centre is memory:
  - who tells which stories;
  - how versions grow;
  - why Udaypur's own tales name only Udayāditya while the region's folklore belongs to Bhoja.
- **Builds on:** §1.1 (the fair, Berni Mata's plot, the snake legend, the 1870s shared shrine); §1.4–§1.5 (legends); Ch 8 (the time-keepers). Recalled in clauses only.
- **Establishes for Ch 10:** the town's own memory of decline, which Ch 10 tests against the record.

## 2. Arc

A lane of neighbours, now (9.1) → the society the town was founded into (9.2) → speech and the written word (9.3) → the festival year (9.4) → the tellers and the founder in the stories (9.5) → Bhoja's fame and the Garhi's threads, ending on the remembered deserted market of Daulatpur (9.6, into Ch 10).

**Opening:** the lane on the left as one leaves the temple: Hindu and Muslim households, described by community, never by residents' names.

**Closing:** Daulatpur, as memory only, with no location given.
