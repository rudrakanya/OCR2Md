## I.2 Evidence rules

Chapter entries cite these by number and do not restate them.

- **R1. Three late records, kept separate.** Three objects are never identified with one another, because no source does so. A draft may compare them but never merges them.
  - **Record A, "the 1513 record":** Ganguly's fragmentary record "dated 1513 A.D." (*JAOS* VII p. 35). It states that Udayāditya built the temple in V.S. 1116; Ganguly calls it "very perfunctorily executed". Items: C01-GANGULY-020, C04-GANGULY-093.
  - **Record B, "the Udayapura pedigree":** CII dates it V.S. 1562 (1505/06) and rejects its genealogy (Śūravīra–Gōndala–Gyātā–Udayāditya) in view of the brother statement (U11). It states that Udayāditya was "ruling in Sam 1116, or Śaka 981". Items: C04-CII-131, C04-CII-132, C04-GANGULY-079, C04-GANGULY-081, C04-GANGULY-094, C01-CII-014.
  - **Record C, "the long V.S. 1116 inscription":** twenty-two lines on the temple, read as V.S. 1116 / Śaka 981 by S. K. Verma (reported by Tiwari) and by INTACH, and described as faulty in language and execution. Items: C06-JAGTA-EN-011, C06-INTACH-001, and C04-GAZ-006 (the Gazetteer's "two other inscriptions", which are not identified with C).
- **R2. Karṇa is always named by dynasty:** the **Kalacuri** king of Dāhala (c. 1041–72) or the **Caulukya** king of Gujarat (1064–94). Where an item does not say which Karṇa it means, the text keeps him unspecified and attributes the statement, and the item is listed in Part V, RR-K.
- **R3. The V.S. 1116 statements are not one record.** Record A's building date, Record B's "ruling in Sam 1116" and Record C's date stay distinct, under the labels A, B and C, until they are checked against the primary reports (Bühler, *EI* I; Cunningham, ASR IX–X). That check is RR-1.
- **R4. Administrative geography by date.** No administrative, territorial or institutional term is applied before the date of its earliest attesting record.
  - The Bhāillasvāmi-mahādvādaśaka and the Bhṛṅgārikā *pāṭhaka* (sixty-four villages) are attested in Lūṇapasāka's grant of the early 1170s (C01-GANGULY-010; C01-GAZ-010; C04-GAZ-007). They are never applied to Udayāditya's reign.
  - The "eastern division" governed from Udayapura is attested for 1163 (C08-GANGULY-016).
  - The Mahādvādaśaka-maṇḍala is a twelfth-century name (C04-BHOJDEV-HI-011).
  - The models are §1.2 P4, §3.4 P4 and §4.5 P4.
- **R5. Testimony.** Tiwari's testimony (JAGTA) is never a measurement, distance, count, date or medieval fact. Verma's readings, as Tiwari reports them, are attributed as such. Tiwari is named in full only in §1.1, with no gendered pronouns.
- **R6. One primary home; compatibility is not identity.**
  - An item establishes (E) a claim in one unit only, its home: the unit of its ID prefix unless I.4 names another.
  - Other units cite it as C and tell it in a clause.
  - No two inscriptions, buildings, tanks, streams, traditions, rulers, places, layers or hypotheses are merged unless an item says they are one.
- **R7. Claims stay claims.** A praśasti's statements are what the dynasty claimed. A tradition is evidence for memory. A survey's dates and identifications are proposals. Tier-7 sources are phrased as their own (U8).
- **R8. Proportion.** The Gazetteer supplies at most 40% of a section's cited items. The QC script warns above that.
- **R9. Excluded and staged sources.**
  - *Temple Economics* is excluded (its Udaipur is in Rajasthan).
  - SAMAR items (⚠, old OCR) are normative, are never quoted, and carry the attribution doubt.
  - The staged public-domain sources (Bühler, Beglar, Cunningham, the 1925–26 Gwalior report) are not evidence until hand-corrected and ingested.
- **R10. Settled ranges.** Udaypur lies 13–16 km from Ganj Basoda and 55–70 km from Vidisha. A single figure from one source is not substituted for the range. The 70 km end leans partly on testimony (RR-34).
- **R11. Present-day science stays present.** Modern climate, streamflow and land-use studies describe the present regime only. They are never read back into the eleventh century (§2.1–§2.2 are the models; the trends go in notes).

## I.5 Boundary rulings and the chain of chapters

- **Ch 1 | Ch 2.** Ch 1 ends on the stone and water that both founding answers stand on. Ch 2 opens on the river of the inscriptions and asks what the land gave and withheld. Ch 2 never makes the land a cause.
- **Ch 2 | Ch 3.** Ch 2 ends on what the ground does not say. Ch 3 opens on the oldest traces in the valleys and says how little reaches the hill.
- **Ch 3 | Ch 4.** Ch 3 ends: "Who held the hill itself in those years is not known." Ch 4 opens on Sīyaka II's eastern limit at Vidisha and keeps the gap open.
- **Ch 4 | Ch 5.** Ch 4 ends on the temple as the reign's one dated work at Udaypur. Ch 5 opens on the building facing east, entered from the west. Ch 4 gives no description of the building.
- **Ch 4 | Ch 10.** Ch 4 ends the dynastic story with the succession facts (§4.5 P6). Naravarman's reign and everything after c. 1093/94 belong to Ch 10.
- **Ch 5 | Ch 6.** Ch 5 ends on the flag-bearer and the inscribed name at the east doorway, not read. Ch 6 alone opens at the east door, on the six lines of V.S. 1137.
- **Ch 6 | Ch 7.** Ch 6 ends on what the stones cannot say, with the inscribed mosques dated but undescribed. Ch 7 opens on the largest uninscribed work, the hill wall.
- **Ch 7 | Ch 8.** Ch 7 ends on Vikrama's tank and its uses. Ch 8 opens on the 1229 gift of land.
- **Ch 8 | Ch 9.** Ch 8 ends on the time-keepers' family and its service. Ch 9 opens on a lane of Hindu and Muslim neighbours, described by community.
- **Ch 9 | Ch 10.** Ch 9 ends on the remembered deserted market of Daulatpur, as memory only. Ch 10 opens on the V.S. 1220 record and tests traditions against the record, without retelling them.
- **Ch 9 | Ch 12.** Ch 9 holds community and memory; Ch 12 holds livelihoods and voice.
- **Ch 10 | Ch 11.** Ch 10 ends on the Gazetteer's "once a place of considerable importance", the phrase §1.1 quoted at the start of the book. Ch 11 opens on Beglar, 1871–72: the history of description.
- **Ch 11 | Ch 12.** Ch 11 keeps every administrative act to 2021. Ch 12 keeps the people.
- **Ch 11 | Afterword.** AW.2 compresses Ch 11 into a jurisdiction map and argues implications.
- **Ch 12 | Afterword.** Ch 12 has no recommendations. The Afterword opens on the earliest dated plan, Tiwari's 2019 letter.
