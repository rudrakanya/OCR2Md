# Chapter 6. Inscriptions and Intellectual Life: Epigraphy, Languages and Literary Culture

**Target:** 4,050 words · **Sections:** 8 · **Evidence:** `evidence_index/ch06.jsonl` (223 direct, 177 contextual) · **Rules:** R1–R9, U1.

## 1. Chapter function

- **Central question:** What do Udaypur's inscriptions say, in what language and form, and who made them say it? What can these words establish about authority, patronage and learning, and what can they not?
- **Argument:** The stones are several archives with different makers and registers:
  - signed working records;
  - the king's poem of descent;
  - a grammar-serpent;
  - the grants of officials and governors, under Caulukya overlordship from 1163 to the early 1170s and Paramāra rule again by 1229 (U1);
  - pilgrims' notes;
  - Persian building records.

  Learning at Udaypur is evidenced as something displayed and used by rulers and their literate servants. It is not evidence of a school or a court in the town.
- **What changes:** from the inscription as a source of facts (Chs 1, 4) to the inscription as an act, made by named people for named ends.
- **Builds on:** §1.4 (the praśasti as object, the 1080 readings, the pilgrims, the first readers); Ch 4 (the praśasti's claims as event-claims). Recalled in clauses only.
- **Establishes for Ch 7:** the texts that date buildings (Amērā V.S. 1151; the mosque behind the temple, A.H. 737–39; the 1488 mosque; the Shahi Masjid, A.H. 1041). Ch 7 dates and describes the buildings without re-arguing the words.

## 2. Arc

The temple's first dated words (6.1) → the other early writing near Udaypur, and the faulty long inscription (6.2) → the praśasti as a poem (6.3) → whose poem it is and what its editors trust (6.4) → the serpent of letters (6.5) → the porch archive under changing masters (6.6) → after 1232, and the new language of Persian (6.7) → what the stones cannot say (6.8, into Ch 7).

Chronology is suspended in 6.1–6.5 (one generation, c. 1070–1100, arranged by kind of text) and runs in 6.6–6.7.

**Opening:** the six lines of V.S. 1137 at the east door, with their named poet.

**Closing:** the inscribed mosques, dated but not described (Ch 7).

## 4. Conclusion

- **Demonstrated:** several archives with different makers; learning displayed by rulers and their servants; the porch registering Caulukya and then Paramāra masters.
- **Unresolved:**
  - the praśasti's date and composer;
  - Record C's relation to Records A and B (R1, R3);
  - the lost and unpublished texts.
- **Carried forward:** dated buildings without descriptions.
