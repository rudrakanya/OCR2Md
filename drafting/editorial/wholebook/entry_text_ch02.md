## function
- **Central question:** What could this country give, and what did it withhold, to the people who settled and built at Udaypur, and what did kings and villagers make of it?
- **Argument:** The land the founder found had no river at its door and no dependable groundwater. It had one wet season, black soil and a hill of building stone. Its water had to be made, and its town was built of its own rock. The land is the setting, never the cause. Modern climate and flow data describe the present regime only.
- **Establishes for later chapters:**
  - the Betwa (Vētravatī) and the Kewtan;
  - the rains and the two harvests;
  - the Garhi–Teonda range and the sandstone "quarried in the hill close by";
  - the forest and its grazing;
  - Bhojpur, as comparison only;
  - Udaypur's tanks, *kund*s and stepwells at landscape level (detail in Ch 7);
  - the quarries and Govind Saxena's report.

## sec 2.1
scope: rivers and groundwater
Udaypur belongs to the Betwa's country but not to its banks. The river is intermittent upstream and the sandstone's wells fail in summer, so the place depends on water gathered in natural hollows.
- P1: The river of the inscriptions | the Vētravatī by Vidisha and Bhilsa; Bhilsa as Bhoja's eastern limit (note); Lakṣmīvarman against the Candellas across the Betwa (this sentence carries a duplicated fragment, RR-36); the province of Bhilsa in the twelfth century.
- P2: The Betwa's course, and the Kewtan | source, course and tributaries; the district sloping north; the Kewtan 1 km from the village; no river through Udaypur.
- P3: A river of the rains | the present regime: intermittent upstream, monsoon runoff.
- P4: Groundwater that fails | the sandstone and its wells in 1979; piped water only at Vidisha.
- P5: Water in the hollows | lakes and ponds in depressions; settlement shaped by water.
transition: What the river would not give, the monsoon gave once a year.

## sec 2.2
scope: climate, soil and harvest
The year's water arrives in four months and bad years come in runs. The black soil, the wells and the tanks carry the winter crop. The modern climate trend appears only in a note.
- P1: Four months of rain | 92 per cent in the monsoon (1979 record); evaporation exceeds rainfall in the basin.
- P2: Bad years in runs | 1901–50; the famine of 1899–1900; the Basoda deluge of 1951.
- P3: The soil and the two harvests | black *mar* soil "very good for wheat"; *kharif* and *rabi*; the district by 1971–72; Udaypur's crops at the survey; wells and tanks for the winter crop.
- P4: Silt, and what is not known | the river *doab*s; whether silt reaches Udaypur is not recorded; the climate trend in a note.
transition: Where the rain gathers or runs off is decided by the rock beneath it.

## sec 2.3
scope: geology, landform and forest
Udaypur sits where erosion stripped the trap from bedded sandstone. The temple's sandstone was quarried in the hill close by. The hills are remnants of a wasting tableland, dry on top and hollowed by caves. The thin forest has long been grazed and cut.
- P1: Foothills of the Garhi–Teonda range | heights; the plateau; the range between the Betwa and the Bina.
- P2: Trap stripped from sandstone | the Deccan trap and the Vindhyan beds; the temple's stone "quarried in the hill close by"; sandstone across the district; Patil's geography of monuments (note).
- P3: A wasting tableland | mesa, butte and pinnacle as a proposed pathway; Shiva Pindi; overhangs and caves; the survey's inconsistent heights (note; RR-37).
- P4: The forest on the slopes | the district's forest in 1979; the reserved forest above Udaypur, grazed and cut.
transition: Of all the land offered, the town took its stone; its water it had to make.
