# Udaypur: Master Drafting Outline, Chapter 4 to the Afterword (v2)

*Version 2, 16 October 2026. It supersedes v1 for drafting. v1 (`Chapter_4_to_Afterword_Master_Drafting_Outline.md`) is kept unchanged as the record. The changes are listed in `OUTLINE_V2_CHANGES.md`.*

## Purpose

This outline tells a drafting session what each section of Chapters 4–12 and the Afterword must establish, from which evidence items, and under which book-level rules. It was rebuilt from the evidence maps (`evidence_index/chNN.jsonl`) without any paid model calls. Every direct item of every unit is accounted for as one of three:
- assigned to a paragraph;
- a cross-reference to its home elsewhere;
- excluded, with a reason (Appendix).

## How to read this document

- **Part I holds everything book-wide:**
  - the user's decisions (U1–U9, dated);
  - the evidence rules (R1–R9);
  - the homes of the conflicts and shared clusters;
  - the boundaries;
  - the resolution log of the six v1 flags;
  - the short list of decisions still needed.

  The entries cite these by number and do not restate them.
- **Part II has one entry per chapter, in the v1 component format:**
  - function;
  - arc;
  - sections;
  - conclusion;
  - evidence checklist;
  - the chapter's own research tasks;
  - cross-chapter notes.

  Each entry opens with its "Changes from v1". Structures are no longer fixed at five sections: each chapter has 4–8 sections of roughly 400–1,500 words, as its evidence warrants.
- **Paragraph evidence lines.** Each line under a paragraph gives the item IDs by role:
  - **E** establishes, in its home unit only;
  - **C** corroborates or cross-refers;
  - **X** gives context;
  - **Note** feeds a footnote.

  Tiers are in brackets (T1 is CII; T7 the Gazetteer and surveys; T8 testimony and tradition).
- **Parts III–VI:**
  - the cross-chapter chain and threads;
  - the drafting protocol;
  - the single **Research Register** of open tasks;
  - the continuity checklist.
- **The Appendix** gives each chapter's coverage by sub-topic and its exclusion list.

## I.2 Evidence rules

Chapter entries cite these by number and do not restate them.

- **R1. Three late records, kept separate.** Three objects are never identified with one another, because no source does so. A draft may compare them, but never merges them.
  - **Record A, "the 1513 record":** Ganguly's fragmentary record "dated 1513 A.D." (*JAOS* VII p. 35). It states that Udayāditya built the temple in V.S. 1116, and Ganguly calls it "very perfunctorily executed". Items: C01-GANGULY-020, C04-GANGULY-093.
  - **Record B, "the Udayapura pedigree":** CII dates it V.S. 1562 (1505/06) and rejects its genealogy (Śūravīra–Gōndala–Gyātā–Udayāditya). It states that Udayāditya was "ruling in Sam 1116, or Śaka 981". Items: C04-CII-131, C04-CII-132, C04-GANGULY-079, C04-GANGULY-081, C04-GANGULY-094, C01-CII-014.
  - **Record C, "the long V.S. 1116 inscription":** twenty-two lines on the temple, read as V.S. 1116 / Śaka 981 by S. K. Verma (reported by Tiwari) and by INTACH, and described as faulty in language and execution. Items: C06-JAGTA-EN-011, C06-INTACH-001, C04-GAZ-006 (the Gazetteer's "two other inscriptions", which are not identified with C).
- **R2. Karṇa is always named by dynasty:** either the **Kalachuri** king of Dāhala (c. 1041–72) or the **Caulukya** king of Gujarat (1064–94). Where an item does not make clear which Karṇa it means, the draft keeps him unspecified and attributes the statement, and the item is listed in the Research Register (item list: Part V, RR-K). Spelling of the dynasty names is pending the user's decision (I.8, D1).
- **R3. The V.S. 1116 statements are not one record.** Record A's V.S. 1116 building date, Record B's "ruling in Sam 1116" and Record C's date stay distinct objects with the labels A, B and C. They are not treated as one record until they have been checked against the primary reports (Bühler, *EI* I; Cunningham, ASR IX–X), which are staged but not yet ingested. The check is Research Register task RR-1.
- **R4. Administrative geography by date.** No administrative, territorial or institutional term is applied before the date of its earliest attesting record.
  - The Bhāillasvāmi-mahādvādaśaka and the Bhṛṅgārikā *pāṭhaka* (sixty-four villages) are attested in Lūṇapasāka's grant of the early 1170s (C01-GANGULY-010; C01-GAZ-010; C04-GAZ-007). They describe twelfth-century arrangements and are never applied to Udayāditya's reign.
  - The "eastern division" governed from Udayapura is attested for 1163 (C08-GANGULY-016).
  - The Mahādvādaśaka-maṇḍala is a twelfth-century name (C04-BHOJDEV-HI-011).
  - The model is Ch 4's treatment of the 1173 record (now §4.6).
- **R5. Testimony.** Tiwari's testimony (JAGTA) is never a measurement, distance, count, date or medieval fact. Verma's readings, as Tiwari reports them, are attributed as such. Tiwari is named in full only in §1.1.
- **R6. One primary home; compatibility is not identity.**
  - An item establishes (E) a claim in one unit only, its home: the unit of its ID prefix unless I.4 names another.
  - Other units cite it as C and tell it in a clause.
  - No two inscriptions, buildings, tanks, traditions, rulers, places, layers or hypotheses are merged unless an item says they are one.
- **R7. Claims stay claims.** A praśasti's statements are what the dynasty claimed. A tradition is evidence for memory. A survey's dates and identifications are proposals. Tier-7 sources are phrased as their own (U8).
- **R8. Proportion.** The Gazetteer supplies at most 40% of a section's cited items. The QC script warns above that.
- **R9. Excluded and staged sources.** *Temple Economics* is excluded (its Udaipur is in Rajasthan). SAMAR items (⚠ old OCR) are normative, are never quoted, and carry the attribution doubt. The staged public-domain sources (Bühler, Beglar, Cunningham, the 1925–26 Gwalior report) are not evidence until hand-corrected and ingested.

## 1. Chapter function

- **Central question:** What do Udaypur's inscriptions say, in what language and form, and who made them say it? What can these words establish about authority, patronage and learning, and what can they not?
- **Argument:** The stones are several archives with different makers and registers:
  - signed working records;
  - the king's poem of descent;
  - a grammar-serpent;
  - the grants of officials and governors, under Caulukya overlordship from 1163 to the early 1170s and Paramāra rule again by 1229 (U1);
  - pilgrims' notes;
  - Persian building records.

  Learning at Udaypur is evidenced as something displayed and used by rulers and their literate servants. It is not evidence of a school or a court in the town.
- **What changes:** from the inscription as a source of facts (Chs 1, 4) to the inscription as an act, made by named people for named ends.
- **Builds on:** §1.4 (the praśasti as object, the 1080 readings, the pilgrims, the first readers); Ch 4 (the praśasti's claims as event-claims). Recalled in clauses only.
- **Establishes for Ch 7:** the texts that date buildings (Amērā V.S. 1151; the mosque behind the temple, A.H. 737–39; the 1488 mosque; the Shahi Masjid, A.H. 1041). Ch 7 dates and describes the buildings without re-arguing the words.

  - Ch 3 (the cave, the Mothers panel).

  Recalled in clauses only.
- **Establishes for Ch 8:** the water built and used for drinking, fields and rites, and the tank as a work of merit.

## 1. Chapter function

- **Central question:** What kept Udaypur's temple and town going, in land, water, craft, trade and service? What can be said of it for Udaypur itself, as distinct from the kingdom?
- **Argument:** Gifts, not a budget. Three dated endowments are recorded at Udaypur:
  - 1163, under the Caulukya overlordship (U1);
  - the early 1170s, Lūṇapasāka under Ajayapāla (U1);
  - 1229, under the Paramāra Devapāla.

  No founding endowment is known. Everything wider (tolls, shops, levies, workshops, trade) is the regional or normative pattern, labelled as such, and each section says once what it cannot show for Udaypur.
- **Builds on:** §1.1 (the time-keepers, the fair, living worship); Ch 6 (the porch texts); Ch 7 (Vikrama's tank and its uses).
- **Establishes for Ch 9:** service and memory held by a family; the people around the temple.

## B. The principal argument across Chapter 4 to the Afterword

A king's name and a king's temple are certain; a king's town is not (Ch 4). The building is read for its own making (Ch 5), and the writing for who made it speak (Ch 6). The rest of the fabric is an order of layers more than a calendar (Ch 7). What kept the temple was gifts, not a budget (Ch 8). The town narrates itself (Ch 9). It lost rank in steps under successive masters, among them the Caulukya kings in the twelfth century (U1, Ch 10). It was made known and protected in pieces (Ch 11), and lives on as a working village in an old town, seen mostly by others (Ch 12). The book argues, once and labelled, for conservation by use (AW).

# Part IV. Downstream Drafting Protocol (v2)

1. **Authority:**
   - `chapters.md`;
   - the decisions U1–U9;
   - the rules R1–R9;
   - this outline;
   - `style_profile.md` and `style_sheet.md`.
2. **No paid API calls (U5).** The free checks only: `inhouse.py check`, the prose checks and the ledger check. `ledger.py update` is not run; each chapter's ledger is written by hand and merged with `ledger.py merge`.
3. **Per chapter:**
   1. read the entry and Part I;
   2. print the quotations of every cited item (`_tools/quotes.py`);
   3. write `chNN/chNN_plan_v1.md` (headings `## N.x Title *(scope · NNN w)*`);
   4. make the cfg (model: `ch04/cfg_ch04v1.py`, with BUDGET 0) and the pack (`_pipe/extend_pack.py`);
   5. draft `run/chN/N.x.md` section by section;
   6. verify in-session;
   7. run the checks;
   8. build with `_pipe/build_outputs.py --chapters N`.
4. **Flexible structure.** The section count and lengths are set per chapter in Part II, between 4 and 8 sections, each roughly 400–1,500 words. A drafting session does not add, remove, merge or reorder sections; that is a structural change for the user. A section may land at 70–120% of its target. Never pad. If evidence runs out, report the shortfall with its reason.
5. **In-session verification:**
   - every factual sentence is checked against its cited items' quotations;
   - every quotation is matched verbatim (the validator checks it);
   - pages come from the item locators;
   - anything unverifiable goes in `chNN_flags.md`.

   Every deliverable says "verified in-session, not by an independent model".
6. **Rules cited, not restated.** R1–R9 and U1–U9 govern every chapter. Specifically:
   - Karṇa always takes his dynasty (R2);
   - administrative terms are dated (R4);
   - Records A, B and C are kept separate (R1, R3);
   - the Gazetteer's claims are phrased as its own (U8);
   - no testimony supplies a measure (R5).
7. **Evidence roles:**
   - E items establish, and only in their home unit (R6);
   - C items corroborate or cross-refer;
   - X items give context;
   - Note items feed `{{note: …}}`.

   Items marked optional are omitted unless a section would land below 70%.
8. **Gap-filling:** free local search only, through `_tools/kb_new.py`: verbatim text, page from the marker, tier, `kb-new`.
9. **What needs the user:** any structural change, any conflict the outline keeps open, and the legal-review passages (LR-1, LR-2).
