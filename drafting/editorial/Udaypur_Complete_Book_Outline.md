# Part A. The spine

## A.1 The book in one paragraph

The book begins in an ordinary village and asks how it came to hold the remains, records and memories of a far more important place. It follows the evidence outward and back:
- **The land** gave the town its stone and forced it to make its water, but decided nothing (Ch 2).
- **The country** was old, named and carved long before, though its past reaches the hill only as proposals, and by the tenth century the range was a crowded frontier (Ch 3).
- **The founder.** A Paramāra house took that frontier. Bhoja's brother Udayāditya recovered the kingdom after a three-sided war and put his name on a town, a temple and a tank. The record makes the name and the temple certain by 1080, but does not make the town his creation (Ch 4).
- **The temple and its writing.** The book reads the temple for its own making (Ch 5) and the inscriptions for who made them speak (Ch 6).
- **The town's life.** Then come the town's other works, read as layers more than as a calendar (Ch 7); the gifts that kept the god (Ch 8); and the stories the town tells of itself (Ch 9).
- **The later centuries.** Udaypur did not fall into ruin. It lost rank in steps under successive masters, the Caulukya kings of Gujarat among the first (Ch 10).
- **The modern record.** It was described and protected in pieces from 1871 to 2021 (Ch 11), and it lives on as a working village in an old town, seen mostly by others (Ch 12).

The Afterword argues, once and labelled, for conservation by use.

Two questions stay open through the whole book: whether Udayāditya founded Udaypur or renamed an older place, and how far a king's claims in stone can be taken at their word.

# Part B. House rules

Every chapter follows these. Entries refer to them by short name.

| Rule | In brief |
|---|---|
| **Caulukya governors** | Rājyapāla (1163) and Lūṇapasāka (early 1170s) governed Udaypur for the Caulukya kings of Gujarat, not for the Paramāras. No date is recorded for the Paramāra return. |
| **Bhoja's brother** | Udayāditya was Bhoja's brother, citing the Dōngargāon and Jainād inscriptions. The dissent (the Nagpur *bandhu*, "a relative in general"; Ganguly's junior branch; a rejected sixteenth-century pedigree) goes in one note. Never "nephew". |
| **1080 firm, 1059 a reading** | Wherever dates are treated, say whose reading 1059 is and why it is less secure. Accession c. 1070 is the working position. |
| **Three late records kept apart** | Three records are never merged until checked against Bühler and Cunningham: the 1513 record (Ganguly), the V.S. 1562 Udayapura pedigree, and the long, faulty V.S. 1116 inscription. |
| **Karṇa by dynasty** | Always the Kalacuri Karṇa or the Caulukya Karṇa. Where a source does not say which, keep it unspecified and attribute it. |
| **Dynasty names** | IAST forms: Kalacuri, Candella, Caulukya (Gujarat), Cālukya (Deccan). The familiar form is given once, in Ch 1. Quotations keep their own spelling. |
| **Dated geography** | No administrative term is used before its first record. The Bhilsa province and the 64 villages of Bhṛṅgārikā belong to the 1170s, never to the founding. |
| **Testimony** | Tiwari's *Jagta Hua Kasba* is testimony to modern life. It never supplies a measurement, distance, count, date or medieval fact. Tiwari is named in full once (Ch 1), with no gendered pronouns. |
| **Claims stay claims** | A praśasti's statements are claims; tradition is memory; survey dates are proposals; the Gazetteer's statements are phrased as its own ("The district Gazetteer credits…"). |
| **One home** | Each topic is told in full once (Part C says where); elsewhere it is recalled in a clause. Similar things are not merged unless a source says they are one. |
| **Proportion** | The 1979 Gazetteer supplies at most 40% of a section's sources. Accepted exceptions: 8.5 and 10.5. |
| **Settled ranges** | Udaypur is 13–16 km from Ganj Basoda and 55–70 km from Vidisha. |
| **Present-day science** | Modern climate and flow studies describe today's regime only and go in notes. |
| **Excluded sources** | *Temple Economics* is excluded (its Udaipur is in Rajasthan). The architectural treatise is a norm, never evidence of Udaypur. The colonial reports are used only through later scholars until they are corrected and added to the corpus. |
| **Legal care** | Tiwari's mining allegation gets one attributed sentence, naming no one. The 2021 signboard's claimants are not named. Both passages go to legal review. |
| **Length** | Sections land at 70–120% of target. Never pad. |

# Part C. The chapters

## Chapter 1. Udaypur: A Small Town with a Long Memory

**Target:** 4,000 words. **Question:** How did an ordinary village come to hold the remains, records and memories of a far more important place, and what royal act stands at its beginning? **Argument:** The village lives inside a Paramāra king's town. Everything it keeps reaches back to one eleventh-century act of naming, and the evidence leaves open whether that act founded the place or took it over. **Hands on to Ch 2:** the stone and water both answers stand on.

**In the Paramāra story:** the polity in outline, with the founder's kinship, the triad of names and the later masters (told in full in Chs 4 and 10). **In the Udaypur story:** the village today, its layers, and how it is known.

### 1.1 The approach and the village *(the town today · 650 w)*
Establishes the village as it is: large, ordinary, living below the hill and inside a much greater medieval town.
1. **The approach:** the road from Ganj Basoda, the Shiv pindi hill, the temple seen before the village; the 1979 Gazetteer's "once a place of considerable importance" (planted here; recalled at the end of Ch 10).
2. **A village by its counts:** panchayat, the 2011 census (6,383), houses, schools, the Monday market.
3. **Tiwari introduced:** Vijay Manohar Tiwari, named in full once, as the witness to modern life.

### 1.2 Inside the walled town *(keepers of time and memory · 600 w)*
Establishes the walled town as lived in, and the village's own memory.
1. **Gates and lanes:** Teen Darwaza, lanes narrowed by houses, dry-masonry dwellings, the twelve-courtyard house.
2. **The time-keepers:** the Ghadiyali family and the bells at the hours of worship.
3. **Remembered water:** the tally of stepwells, wells and lakes, as memory; the tank's bed now fields.
4. **Berni Mata:** the legend told once, with its *samādhi* version and Vyas's *yakṣa* reading in a note (home of the versions: 9.5).
5. **The temple in worship:** the east-facing temple under the ASI; daily worship and the fair; Hindus and Muslims at the shrine in the 1870s; the name confused with Udaipur; the snake-and-fire founding legend.

### 1.3 A king's town on Malwa's eastern edge *(the Paramāras in outline · 750 w)*
Establishes the Paramāras as a kingdom: ruled from Dhārā, reaching Bhilsa, nearly lost at Bhoja's death and recovered by his brother.
1. **A kingdom ruled from Dhārā:** its eastern reach, where it met the Kalacuris (Kalachuris) and Candellas (Chandelas); the familiar spellings given here once.
2. **The fall and the recovery:** Bhoja's death at war; Jayasiṃha restored with Cālukya help; the Kalacuri Karṇa's return; Udayāditya, Bhoja's brother, accession c. 1070; the Nagpur Boar image as the dynasty's telling (its Karṇa identified only by the editor).
3. **One name for three works:** Udayapura, Udayeśvara, Udayasamudra; V.S. 1116 and 1137 as readings; the question of a lord's seat or a new king's work, stated once.
4. **How the east was governed:** *maṇḍala*s, *bhoga*s and their officers; the eastern division under the Caulukyas in the 1160s; the Bhilsa province in the 1170s; the Nēmaka house in a damaged record.

### 1.4 Eight centuries in one precinct *(layers of rule, 1133–1784 · 750 w)*
Establishes the town's later masters in sequence, as an outline for Ch 10.
1. **The kingdom breaks:** Yaśovarman and Gujarat; the Candellas at Bhilsa (1134); the *mahākumāra*s; the Caulukya governors; Devapāla in residence (early 1230s); Jayavarman to 1269.
2. **1305 and the mosque in the court:** ʿAin-ul-Mulk; the court's plan; the mosque of 1336–38 on the western hall; the tradition of 1325.
3. **Mandu and the Mughals:** the mosque of 1488; the Shahi Masjid, 1632; the governor's mansion, its founder disputed.
4. **Later patrons, custody now:** the brass cover of V.S. 1841; "Garde Sahib" (Tiwari's phrase); the state's departments as custodians.

### 1.5 How Udaypur is known *(the record · 700 w)*
Establishes the kinds of evidence and what each can bear.
1. **Two broken slabs:** the praśasti, its fire-born hero (legend, in the editor's judgement), its uncorroborated claims; Garde's find of the second slab (1925–26).
2. **The firm date:** 1080 as the earliest secure date, and its three readings (flag hoisted, staff raised, staff repaired); the "1059–1080" caption claims too much.
3. **Pilgrims and first readers:** inscriptions in three languages; Turasadasa (V.S. 1696); Beglar, Cunningham, Bühler; a slab reset face-out.
4. **Stories of survival:** Tughluq's gunpowder, Aurangzeb, a temple built in a night, treasure, the flour-grinder woman. These are memory, tested in 10.3.

### 1.6 Founded or taken over? *(the open question · 550 w)*
Establishes the question as genuine.
1. **An older place proposed:** the Bhujariya Talab, the eighth-century inscription, reused columns, an uncemented wall (survey proposals).
2. **A new town argued:** the argument from absence and the frontier post (Tiwari); the Bhringrajpur and Gond traditions, told from the founder's side.
3. **Two acts of rule:** founding or re-inscription; the later records (Udhapuram, the 1513 record) cannot choose.
4. **Stone and water:** the quarry beside the older tank. Hand on to Ch 2.

## Chapter 2. The Betwa Valley: Geography, Geology and Natural Environment

**Target:** 4,000 words. **Question:** What could this country give, and what did it withhold, to the people who settled and built at Udaypur? **Argument:** The founder found no river at the door and no dependable groundwater, one wet season, black soil and a hill of building stone. Water had to be made and the town was built of its own rock. The land is the setting, never the cause. **Picks up:** stone and water. **Hands on to Ch 3:** whether anyone used the country before Udaypur had its name.

**In the Paramāra story:** the Betwa as the Vētravatī of the inscriptions; Bhoja's dam as a comparison. **In the Udaypur story:** the setting every later chapter assumes.

### 2.1 The river of the inscriptions *(the Betwa and its country · 600 w)*
Udaypur belongs to the Betwa's country but not to its banks.
1. **Vētravatī:** the river by Vidisha and Bhilsa; Bhilsa as Bhoja's eastern limit; the *mahākumāra*s against the Candellas across the river.
2. **The course:** source, tributaries, the district sloping north; the Kewtan 1 km from the village; no river through Udaypur. Stream names are kept apart.

### 2.2 A river of the rains, ground that fails *(flow and groundwater · 650 w)*
1. **A river of the rains:** intermittent upstream, with most of its runoff in the monsoon (today's regime).
2. **Groundwater that fails:** wells in the sandstone dry in summer.
3. **Water in the hollows:** lakes and ponds in depressions; settlement shaped by water.

### 2.3 Four months of rain and two harvests *(climate, soil, crops · 750 w)*
1. **The rains:** 92% in four months.
2. **Bad years in runs:** 1901–50; the famine of 1899–1900; the 1951 deluge.
3. **Black soil and the two harvests:** *mar* soil, *kharif* and *rabi*, Udaypur's crops; the winter crop lives on stored water.
4. **What is not known:** whether river silt reaches Udaypur. The climate trend goes in a note.

### 2.4 A seam of stone *(geology, landform, forest · 800 w)*
1. **The range:** the Garhi–Teonda range and its western foothills.
2. **Trap stripped from sandstone:** the temple's stone "quarried in the hill close by".
3. **A wasting tableland:** mesa, butte and pinnacle as a proposed pathway; Shiva Pindi and its caves (the survey's heights disagree).
4. **The forest:** reserved forest, grazed and cut.

### 2.5 Water made by kings and villagers *(storage · 750 w)*
1. **Bhoja's lake, by comparison:** the Bhojpur dams and their breach.
2. **Water in the treatise:** three kinds of country, as norms only.
3. **Udaypur's water, sorted by use:** the triad's tank; tanks for fields, wells for drinking; *kund*s on the hill; stepwells with proposed dates; the Bhujariya Talab possibly older. Landscape level only; the detail is in Ch 7.

### 2.6 A town of its own stone *(stone then and now · 450 w)*
1. **Built from its ground:** dry masonry; stone shops; the quarries and their export; illegal quarrying, from Govind Saxena's report.
2. **What the ground does not say:** stone at hand, rain to be held; nothing decided. Hand on to Ch 3.

**Drafting notes:**
- The land is never a cause.
- Present-day data stay in notes.
- Keep water at landscape level; the detail is Ch 7's.

**Key sources:** the Gazetteer, the INTACH geo-heritage survey, the modern Betwa climate and flow studies (present only), Ganguly and Mahesh Singh (Bhojpur). **Open:** the chapter is short (2,608 words as written, decision D5); the survey's heights; the stream names; a duplicated phrase in the river paragraph, to fix.

## Chapter 3. Before Udaypur: Prehistory, Early Vidisha and the Historical Landscape of Central India

**Target:** 3,550 words. **Question:** What was already in this country when a king put his name on Udaypur's hill, and how much of that past can be found at the hill itself? **Argument:** The hill stood in an old, named country whose centre, roads and carved shrines lay elsewhere and reached the hill only as proposals. By the tenth century its own range was a crowded frontier. Vidisha enters only where it changes what Udaypur means. **Hands on to Ch 4:** "Who held the hill itself in those years is not known."

**In the Paramāra story:** the powers they displaced, namely the Rāṣṭrakūṭas, Pratihāras and Candellas. **In the Udaypur story:** the province's name and the hill's earliest proposed traces.

### 3.1 Old ground *(prehistory · 450 w)*
1. **Traces in the valleys:** stone tools; a chalcolithic mound; the ASI's sequence near Vidisha.
2. **Nothing on the hill:** the caves recorded, their early use unknown.

### 3.2 The country's names *(Daśārṇa and Ākara · 450 w)*
1. **Daśārṇa:** the literary name and the Dhasan.
2. **Ākara:** the inscriptions' east Malwa; the Haihayas and the Pradyotas (Patil's certainty in a note).
3. **Vidisha at the centre.**

### 3.3 Vidisha and its roads *(the old centre · 600 w)*
1. **The city in the fork:** Besnagar, its excavation, the palace wall.
2. **Śuṅga Vidisha:** Agnimitra, Bhagabhadra.
3. **Two roads and a crossing:** none mentions Udaypur's hill.
4. **Wealth in stone:** Sanchi and Vidisha's merchants.

### 3.4 Worship in stone before the Paramāras *(the sacred landscape · 800 w)*
1. **Viṣṇu's early temples:** the elliptical temple; Heliodoros's pillar.
2. **Śaiva images:** the colossi; the Mothers and *liṅga*s of Besnagar.
3. **The Guptas in the hills:** Udayagiri and its Boar; the flat-roofed shrines at Ramgarh, Chhapara and Badoh.
4. **Onto Udaypur's range:** the Seven Mothers panel between Badoh and Pathari.

## Chapter 5. The Udayeśvara Temple: Architecture, Sculpture and Religious Iconography

**Target:** 4,000 words. **Question:** What is the temple by its own fabric, and what has its image programme been made to mean? **Argument:** It is the fullest statement of the Bhūmija system: a rotated-square star of seven tiers whose wall projections rise into the tower. Its walls carry a programme set by rule (dancers, guardians, Mothers), which Pande reads as Śaiva Siddhānta. Description comes first and interpretation after, attributed and bounded. Its losses (court shrines, the western hall) are part of the evidence. **Picks up:** the dated temple of Ch 4. **Hands on to Ch 6:** the unread name at the east door.

**In the Paramāra story:** the reign's one securely dated work, read as a royal gift in stone. **In the Udaypur story:** the building every later layer is set against.

### 5.1 The court and the building it holds *(plan, enclosure, losses · 750 w)*
Establishes the eastern axis, the hall and three porches, the separate *vedī*, and a court of attendant shrines, some ruined and some gone.
1. **The design's own axis:** sanctum, vestibule, hall, porches and *vedī* on an east–west line, now entered from the west.
2. **Hall and porches:** the public interior, balconied porches with seats and guardians, the main east entrance.
3. **The *vedī*:** a separate low, grilled, flat-roofed building on the axis. The treatise's pavilion rule is a norm, not proof of purpose.
4. **The court and its shrines:** eight in Pande's count, some in ruin and two gone; Kramrisch reads an eight-temple composition. This section is the home of the shrine count, and Ch 1's description of the court is not contradicted.
5. **What is missing:** the western hall and gateway, as fabric. The mosque is recalled in a clause from Ch 1.

### 5.2 A star of seven tiers *(the Bhūmija rule and the tower · 650 w)*
Establishes how the plan becomes the tower.
1. **The rotating square:** the star plan from a rotated square. The treatise's rule is normative.
2. **Wall becomes tower:** the wall's offsets continue as seven tiers of five miniature spires between four spines.
3. **The antefixes:** the frames over the porch roofs and the figures they carry.

### 5.3 Where Udayeśvara stands *(origin and comparisons · 550 w)*

1. **No precedent:** the origin argued three ways (Malwa, the Deccan, the lower Deccan), each attributed, the question left open.
2. **Neighbours and successors:** Un, Nemawar, Onkar Mandhata, Jamli and the Deccan series. The Amareśvara's "1063" is unverified, and Vidisha's Bijamandal is not Udaypur's.

### 5.4 Surfaces *(mouldings, doorways, pillars, ceilings · 550 w)*
Establishes the craft, and that the ornament's dating is a proposal.
1. **Base and wall:** the moulding sequence, read as workmanship.
2. **Doorways:** the sanctum and hall frames.
3. **Pillars and ceilings:** the central pillars, the ceiling, and the lotus-bud ornament as a proposed stylistic date.
4. **What the craft shows:** one workshop's finish. Construction time appears only as memory, never as a figure.

### 5.5 The gods on the walls *(by position · 650 w)*
Establishes the programme by position only.
1. **The dancer at the head of each face:** the dancing Śiva images on the cardinal faces.
2. **Guardians in their quarters:** the *dikpāla*s set by rule, each with his mount (Īśāna's crown described by Pande).
3. **Mothers round the sanctum:** the eight Mothers on each spine, with Vīrabhadra and Cāmuṇḍā.

### 5.6 The many and the few, and the broken *(remaining images · 400 w)*
1. **Single images:** Naṭeśa by the south porch, Umā-Maheśvara, Gaṇeśa, the three-faced Brahmā, Sūrya; Pārvatī and Sarasvatī as Mahesh Singh singles them out.

## Chapter 10. Political Change and Economic Decline: Udaypur under Successive Regimes

**Target:** 4,200 words. **Question:** When and how did Udaypur stop being a seat of power, and what can "decline" mean for it? **Argument:** It was not destroyed by one conquest. It was lowered in rank step by step over six centuries, while each new power still invested in it:
- an administrative seat under several masters, including the Caulukya governors;
- a royal residence in the 1230s;
- a *qasba* of Sarkar Chanderi;
- a large village.

Economic decline, as distinct from political demotion, is an inference the evidence cannot yet date. **Picks up:** remembered decline. **Hands on to Ch 11:** the official verdict.

**The lineage after Udayāditya** (Part A.2) is told here, from Naravarman to 1305. Each period is asked the same four questions: rank, patronage, settlement and institutions. **Waits on:** the V.S. 1841 check.

### 10.1 A seat that changed hands *(c. 1093–c. 1190 · 800 w)*
1. **A Gujarat king named at Udaypur:** the V.S. 1220 record, the first stone at Udaypur to name a king of another house, and the chapter's question.
2. **A kingdom fraying at its eastern edge:** Naravarman and the Candellas on the Betwa.
3. **The branch that held the east:** the *mahākumāra*s, "at least up to 1157".
4. **A Caulukya headquarters:** Kumārapāla's governor Rājyapāla (the title *mahāsādhanika* explained), then Lūṇapasāka under Ajayapāla. The Caulukya conquest of Malwa is dated 1138–45.
5. **Recovered, and reunited:** Vindhyavarman restores Paramāra rule (no date recorded for Udaypur), and Devapāla's line succeeds.

### 10.2 A king's residence on a shrinking frontier *(c. 1190–1305 · 800 w)*

2. **What the king's records give:** rank and endowment, read for who governed (texts in Ch 6, gifts in Ch 8).

### 10.3 An end that took decades *(1305–c. 1530 · 850 w)*
1. **The record after the end:** a Mahārājādhirāja Jayasiṃha inside the east entrance (V.S. 1366, in Ganguly's report), introduced fresh.
2. **A mosque in the temple court:** a new institution, with the shrine left in worship.

### 10.4 A *qasba* of Sarkar Chanderi *(1528–c. 1720 · 750 w)*
