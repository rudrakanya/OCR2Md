# Style sheet: the Udaypur history

*Copy-editing conventions. Decided once and applied everywhere. Voice, structure and evidence rules are in `style_profile.md`; this sheet does not override it.*

## 1. Spelling and fixed names

- British spelling with **-ise**: organise, recognise; centre, colour, metre.
- **Udaypur** is this town in Vidisha district. **Udaipur** is used only for the city in Rajasthan.
- Fixed forms: Udayāditya, Udayeśvara, Paramāra(s), Bhoja, Dhārā, Bhūmija, Ganj Basoda, Betwa.
- **Dynasty names take IAST forms** (user decision D1, 16 Oct 2026): Kalacuri, Candella, Caulukya (the Gujarat house), Cālukya (the Deccan house), Paramāra, Rāṣṭrakūṭa, Pratihāra, Cāhamāna.
  - At the name's first mention in the book, give the familiar form once, in brackets: "the Candellas (Chandelas)", "the Kalacuris (Kalachuris)", "the Caulukyas (Chaulukyas)", "the Cālukya (Chalukya) king". These are in §1.2.
  - Verbatim quotations keep their source's spelling.
  - The free check `_tools/dynasty_spelling.py check`, also run by `inhouse.py check`, flags any other form outside quotation marks.
- **Vidisha** is the modern town and the ancient city. **Besnagar** is the ancient site. **Bhilsa** is the medieval and pre-1956 town. Write "Bhilsa (now Vidisha)" once in the book.
- **Vētravatī** appears only where the inscriptions' name for the Betwa matters: "in which the river appears as the Vētravatī".

## 2. Indic terms

- Use IAST diacritics for Sanskrit and Hindi terms and for transliterated titles.
- At a term's first appearance in the **book**, italicise it and gloss it once: "a *panchayat*, a unit of village government". The term ledger records where each gloss has been given. After that, keep the italic and give no further gloss.
- *praśasti* is always italic and lower case.
- Never italicise proper names (people, places, dynasties, temples, deities, families) as terms: Udayeśvara temple, Shiv pindi, Berni Mata. Titles of texts are italic because they are titles: *Jagta Hua Kasba*. Names of monuments are roman: Teen Darwaza, not *Teen Darwaza*.
- English plurals of Indic terms take a roman "s": *liṅga*s.

## 3. Dates and eras

- Write "1059", "c. 1070" and "the eleventh century" (spelled out, lower case). Hyphenate the adjective: "eleventh-century temple".
- Ranges take an en dash and a two-digit closing number: 1058–59, 1870–71. Write "the 1870s", with no apostrophe.
- Use **BCE/CE**, never BC/AD, and only where a date could be ambiguous (mostly ancient Besnagar). Medieval and modern years are unmarked.
- Vikrama Saṃvat: write "V.S. 1116 (1059 CE)" at its first appearance in the book. One note explains the conversion and why years can be off by one. Afterwards write "V.S. 1116 (1059)".

## 4. Numbers and measurements

- Spell out one to ninety-nine: "fifty-two wells and twelve and a half lakes".
- Use numerals for measurements, dates, percentages, figures in a run of statistics, and 100 and above: 6.5 km, 410 m, 15 m below the surface; "a population of 6,383 and about 1,247 houses".
- Write "per cent" as two words: "nearly 80 per cent". Do not use %.
- Use metric units, abbreviated after a numeral: 1.8 km, 13–16 km. Spell the unit out only when no numeral is attached ("some kilometres off"), or at the start of a sentence, where both number and unit are spelled out ("Ten kilometres out on the road…"). Miles appear only inside quotations.
- Use commas in thousands (6,383); lakh and crore only in quotation.

## 5. Quotation

- Use double curly quotes “ ”, with single curly quotes ‘ ’ for a quotation inside a quotation. Apostrophes are curly (’).
- Punctuation goes outside the closing quote unless it belongs to the quoted words: could still call it “once a place of considerable importance”.
- Use a block quotation only for a passage over about forty words, and rarely. It has no quotation marks.
- Use … for omissions and [ ] for insertions. Quote spelling exactly.

## 6. People

- Give a full name at the first mention in the book, and the surname afterwards: Vijay Manohar Tiwari, then Tiwari. Do not use gendered pronouns for Tiwari; repeat the name or recast the sentence.
- Name scholars in the text only when their identity matters (`style_profile.md` §C). Otherwise they go in the notes.
- Kings take no honorifics in the narrator's voice; inscriptional titles appear only in quotation.

## 7. Tense

- Use the past for historical events: "Bhoja died", "Udayāditya recovered the kingdom".
- Use the present for surviving objects ("the temple comes into view"), for what sources and inscriptions say ("a later Paramāra inscription remembers"), and for the village today.
- Where the source is decades old, date the observation: "In 1979 the village had primary and higher secondary schools…".

## 8. Terms in the narrator's voice

- Use "Muslim", never "Mohammedan" or "Muhammadan". Use "Hindu", never "Hindoo". These old forms survive only inside verbatim quotation.
- Use "Sultanate", "Mughal", "Scindia", "Malwa" and "Archaeological Survey of India" (ASI after the first mention).
- Avoid "native" for people, "aboriginal", "tribe/tribal" as a label for unnamed people, "idol" (use "image"), "pagoda", "Gentoo", "Moorish", "infidel", "caste" as a loose synonym for community, and "Orient/oriental".
- No colonial spellings (Bhelsa, Betwah) outside quotation.

## 9. Notes

- Follow Chicago notes-bibliography style.
- Consolidate to one note per paragraph or claim cluster, at the end of the sentence or clause it supports. Do not end a paragraph with a run of notes.
- Give the full citation at a source's first appearance in the book and the short form (author, short title, page) after that. Do not use *ibid.*; repeat the short form instead.
- Use discursive notes for historiography, disagreements and minor variants: "Ganguly reconstructed this eastern limit from the inscriptions together with Al Bērūnī's statement."
- Put "See" before a source that supports an explanation rather than a quoted or stated fact.
- In drafts, `[ID]` markers and `{{note: … [IDs]}}` stand in for notes; leave them in place.

## 10. Punctuation, hyphens and dashes

- Use an en dash, unspaced, for ranges: 13–16 km, pp. 41–47, the eighth–ninth century. Write "from … to" and "between … and" in full, never "from 1058–59 to".
- Use em dashes unspaced (a remembered abundance—rather than…) and sparingly: at most one pair per paragraph. Prefer commas, parentheses or a new sentence.
- Use the serial (Oxford) comma: "worship, ablution, and other rites".
- Hyphenate compound directions: south-west, north-east.
- Hyphenate compound adjectives before a noun ("black-soil fields", "a twelfth-century grant") but not after it.

## 11. Capitalisation

- Lower case "the temple"; "Udayeśvara temple"; "the Udaysagar" as a name, but "the tank".
- Directions are lower case: "south-west of Raisen district". Capitalise them only in a name ("Central India" in a title).
- Capitalise "the Paramāra kingdom" and "the Gazetteer" (the 1979 district volume), but lower case "the survey", "the inscription" and "the dynasty".
- Centuries, seasons and the monsoon are lower case. Headings use sentence case.

## 12. Mechanical checks the code enforces

1. **-ize forms**: any -ize, -ization or -izing word outside quotation is flagged.
2. **Straight quotes**: straight " or ' in prose is flagged; quotes and apostrophes must be curly.
3. **Numerals under 100**: a bare numeral from 1 to 99 is flagged unless it is a date, is followed by a unit (km, m, ha) or "per cent", has a decimal point, or sits in a run of statistics.
4. **Repetition**: the same content word (excluding names and function words) used within two consecutive sentences is flagged.
5. **Uniform openings**: a paragraph whose sentences all begin with the same word is flagged.
6. **Banned terms**: Mohammedan, Muhammadan, Hindoo, "Udaipur" outside a Rajasthan context, and BC/AD are flagged outside quotation.
7. **Spacing**: spaced em dashes, hyphens in numeric ranges, and "%" are flagged.

## 13. Canon carried forward (settled before the 17 October 2026 redraft)

These bind every chapter. The full table is in `editorial/Udaypur_Complete_Book_Outline.md`, Part B.

- **U1, the Caulukya governors.** Rājyapāla (1163) and Lūṇapasāka (early 1170s) governed Udaypur for the Caulukya kings of Gujarat (Kumārapāla, Ajayapāla), not for the Paramāras. No date is recorded for the Paramāra return.
- **R1, three late records kept apart.** The 1513 record (Ganguly), the V.S. 1562 Udayapura pedigree, and the long, faulty V.S. 1116 inscription are never merged.
- **R2, Karṇa by dynasty.** Always "the Kalacuri Karṇa" or "the Caulukya Karṇa". Where a source does not say which, keep it unspecified and attribute it.
- **R3, the V.S. 1116 records.** They stay distinct until checked against Bühler and Cunningham. 1080 (V.S. 1137) is the earliest firm date; 1059 is a reading, and the text says whose. Accession c. 1070 is the working position.
- **R4, dated geography.** No administrative, territorial or institutional term is used before its earliest attesting record. The Bhāillasvāmi-mahādvādaśaka and Bhṛṅgārikā's sixty-four villages belong to the 1170s; the "eastern division" to 1163.
- **D1:** IAST dynasty names (§1).
- **D2, the kinship.** Udayāditya was "Bhoja's brother", as two inscriptions of the time of his son Jagaddēva state (Dōngargāon, 1112; Jainād). The dissent goes in one note. Never "nephew".
- **D4, the Nagpur Karṇa.** The verse names Karṇa without saying which; the editor of the Paramāra inscriptions is inclined to take him for the Kalacuri king. This is said in a note.
- **Other settled rules:**
  - *Temple Economics* is excluded (its Udaipur is in Rajasthan).
  - The *Samarāṅgaṇasūtradhāra* is a norm, never evidence of Udaypur's practice.
  - Tiwari's testimony never supplies a measurement, distance, count or date.
  - Settled ranges: 13–16 km from Ganj Basoda, 55–70 km from Vidisha.
  - Each subject is told in full in its home chapter only (one-home rule).
