**Historical question.** What does the Udayeśvara, read as evidence, show about the king who built it, the faith it served, the craft behind it and its place among the temples of its age, and how far can its meanings be read?

**Through-line.** By the end, the reader knows the parts of a northern temple and how Udayāditya's builders used them; the temple's body, its separate *vedī* and its court of attendant shrines, with what is lost; the spire and the star plan that make it a Bhūmija temple, the debate over the style's origin, its standing among the Paramāra temples and the Khajuraho-or-Deccan question; the craft of its base, wall, doorways, pillars and ornament, and what is known of its stone and masons; the image programme set by rule (the dancing Śiva of the antefixes, the guardians of the quarters, the Mothers, the hall's gods, the composite images, the couples, the porch figures) and its damage; the sculptural style; the texts that set norms for images and buildings, the treatise attributed to Bhoja, the Śaiva Siddhānta doctrine; and the readings laid on the programme and on the temple as cosmos, each attributed and bounded.

**Arc.** The type and the building as it stands (5.1–5.4) → the tower and its style (5.5–5.9) → the surfaces and the makers (5.10–5.13) → the gods by position (5.14–5.20) → condition and style (5.21–5.22) → the texts (5.23–5.25) → doctrine and readings (5.26–5.28), ending on what the temple's own inscribed words say, which Chapter 6 reads.
