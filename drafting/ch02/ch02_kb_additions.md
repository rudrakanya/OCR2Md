## Geo-Heritage of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh: The Third Eye (GEOHER, 6)

Bhujariya Talab is a natural water body located to the southeast of the village Udaypur. This large water reservoir located on the outskirts of the village near a live stone quarry covering an area of 8.04 acre. This talab is one of the “chappan baoli, bavan kuae and saadhe baarah talab”, or 56 stepwells, 52 wells and 12 and half lakes, according to the locals. When King Udayaditya laid the foundation for the city of Udaypur and Udayeshwar, also known as Neelkanthswar Temple, in the 11th century CE, it is also mentioned in the Udaypur Prashasti that he built a lake called Udaysagar.
`C02-KB-GEOHER-001`   p. 75   (step B; sub S5; direct)

Drinking water, instead, was obtained from a ground water source like a dug well, or from a stepwell as documented.
Such Kund/water tanks were invariably positioned between the settlement and the talav. Observations show that wherever the talav still holds water, where the talav has been filled in or its sources blocked, the Kund on the side of hill slope has dried out. This points to an implicit relationship between surface water of talav and water of stepwells.
`C02-KB-GEOHER-002`   p. 76   (step B; sub S5; direct)

A baoli is a reservoir in which water can be stored. It is also a source of groundwater. Numerous tanks and baolis were commissioned by the ruling clans in which water was collected mainly during the rainy season and was then used throughout the year by the people in the neighborhood.
The baoli at the middle hill level supposedly could have been used as a major source of water to meet the needs of the village; however, some fragments of columns with traditional carvings indicate a particular time period of rich Hindu architecture designs over it.
`C02-KB-GEOHER-003`   p. 112   (step B; sub S5; direct)

At about 100 meters from this Natraj sculpture, we see a long wall that measures 1400 meters. It is a very thick wall encircling the hillock from all side, a close examination of this 1400 m wall show that this is a fortification wall, meaning that the encircled land is part of a fort complex, which existed prior to Udayaditya, or could have been constructed during the time of Udayaditya as a military Cantonment, because this was the most secured hillock, with very steep slopes.
Walking along this hill fortification wall, we see bastions of different dimensions, the semicircular bastion, the rectangular bastion, and gateways on both sides of the entry gate.
`C02-KB-GEOHER-004`   p. 19   (step B; sub S5; direct)

The slopes of Pindi Hill on the western side have a large three-sided stepwell, which sometimes must have been used as a reservoir to cater to the needs of users of this hill area. This water retention structure is unique in many aspects, such as:
• It has a regular shape on three sides
• The steps going down to the base are 20 in number.

Udaypur's allure extends beyond the temple, with a distinctive feature being its abundance of dry masonry stone structures in abundance all over. These traditional dwellings, constructed from locally sourced materials, showcase the architectural prowess of the region.

## Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha — Government of Madhya Pradesh (GAZ, 8)

The Bina and Nion or Nihar rivers rise in Raisen District on either side of the Vindhya spur called Garhi-Teonda range. The Bina flows to the north in Raisen and Sagar districts and receives the waters of the Babnai and the Bagaru on its left bank. Both these tributaries of the Bina drain the eastern part of Vidisha District. The Bina enters the plain of Vidisha District in its later course and separates Sagar district from Vidisha.
`C02-KB-GAZ-001`   p. 20   (step B; sub S1; direct)

The river joins the Betwa 3 km. north of Vidisha. Its total length is about 65 km.
`C02-KB-GAZ-002`   p. 19   (step B; sub S1; direct)

Groundwater conditions within the Vindhyan are mostly far from being satisfactory. Wells within the formation generally dry up in summer, and the water level is fairly deep—more than 15 metres.
`C02-KB-GAZ-004`   p. 23   (step B; sub S5; direct)

Vidisha is predominantly a rabi crop area, the proportion of rabi to kharif being 65:35 in the year 1971-72. In the same year 391.8 thousand hactares were devoted to the cultivation of rabi crops and 109.6 thousand hactares to kharif crops. Amongst the crops, wheat is the most important crop, commanding 50 per cent of the cropped area of the District in 1971-72. Next in importance came gram and jowar each of which was responsible for 18 and 8 per cent of the cropped area respectively, in the same year the latter of which is by far the largest kharif crop of the District.
`C02-KB-GAZ-005`   p. 99   (step B; sub S5; contextual)

In Malwa, fields are first broken up and cleared of weeds by means of the bakhar, a sort of harrow or weeding plough. Ploughing (halna) for the autumn crops then commences about the end of May. As soon as sufficient rain has fallen to moisten the soil the field is sown (boni). The spring crops are sown till October, the ground being ploughed several times in
`C02-KB-GAZ-006`   p. 100   (step B; sub S5; direct)

Most soils in the District are capable of bearing two crops and double cropped area of Vidisha amounts to 10 thousand ha (1971-72). Mixed sowings are also common. Jowar is sown in the same field with tuar, maize with urad and wheat with linseed. The agricultural year contains two seasons, the kharif or shialu, the autumn crop season and the rabi or unhalu the spring crop season.
`C02-KB-GAZ-007`   p. 100   (step B; sub S5; direct)

The Garhi-Teonda range extends from south to north between the valleys of the Betwa and the Bina. It is the most extensive hill range in the District, and also the most prominent, the highest peak of Lakholi The Eastern Range (665.7 meters) being located in its southern part. Other peaks are near Mahuakhera (60J metres), Nawab Basoda (630.3 metres), Teonda (569.3 metres), and Pathari (575.5 metres). The average elevation is 520 metres.
`C02-KB-GAZ-008`   p. 17   (step B; sub S2; direct)

The hills and slopes are covered with forests. Gyaraspur and Udaipur are located amidst west side foothills of this range. Basoda, Mahammadgarh, Bagrod, Teonda and Pathari settle near the eastern slopes of this range.
`C02-KB-GAZ-009`   p. 17   (step B; sub S2; direct)
