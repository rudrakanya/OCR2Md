**Historical question.** What do the walls, shrines, mosques and water works beyond the great temple tell about who held the hill, how the town was organised, how worship and community changed, how several faiths built on one ground, and how a dry country was supplied with water?

**Through-line.** By the end, the reader knows how the monuments have been counted and dated; the hill wall, what it enclosed and why its date is open; the treatise's norms for forts and towns; the hill's panels, its cave and its abandoned colossus; the walled town, its gates, palace and governor's mansion, its lanes and reused stone houses; the Bijamandal, the Barakhambi and Pisanhārī, the later temples, Jain images and memorials; three dated mosques and the undated Muslim monuments; the water works of hill and town, the tanks, Vikrama's tank and the treatise's classes of water structure, leading to the gifts of Chapter 8.

**Arc.** Counting (7.1) → the hill (7.2–7.7) → the town (7.8–7.11) → shrines after the temple (7.12–7.15) → mosques and Muslim monuments (7.16–7.19) → water (7.20–7.25).
