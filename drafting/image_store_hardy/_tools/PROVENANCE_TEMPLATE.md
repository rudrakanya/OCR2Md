# Provenance

- **Book:** Adam Hardy, *The Temple Architecture of India* (Chichester: John Wiley & Sons, 2007).
- **Source PDF:** `PDF Originals/{pdf}` ({size} MB, {pages} pages; an Internet Archive scan, archive.org/details/templearchitectu0000hard, digitized 2022 from a Toronto Public Library copy; repackaged by {creator}).
- **Extracted:** {date}, locally, with no paid API: PyMuPDF (page rendering, text layer), OpenCV/NumPy (region detection), Tesseract 5.4 `eng` (caption cross-reading). {n} image files.
- **Scan:** every page is one colour JPEG-2000 scan, 2700 px wide (≈ 355–385 dpi depending on page width), stored as MRC layers with a hidden OCR text layer. There are no per-figure embedded images, so each figure was cropped from the page rendered at the scan's own resolution (nothing resampled up).
- **Labels:** the author numbers figures chapter.figure ("1.8", "24.6"), printed in bold at the head of each caption, and cites them that way in the text ("(1.8, 1.9)"). The front matter has four maps ("Map 1"–"Map 4"). There is no List of Illustrations; the captions on the page are the only authority. Unnumbered images (cover, frontispiece, six part-opener photographs, one tailpiece ornament) are stored with descriptive labels.
- **Captions:** each caption region was located from the hidden text layer and the page image, then read by Tesseract at the scan's native resolution. On this scan that reading is markedly better than the Internet Archive OCR layer, which splits words, drops lines and absorbs picture noise. The two readings were then reconciled: (1) a fixed table of glyph confusions both engines make (`rn`→`m` as in "Kamataka", `¢` for the italic *c*, small-caps AD/BC) restores the printed spelling; (2) where a Tesseract word is unrecognised and the text layer has a recognised word in the same aligned slot, the text-layer word is used; (3) every remaining word found neither in the book's text layer nor in the project's other Markdown sources was checked against the caption image, and corrected by hand where it was a misreading (`overrides.json` → `caption_fixes`, `caption_text`). Numbers were cross-checked between the two readings and the conflicts read on the page. Captions that continue in another column or in legend paragraphs (3.3, 10.1, 11.10, 11.13, 13.5, 16.10, 16.12, 18.13) are stored whole. Both raw readings are kept on every record (`caption_tesseract`, `caption_textlayer`), with the corrections applied (`glyph_fixes`, `word_merges_from_textlayer`, `hand_fixes`). Transliteration is the author's own (no diacritics: "Shekhari", "Bhumija"); the store keeps it as printed.
- **Crops:** one rectangle per figure, cropped to the figure's own extent with a 6 px margin that never reaches into a caption; multi-part figures are saved whole and as parts, or as parts only where any single rectangle would take in body text. No image was rotated (none is printed sideways). Fig. 7.4 surrounds its own caption: that caption area is filled with the paper tone in the saved file. Hand decisions are in `_tools/overrides.json`.
- **Library marks:** the cover carries the Toronto Public Library's stickers; p. 263 has its stamp. They are part of the scan and are left as they are (cover) or not extracted (p. 263, publisher's advertisement pages).
- **Rebuild:** `_tools/captions.py` → `_tools/caption_regions.py` → `_tools/run_detect.py` → `_tools/build.py` (hand decisions in `_tools/overrides.json`; review sheets with `_tools/review.py`).
