### 6.1 The temple inscription of 1080 — FULL (for a six-line record)
- **What it says, line by line** (CII text; EN translation of Tiwari's Hindi, from Verma): l. 1 *svasti* and a wish that King Udayāditya make the earth one under a single umbrella; ll. 2–3 praise of the king as giver of *siddhi*, a prayer that he gain glory from the earth, an expression the editor finds unclear (CII n. 3), then the Sun's transit (*ravi-saṃkrānti*) and *karaṇa*; ll. 4–5 the signature: the śloka of Pandit Mahipāla, son of Pandit Śṛṅgavāsa; l. 5 the date V.S. 1137 Vaiśākha śu. 7, which CII n. 5 marks as the day the Ganges descended; l. 6 the flag-hoisting of Udayeśvaradeva is complete, *maṅgalaṃ mahāśrīḥ*.
- **Language and form:** Sanskrit; 11th-c. Nāgarī; prose with one *anuṣṭubh* verse (CII "Metre: Verse 1 Anushṭubh"; Verma).
- **Date:** earliest known date for Udayāditya (CII). Verma: no weekday, *karaṇa* unnamed; his calculation is Monday 30 March 1080 (in the note only; Tiwari never supplies a date).
- **What it does not say:** no geographical name (CII); no donor, builder or witness; no "Nīlakaṇṭheśvara".
- **Publication history:** noticed by Cunningham (ASR X p. 83), Bhandarkar (PRASWC 1913–14 p. 22) and Garde (Gwalior AR) — per Tiwari; in the note only.
- **Thin spots:** CII p. 112, the editor's description and find-notice, is missing from the OCR. CII n. 2 (a word "in imitation of the writings of old in which some king is addressed") cannot be tied to a line with certainty, so it is not used.
- **Candidates judged:**
  - Used: C04-CII-108 (earliest date).
  - Same content as cited EN items, so not used: JAGTA-HI-001/003/004/007/008/009/010/028/038/042.
  - Belong to 6.16 (archive counts): JAGTA-EN-003, -008, -025, -026.
  - Not usable here: GANGULY-068 (Abu), RAJABHOJ-EN-018 (Jala-maṅgala), CII-029 (plate list).

### 6.2 The long inscription of V.S. 1116 — LIMITED (HELD RR-1)
- **What the corpus holds:**
  - Verma's reading via Tiwari only: 22 lines, full of errors, faulty language, grammar and craft; opens with Śiva.
  - Verses 2, 3 and 5 as Tiwari gives them: a Paramāra king who founded the Malava kingdom → son Gandal (Gondal) → son Pata (Jnata), *arivala-mathan*, who went to Madhyadesh Malava and built temples with water reservoirs → a giver who made Udayāditya king.
  - Verma infers a junior branch (*avara-shākhā*).
  - Verma's remark that Paramāra records give V.S. alone and put any Śaka year after it.
  - INTACH: six blocks, Devanagari, 1059.
- **New finding (compatibility, not identity; R1 kept):**
  - The verses match the Udayapur pedigree described by Ganguly/Hall: Gondala → Gyātā, printed "Pātā" → Udayāditya, ruling in Sam 1116 / Śaka 981, JAOS VII p. 35. CII No. 134 dates that pedigree V.S. 1562.
  - Ganguly cites the same JAOS VII p. 35 for the "1513" fragment.
  - All three are kept apart, and the likeness is set out in the HELD note for RR-1.
- **Unsupported:** no edition, line count or text of the 22-line record in the corpus (Bühler/Cunningham staged, uncorrected). The "later or careless copy" reading is inference, hedged.
- **Candidates:**
  - JAGTA-HI-005/011 duplicate cited EN items.
  - JAGTA-HI-006 (Verma's method) adds nothing to the record.
  - JAGTA-HI-013 is the praśasti's second slab (belongs to 6.4).

### 6.3 The Amērā tank inscription — FULL
- **CII No. 30 gives everything:**
  - Find: Garde, 1923–24, Gwalior AR p. 16 and ASI AR p. 135.
  - Slab 95.5 × 78 cm; panel c. 60 × 53 cm; 24 lines, crude; letters 1.2–2 cm.
  - First eight lines small and crowded; mutilated by weather and "human frivolities"; middle of ll. 1–11 illegible; an older Gwalior-museum impression shows less abrasion.
  - Script: late 11th–early 12th-c. Nāgarī.
  - Orthography: v for b, dental for palatal, doubling after r, j for y; *rāya* for *rāja* as local influence.
  - Invocation: Pāñcajanya, the conch of Murāri, and Nārāyaṇa (Vaiṣṇava).
  - Four conventional verses on Vākpati, Sīyaka and Udayāditya; Bhoja is not present; nothing new for the genealogy. Verse 5 sets the record in Naravarman's reign.
  - Verses 5–9: Stambha of the Bhāradvāja *gotra* → Ahalla(?) → Vikrama. Cost 2,500 *ṭaṅkaka*, in words and figures.
  - Verses 10–15: the tank. Money "by just means and the strength of his arms", from which the editor infers military service.
  - Metres: śārdūlavikrīḍita, sragdharā, indravajrā/upendravajrā, upajāti, anuṣṭubh.
  - Writer: a *paṇḍita*, …pāla. Engraver: his son Saumatika. *Māluḥ* is a later addition. No place-name.
  - Date: Āṣāḍha 7, V.S. 1151. The editor takes it as expired, Friday 23 June 1094, to fit Naravarman's accession in V.S. 1151 current.
- **Correction to v2:** v2 left the date at three equivalents. The edition prefers 23 June 1094.
- **Not used:** the coin gloss (OCR-garbled "Yaikaka"). GEOHER-005 belongs to 6.21.

### 6.4 The stones and the text — FULL
- **CII p. 131 (B31, B01, B02, CII-008, CII-007).** Language, prose obeisance, metrical; 22 unnumbered verses plus the first 10 akṣaras of the 23rd. Orthography: v for b, doubled consonant after r, dental for palatal sibilant, avagraha, anusvāra for class nasals. Quality: fluent and echoing old poets (v. 2 echoes Bhavabhūti's *Mālatī-Mādhava*), but with errors (*khaḍga* as a neuter in v. 15; *ālavāla* split across quarters in v. 1; v. 2's *sura-vāsa-veśyāḥ* "not happy"). Undated, no purpose stated.
- **CII p. 135 (B20).** Metres verse by verse: nine metres. The *sragdharā* carries v. 5 (origin) and v. 21 (Udayāditya).
- **CII p. 139 (CII-009, B21).** Second slab: 27 lines, 62.5 × 53 cm, letters 1.5 cm, mostly undecipherable. Its legible opening covers the rout of the Dāhalādhīśa, then the Nēmaka line; that content is homed in Ch 4 and Ch 1, so it appears here as a recall clause only.
- **Find-spots.** Ganguly (1933): the first slab "lying in front of" the temple (GANGULY-021). Tiwari: found in the courtyard, now at Gwalior (JAGTA-EN-015). Second slab from a house near the Chatua Darwaza (JAGTA-EN-014).
- **Print history.** Cunningham's reports, Bühler EI I (JAGTA-EN-016, PANDE-002, GANGULY-035), Gwalior AR 1925–26 (JAGTA-EN-013). Tiwari's full rendering is the corpus's only verse-by-verse text in translation.
- **Candidates.** JAGTA-EN-015 used; JAGTA-HI-014 and -029 duplicate the EN items. CII-018, -026, -027, -028 and KRAM-1-001 belong to other inscriptions. BHOJA-TIMES-054 is not usable here. GANGULY-023 and BHOJA-TIMES-028 moved to 6.5.
- **Thin spots.** The first slab's dimensions are lost with the OCR'd p. 130. Nothing in the corpus says who found the first slab or when.

### 6.6 Genealogy and critics — FULL
- **Origin, vv. 5–6 (EN-B04/B05, GANGULY-002).** CII calls it "purely legendary, a later creation" claiming *agni-kula* (B32), and the first record of the house to tell it, echoed in Nagpur and Māndhātā (B04). But the Abu Paramāras' Vasantagaḍh record (1042) and the *Navasāhasāṅkacarita* (c. 1005) tell it earlier, and Padmagupta appears to be its originator (B05). So the poem is the first Malwa inscription to tell the legend, not its first teller.
- **Upendra.**
  - "Won the high honour of kingship by his valour" (EN-B06, GANGULY-003, B06).
  - Identified with the Kṛṣṇarāja of the land grants (B06).
  - The NSC also names him first (PARMAR-RAJPUT-001).
- **The line.**
  - vv. 8–11 are conventional, except the name Vajraṭa and the hint of Dhārā as capital (B07; EN-B07/B08).
  - Vākpati I appears as the sun to the eyes of Avanti's maidens (GANGULY-006, PARMAR-RAJPUT-002).
- **Completeness.** Nagpur starts at Vairisiṃha (B27); the *Tilakamañjarī* gives only Vairisiṃha; the NSC omits Vairisiṃha I and Sīyaka I (B07). This makes Udaypur the only complete list (BHOJA-TIMES-008/012, GANGULY-021).
- **Critics.**
  - Vaidya, reported by Mahesh Singh (BHOJA-TIMES-016/017), and Ganguly's reply (GANGULY-004).
  - CII on repeated names (CII-002) and Harṣa = Sīyaka (CII-022, B08); the queen's name in v. 11 (CII-017).
  - The poem is silent on Bhoja's early life (BHOJA-TIMES-030).
  - The Pānāhedā praśasti resembles Udaypur and Nagpur in how it describes kings (CII-020).
- **Candidates.** CII-020 used. JAGTA-HI-014 duplicates. PARMAR-RAJPUT-003/004 belong to 6.13; JAGTA-HI-023 belongs to 6.20. BHOJA-TIMES-013 moved to 6.7.

### 6.7 Conquests claimed — FULL
- **CII's verse-by-verse verdicts on the claims, set against corroborating records.**
  - v. 12: Harṣa/Sīyaka and Khoṭṭiga, corroborated by the Harsolā grants (B08, EN-B09).
  - vv. 14–15: Vākpati.
    - Yuvarāja of Tripurī: possible, "no evidence".
    - Karṇāṭa: "evidently inadmissible", since he lost his throne and life to Tailapa.
    - Cola and Kerala: no credit, too distant (B10, B11).
    - His end: the poem is silent (B12).
  - v. 16: Sindhurāja, given half a verse, over the Hūṇas; the NSC corroborates (B13, EN-B13).
  - v. 19: Bhoja's list (EN-B16, BHOJA-TIMES-031).
    - Cedi, Lāṭa and Karṇāṭa: matched in Kalvan (CII-005, GANGULY-012/014/016).
    - Bhīma: the Caulukya (GANGULY-017/018, BHOJA-TIMES-009).
    - The Gurjara king: possibly the Pratihāra, "no evidence".
    - Indraratha and Toggala: unknown to CII (B16), though Ganguly is confident about Indraratha (GANGULY-013).
    - Turuṣka (GANGULY-015, BHOJA-TIMES-004/034).
    - Kanauj and Abu inferences (BHOJA-TIMES-005, GANGULY-019).
  - v. 21: the enemies are unspecified (B18). Part B names the Dāhalādhīśa (B21; R2: the Kalacuri Karṇa).
  - Bhoja's end (BHOJA-TIMES-002/033).
- **Correction to v1.** v1 gave the poem's Hūṇa victory to Sīyaka. The verse (v. 16) gives it to Sindhurāja; CII p. 45 (CII-003) says Sīyaka, a discrepancy inside CII, now noted.
- **Candidates.** BHOJA-TIMES-013 and -018 and GANGULY-018 used. BHOJA-TIMES-032 numbers the conquest verse 18, against the v. 19 of CII and Tiwari: noted, not followed. RAJABHOJ-EN-001, BHOJA-TIMES-003 and -021 are redundant. JAGTA-EN-033 belongs to 6.15.

### 6.8 Makers of Paramāra praśastis — FULL for the house; LIMITED for Udaypur itself
- **Udaypur.** The poet's name is lost with the end of the slab (CII-008). Tiwari credits Udayāditya with having it prepared (JAGTA-EN-012). Mahipāla is not tied to it (PARTEMPLES-003). Nothing more is in the corpus, so the Udaypur part is necessarily short.
- **Pānāhedā praśasti (Vāgaḍa branch, V.S. 1116).**
  - The composer's name is lost in v. 60, but the engraver, Āsarāja son of Śrīdhara, a Vālabhya kāyastha, is named (B29).
  - The poet claims high-sounding words (B34).
  - It is in two parts, main line and branch (B35).
- **Nagpur praśasti.**
  - Content: 58 verses (CII-015); a prose line to Bhāratī and seven *maṅgala* verses (B25); the *gauḍī* style and figures (CII-072); rare metres (B23); "highly bombastic and artificial" (CII-071).
  - Authorship: Naravarman, by v. 55 (CII-071); the name is inferred from v. 56, where the temple is said to be adorned with many praśastis by the king (B26, B24).
  - On the stone: an extra line in double-size letters begs readers to value the work (B33); there are misspellings by the man who wrote it on the stone (B22).
  - Ganguly's verse counts (GANGULY-022).
- **Somēśvara's praśasti** (Maru-mandala branch; B30, CII-023): Narasiṃha composed it, Yaśodeva wrote it, Yaśodhara engraved it (CII-084).
- **Jayavarman's grant:** Śrīkaṇṭha and Kānhāka (CII-079).
- **Chittapa** (CII-075) is homed in 6.13, so it appears here as a recall clause. Amērā (CII-011) is homed in 6.3, also a clause.
- **Candidates.**
  - CII-016 (the Nagpur slab) and CII-023 used. CII-014 (Nagpur first edited 1843) is usable in a note.
  - JAGTA-HI-012 duplicates EN-012. JAGTA-HI-024 belongs to 6.20.
- **Correction to v1.** "Other praśastis of the house" put Somēśvara's Kirāḍu-branch praśasti in the Malwa house; it is now named as a branch record.

### 6.15 The Nāgarī script and lettering — FULL
- **CII** (T1) gives a palaeographic note for nearly every record, so the comparative frame is deep:
  - head-stroke crescents in the tenth century;
  - crude, redundant chisel strokes on a Bhoja grant;
  - the hook habit shared with the Udaypur praśasti;
  - *kh* on the Amērā stone;
  - *ch*, *dh* and *v* told apart in the twelfth century.
- **New from CII:** Udaypur's own thirteenth-century stones.
  - 1229: palatal and dental sibilants not told apart; *kh* for *ṣ* in *-mukha*; letters omitted.
  - 1232: *kh* written *ṣ*; Brāhmaṇa written *Vrāmhaṇa*.
- **New:** the Māndhātā plates of Devapāla (1225), the same king's chancery: the *ch*/*v* distinction, the *dh* bend, *v* for *b*, a doubled consonant after *r*, and numbered verses that preserve the numerals and the sign for ½.
  - The *v*-for-*b* and doubled-*r* habits also stand on the serpent slab (SERPENT-004). This links the eleventh-century and thirteenth-century hands from evidence; it is not inferred.
- **Script family:** BHOJA-TIMES-035 (Bhoja's Ujjain and Banswara plates, Yaśovarman's Kalvan plates and Arjunavarman's Arthuna record are all Devanagari) and BHOJA-TIMES-045.
- **Thin spot:** the 1080 record and the praśasti have no full palaeographic description in the corpus beyond the hook habit (CII-066).
- **Open:** Tiwari's "in the fort (*gadh*)" for the 1229 pillar stays RR-10 and is used only in a note.
