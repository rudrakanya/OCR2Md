## 6.10 Serpent charts across the kingdom — FULL
Evidence, by source:
- **CII No. 25, Ujjain.** The praśasti was probably cut by Naravarman for the Mahākāla restoration, with his father's name joined to his "in honour" (CII-052). The counted alphabet in ll. 18–22 gives 14, 2, 2, 51 consonants and 47 in the Māheśvara-sūtras (CII-053/054). The dagger form is CII-055. Three verses carry a pun on *varṇa* (CII-052).
- **CII No. 26, Dhār.** Two grey limestone pillars flank the pulpit (CII-056).
  - A: faces east, 70 × 30.5 cm, letters c. 1 cm, identical to Ujjain (KBN-CII-C-001, CII-057).
  - B: faces south, 91.55 × 45 cm, with *svasti* and Ujjain vv. 86–87 (CII-058, KBN-CII-C-002).
  - Two serpents, probably male and female, carry the ten lakāras and 16 dhātu-pratyayas, 180 endings with 90 per pada (CII-058, KBN-CII-C-003).
  - Chāndra (Sastri) or Kātantra (Lele) is disputed (KBN-CII-C-004, GANGULY-041).
  - The verses name Bhoja's two successors (BHOJA-TIMES-043).
- **CII No. 27, Un.**
  - Chaubērā Dēra, West Nimar; 138 × 90 cm; coarse, peeled stone (KBN-CII-C-005).
  - Letters 2.5 cm; undated (C-006).
  - Single serpent and dagger, 80 × 61 cm; top lost; only ē and ai survive (C-007).
  - Body and limbs (C-020).
  - Top verse *Udayāditya-dēvasya* (CII-059); four blurred lines (C-008); "much smaller", with a fish figure (C-009).
  - Adhikari's vestibule (PARTEMPLES-005); Ganguly's northern wall (GANGULY-039).
- **The two readings.**
  - Schoolroom: CII-068/069/070, GANGULY-041/042, BHOJA-TIMES-039, PARTEMPLES-006.
  - Emblem: SERPENT-008/009, now including the Ujjain praise of Śiva as language (SERPENT-009, as briefed).

Candidates:
- Used: GANGULY-039, GANGULY-042, BHOJA-TIMES-039.
- Not used: BHOJA-TIMES-041 (duplicates GANGULY-041); PARTEMPLES-004 (the temple list belongs to 6.5); BHOJA-TIMES-048 (Dhanapāla, 6.13 material, not needed).
Unsupported: none.

## 6.11 Halls of learning at Dhārā — FULL for Dhār; Udaypur has no such record (said plainly)
- **New T1 anchor: CII No. 14, the British Museum Vāgdevī** (KBN-CII-C-012…015).
  - From the ruins by the Bhōja-sālā; four lines, a *śārdūlavikrīḍita* verse plus prose; reign of Bhojadeva, "moon among the kings".
  - V.S. 1091 (1033–34); sculptor Manathala, son of mason Sahira; writer Śivadēva.
  - The *Tilakamañjarī* name Bhāratī-bhavana / Sarasvatīkaṇṭhābharaṇa; possibly installed by Bhoja.
  - *Bhojadeva* reads the father's name as "Mihira" (BHOJDEV-HI-021, noted).
- **The building.** The mosque plan (MADHYABHARAT-005). Floor inscriptions on black stone were deliberately chiselled off, leaving only Sanskrit and Prakrit traces; the pillars survived (KBN-CII-C-010/011).
- **Texts found there.** *Kūrmaśatakas*, 1903, 109 āryās each (BHOJA-TIMES-010). Other Prakrit fragments (BHOJDEV-HI-001/009). Inscriptions of four reigns (GANGULY-040). Madana's play (RAJABHOJ-EN-007, BHOJA-TIMES-015).
- **The halls.** Names (RAJABHOJ-EN-004); the Sarasvatī well (KBN-RAJABHOJ-EN-C-001); "Bhojaraja-ki-Nisal" (BHOJA-TIMES-037); assemblies (RAJABHOJ-EN-017); festival tradition (BHOJDEV-HI-006); Mandu hall and hymn (KBN-RAJABHOJ-EN-C-002); Mandu hostel (JAGTA-HI-041, Tiwari testimony).
- **Thin.** Every account of activity in the halls is T5–T9 or tradition; only the image and the edited inscriptions are T1.

Candidates:
- Used: BHOJDEV-HI-001, -006, -021; BHOJA-TIMES-037.
- Not used: BHOJA-TIMES-038 (duplicates GANGULY-042, used in 6.10); RAJABHOJ-EN-006 (superseded by C-001); BHOJA-TIMES-053 (moved to 6.12); BHOJDEV-HI-008 (moved to 6.13); BHOJDEV-HI-022 (duplicates the CII reading).

## 6.12 Bhoja as author — FULL, but all contextual (T4–T9); no Udaypur material beyond the praśasti's *kavirāja*
- **Praśasti link.** "The Udayapur prasasti glorifies Bhoja as the king of poets" (KBN-GANGULY-C-001, GANGULY-026).
- **Only dated work.** The *Rājamṛgāṅka*, Śaka 964 = 1042–43 (KBN-GANGULY-C-002).
- **Citers.** Keśavārka, Kṣīrasvāmi, Sāyaṇa (C-003); Mallinātha (BHOJDEV-HI-011).
- **Works.** *Tattvaprakāśa* and *Bṛhadrājamārtaṇḍa* (SAMAR-1-001, unquoted); commentaries on Yoga, Amara and Manu (BHOJDEV-HI-017); *Śṛṅgāramañjarīkathā* (RAJABHOJ-EN-014) with Bhailasvamipur as a quarter of Vidisha (KBN-RAJABHOJ-EN-C-003); the Ujjain curriculum (RAJABHOJ-EN-013); Kashmir to Maharashtra (BHOJA-TIMES-042); new poetry (BHOJA-TIMES-053).
- **Removed.** The copper-plate verse pair (BHOJDEV-HI-024): 6.17 is its home.

Candidates:
- Used: SAMAR-1-001, BHOJDEV-HI-011, -017; RAJABHOJ-EN-013, -014; BHOJA-TIMES-042, -053.
- Not used: BHOJDEV-HI-002, -007, -010, -014, -016, -020; RAJABHOJ-EN-012, -015; GANGULY-049; BHOJA-TIMES-050; YOGASUTRA-001; JAGTA-HI-037, -040. These are duplicates or general praise.

## 6.14 Learning in the later kingdom — FULL; Udaypur appears only as Devapāla's residence (1232)
- **Naravarman.** The Jinavallabha riddle story (KBN-GANGULY-C-012), a Jain tradition, marked "it is said".
- **Arjunavarman.** His inscriptions' praise of him (C-013). Madana's Gauḍa family (C-018).
- **Arjunavarman's grants.** Composed by Madana at the instance of Bilhaṇa, with the king's sign-manual, engraved by Bāpyadeva (KBN-CII-C-016, T1). Bilhaṇa (C-015).
- **Narapati, 1175** (C-014).
- **Āśādhara.** His pupils (C-016); the Nalakacchapura commission (C-017).
- **Devapāla's Māndhātā grant.** V.S. 1282, from Māhishmati, composed by Madana under Bilhaṇa, dated 19 August 1225 by Kielhorn (KBN-CII-C-017/018/019, T1). This links the Udaypur king of 1232 to the same literati.
- **Close.** Ganguly ranks the temples of Udayapur and Un among the best architecture of the period (C-019), attributed in the note.
- **Removed.** The Śrīkaṇṭha/Jayavarman sentence, which duplicated 6.8.

Candidates:
- Used: none as such. GANGULY-028 (1229 record) belongs to 6.18.
- Not used: GANGULY-067 (generic praise); BHOJA-TIMES-051 (used via Ganguly in 6.13); BHOJA-TIMES-058 (duplicate).
