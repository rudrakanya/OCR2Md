## 6.15 The Nāgarī script and lettering — FULL
- **CII** (T1) gives a palaeographic note for nearly every record, so the comparative frame is deep:
  - head-stroke crescents in the tenth century;
  - crude, redundant chisel strokes on a Bhoja grant;
  - the hook habit shared with the Udaypur praśasti;
  - *kh* on the Amērā stone;
  - *ch*, *dh* and *v* told apart in the twelfth century.
- **New from CII:** Udaypur's own thirteenth-century stones.
  - 1229: palatal and dental sibilants not told apart; *kh* for *ṣ* in *-mukha*; letters omitted.
  - 1232: *kh* written *ṣ*; Brāhmaṇa written *Vrāmhaṇa*.
- **New:** the Māndhātā plates of Devapāla (1225), the same king's chancery: the *ch*/*v* distinction, the *dh* bend, *v* for *b*, a doubled consonant after *r*, and numbered verses that preserve the numerals and the sign for ½.
  - The *v*-for-*b* and doubled-*r* habits also stand on the serpent slab (SERPENT-004). This links the eleventh-century and thirteenth-century hands from evidence; it is not inferred.
- **Script family:** BHOJA-TIMES-035 (Bhoja's Ujjain and Banswara plates, Yaśovarman's Kalvan plates and Arjunavarman's Arthuna record are all Devanagari) and BHOJA-TIMES-045.
- **Thin spot:** the 1080 record and the praśasti have no full palaeographic description in the corpus beyond the hook habit (CII-066).
- **Open:** Tiwari's "in the fort (*gadh*)" for the 1229 pillar stays RR-10 and is used only in a note.

## 6.16 The inscriptions of the temple porches — LIMITED
- **What exists:** survey descriptions (Gazetteer p. 347, Patil p. 116: numerous Sanskrit inscriptions and pilgrims' records on the pillars and seats of all three porches), R. K. Sharma (the eastern porch shows visits and continuing worship), and Saarthak Singh (the archive from V.S. 1137 to V.S. 1985 in Sanskrit, Persian and Hindi, listed in the Gwalior State reports and ARIEp, only a selection published).
- **What is missing:** the corpus holds no catalogue of the porch records. Beyond the records of 6.18–6.19 and the 1080 record, no individual porch inscription is edited.
- **Tiwari's counts:** "more than twenty" on the doorways, "around a hundred" in the town, a "dozen", and "11" in Verma's series. These are testimony; canon says Tiwari never supplies a count, so they go in notes only.
- **Correction:** v1 put Tiwari's eight-line frame "beside one door" in the porch paragraph. Tiwari EN p. 11 places it at the Bija Mandal building, not the temple, so the text now says so.
- **Unit role:** the unit's depth is in describing how the archive is known. It cannot reach 6.9's density without a catalogue (ARIEp/Gwalior lists, not in the corpus).

## 6.17 Reading a Paramāra grant — FULL
- Bhoja's verse pair (BHOJDEV-HI-023/-024); the Vachchala grant (CII-080); the Nālataḍāga announcement (GANGULY-069); the 1260 grant by the *pratīhāra* Gāṅgadeva (CII-019, -083) and its near-faultless verse (CII-077).
- **New:** the Māndhātā copper plates of Devapāla (1225), a complete worked example from the king of the Udaypur porch records:
  - three plates on two rings, weighing 12.10 kg;
  - the Garuḍa emblem between two snakes;
  - 80 lines;
  - an opening obeisance to *dharma* and an *anuṣṭubh* draft repeated from Arjunavarman's grants;
  - a genealogy from Bhoja to Arjunavarman;
  - 32 donees, each with *gotra*, *pravara*, father, grandfather, epithet, place of origin and Vedic school (epithets change from one generation to the next);
  - conditions and imprecatory verses;
  - composed by Madana under the minister of peace and war Bilhaṇa;
  - the king's sign-manual, and *maṅgalaṃ mahāśrīḥ*. The 1229 Udaypur pillar ends the same way.
- **Kept to recall:** the *pañcaśabda* honour (JAGTA-HI-044) and the *pañcakula* (C08-JAGTA-EN-012). The economics of the land stays in Ch 8.
- **Not used:** GANGULY-048 (Sarasvatī image, 6.11/6.12 matter), PARMAR-RAJPUT-005 (T9, duplicates Ganguly), BHOJDEV-HI-004 (anecdote) and BHOJA-TIMES-044 (Betma script; 6.15 has better).

## 6.18 The porch records under Caulukya rule and under Devapāla, 1163–1232 — FULL
- **1163:** the fragmentary Kumārapāla record (V.S. 1220). Rājyapāla is *mahāsādhanika* in charge of the eastern division, with his headquarters at Udayapur. New (GANGULY-D01): one Vasantapāla made donations for the maintenance of the temple of Udaleśvara at Udayapur. Kumārapāla is styled vanquisher of the lords of Śākambharī and Avanti (PARTEMPLES).
- **1172–73:** Ajayapāla's record. New (GANGULY-D02): Kumārapāla's death and Ajayapāla's succession. Lūṇapasāka's grant of Umaratha in the Bhṛṅgārikā-Catuṣṣaṣṭi *pathaka* to Vaidyanātha, for his father's merit, in the Bhāillasvāmi-mahādvādaśaka; the three year-readings 1171/1172/1173.
- **1229 pillar:** right of the east door, 14 lines.
  - *Siddham*; V.S. 1286 Kārttika bright half, a Friday; 26 Oct or 2 Nov 1229 (Caitrādi), or 11 or 18 Oct 1230 (Kārttikādi; CII-036). The tithi is lost, with space for two digits, perhaps the full moon (CII-D07).
  - "In the presence of" Udalmeśvara, evidently Udayeśvara.
  - Devapāla "who has attained the *pañcaśabda*" in Verma's reading (JAGTA-EN-D01/D02), cf. Harsūd's *pañca-mahā-śabda* (CII-D16). Only *mahārāja* in l. 5 (CII-082).
  - The *mahāmudrā*; the *pañcakula*; Dhāmadeva of the treasury.
  - Plots in a village whose name has lost a letter, from his own share, with water poured (*udakapūrvakam*). Four *viśva*, the twentieth part of a *bighā* (CII-D09; Verma's reading D03/D04).
  - *Pañcamukha-pattana* possibly Udaypur itself (CII-D08). No donee, so the gift was to the god. *Maṅgalaṃ mahāśrīḥ*.
- **1232 record:** Thursday, third of the dark half of Mārgaśīrṣa, V.S. 128[9]; the unit figure first cut as 3 or 4 and later altered to 9(?) (CII-D12).
  - Issued while Devapāla was residing at Udayapur; full imperial titles, the only record to claim them.
  - Plots in Devadharmapurī (probably Udaypur), Bhṛṅgārikā-64 (the chief village of sixty-four), Umarata (the modern Umarathā, the name also of Lūṇapasāka's village), and Kharahi (Khiriyā or Khadākhedī, about 6 km north-west).
  - *Pañcamukha-pattana* named after the Śiva temple, so Udaypur bears two names in one record (CII-042, -043). *Brāhmaṇa* is spelt *Vrāmhaṇa* (CII-D11).
  - The latest epigraph of Devapāla's reign; Iltutmish's raid on Bhilsā in 1233–34 goes in a note only (CII-D13; Ch 10).
- **Flagged:** Tiwari says the 1229 record calls Devapāla *Mahākumāra* and infers that he served at Udaypur before becoming king (JAGTA-EN-D05). The edition reads only *Mahārāja* in l. 5 (CII-082). The inference is not followed; it is noted.

## 6.19 The last Paramāra records — LIMITED (HELD RR-11, RR-23)
- **V.S. 1311 (1255):** known only from Kielhorn's notice (IA XX p. 84 n. 3; INI 550); the stone is lost and there is no impression.
  - Ganguly: "nothing can be inferred". CII (T1) nonetheless counts it as the earliest date of Jayasiṃha, "no other than Jayavarman, a son of Dēvapāla" (CII-D14, D15).
  - CII outranks Ganguly, so the text gives CII's use, with Ganguly's caution in the note.
- **[Uda?]pura:** the Harsūd stone of 1218 records a temple built by Keśava, of a merchant family resident at Undapura (CII-D16). Kielhorn thought Undapura appears also in an unpublished Udayapur inscription he dated, reading it [Uda?]pura (CII-034, -035).
- **V.S. 1366 (1309):** the inscription inside the east entrance (Ganguly). His text prints "1909 A.D."; his table gives 1309, "Jayasinha II", while his text says "Jayasiṃha III". Kielhorn's Northern List, EI V App. No. 232.
  - CII VII.2 has no entry for it in the corpus, and it is a single-reader record.
- **Limit:** it cannot reach full depth without the editions (RR-11/RR-23).
