# Ch 6 v3: evidence inventory, worker E (6.20–6.22)

## 6.20 Persian on the temple's stones

Verdict: **LIMITED but deepened.** No edition or text of any Persian inscription is in the corpus. Every source reports where an inscription is, its date and its subject; none gives its wording.

What each source carries:
- **Patil** (C10-MADHYABHARAT-006): two Persian inscriptions "related to this mosque", A.H. 737–39 (1336–38). The mosque was built by Muhammad Tughluq, Sultan of Delhi, from the material of demolished shrines. Patil also records the Aurangzeb legend (Ch 10 tests it).
- **Pande** (C07-PANDE-006): quotes the Gazetteer that the Persian inscriptions in the mosque record its construction by Tughluq.
- **Pande, quoting the survey reports**:
  - Cunningham (KBN-PANDE-E-003): the NW corner temple and the western *bedi* were knocked down and a masjid erected, "as recorded in the two inscriptions over the two small doorways to the right and left of the temple", dated 737 and 739 Hijra respectively. **New detail.**
  - Beglar (KBN-PANDE-E-001): believed "Aurangzeb's name … occurs in them".
  - Pande (KBN-PANDE-E-002): Beglar erred; the inscriptions record Tughluq's name, and Cunningham corrected the mistake. **New reading history.**
- **Tiwari** (C06-JAGTA-EN-020/-024): two free-standing doorways built of the temple's broken pieces, north and south of the rear (west), on a failed wall line; "Arabic or Persian"; the name read clearly on a recent reading. Testimony: no date is taken from Tiwari.
- **Later Persian records** (Patil C07-MADHYABHARAT-011/-012, KBN-MADHYABHARAT-E-001; Gazetteer C07-GAZ-005):
  - the Sher Khan mosque outside Moti Darwaza, the eastern gate of the town wall: Persian and Sanskrit, an agent of Sher Khan under Ghiyās Shāh Khaljī of Mandu, A.H. 894 (1488);
  - the Shahi Masjid, a furlong east of the temple: begun under Jahāngīr, finished under Shāh Jahān, A.H. 1041 (1632).
- **Tiwari** (C06-JAGTA-EN-031): some inscriptions carry Sanskrit and Persian together. His count of about 100 is not used.

Candidates rejected:
- JAGTA-HI-019 and -027 duplicate the EN items.
- JAGTA-EN-023 is rhetoric.
- JAGTA-HI-020 is the personified voice of an image, not evidence.
- The Persian *farman* in Tiwari is a land-claim document, not an inscription (Ch 11/12, legal review).

Thin spots and flags:
- **INTACH** appendix of identified inscriptions (pp. 22–26): further dated records, including one of 1522 at Badi Mata Mandir that mentions the Quran, the Shahi Masjid record, and a Devanagari record of 1730 at a mosque. The OCR is garbled ("OCR unreliable"), so none of it is used. The pages need re-OCR.
- **Tradition of 1325** (Pande p. 92): a Tughluq order to raze the temple. It belongs to Ch 10.

## 6.21 Inscriptions of the hill

Verdict: **FULL for the two pilgrim records; LIMITED for the older ones** (tentative or no readings).

What each source carries:
- **Geo-Heritage survey**:
  - KBN-GEOHER-E-001: texts at four places at the middle level of the mesa and the butte.
  - Inscription I (C06-GEOHER-010, KBN-GEOHER-E-002): two lines, *siddham*, *sūtradhāra* Shurapa, Ghutaddha; early Nāgarī of the 8th century, Sanskrit; the rest unclear. A Devanagari tentative reading exists but is not quoted, because the OCR is unsure.
  - Inscription II (C06-GEOHER-007): Turasadasa of Ranathabhore, son of Tosha, grandson of Sridasa; engraved by the mason (*silavata*) Suvi; Samvat 1696 Chaitra vadi 11; Nāgarī, local dialect.
  - Ustonatha (C06-GEOHER-008, KBN-GEOHER-E-004, KBN-GEOHER-E-003): written by himself, Wednesday; Roman reading; the survey converts the date to 27 April 1639.
  - Inscription IV (C06-GEOHER-009): Jain cave, two lines of 10th–11th-century Sanskrit, chipped (second line and start of the first), not fully interpreted.
  - The monolith inscription is undeciphered (C06-GEOHER-004).
  - "Several inscriptions … in Devanagari" (C06-GEOHER-002).
  - Pilgrims came from Rajasthan (C06-GEOHER-003), from hundreds of km (C06-GEOHER-008).
  - KBN-GEOHER-E-005: today, names are written over the site.
- **INTACH** (C06-INTACH-002, KBN-INTACH-E-001): the Jain cave on the hill south of the village; a two-line inscription beside a sculpture on the wall, noticed for the first time, undeciphered. INTACH dates the cave itself to the 6th century.

Flags:
- The survey's "almost 500 years ago" (C06-GEOHER-006) conflicts with its own 1639. The note says so.
- INTACH's cave inscription and the survey's Inscription IV may be the same; no item says so, so they are kept apart.
- "Ranathabhore" is not identified with any modern place.
