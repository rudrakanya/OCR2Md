## Introduction

The Parmars of Malwa ruled central India for a long time. King Bhoj of this dynasty was a great ruler and a great patron of art and literature. He wrote a book on the Shilpasastra. Udayadity was one of the kings of the Parmar dynasty who re-established the Parmar kingdom after the decline of it due to the death of King Bhoj. King Udayaditya was the relative of the Bhoja, a great Parmar king who established Bhojpur and commissioned various architectural marvels. King Udayaditya established the city of Udaypur. Its foundation was laid in the mid 11$^{th}$ century CE. This village Udaypur is unique in many ways as a large number of historic structures are present in a relatively good state. This city was secured by gateways, which are still standing around the centre of the city settlement. The central point being the famous Neelkantheshawar or Udayeshawara, a tall standing temple of Shiva. The Udaypur inscriptions (now in Gwalior museum) talks about the construction of the city, temple and a water body by king Udayaditya. The city flourished well during the reign of Udayaditya, which is seen by the large number of monuments and structures built during that period. A hill in South of the village is partially covered with a fortification wall. The wall and the other structure over the hill indicate the Parmar ruler conquered the existence of a Gond kingdom before this region. Later the region was occupied by Islamic rulers which is evident from the inscriptions.

This book is an effort to demonstrate the glorious past of this region through the existing monuments at Udaypur. The book has covered nearby villages also which have significant antiquities of the reign of Parmars around 10$^{th}$-11$^{th}$ century CE. Comprehensive fieldwork has been done identify and document the tangible heritages. Wherever an inscription is found it has been deciphered to establish the authenticity of the work. Apart from going through the published and reported matter different experts and scholars of the field were also consulted. The book is a brief about the vast cultural

# Grading the Identified Heritage Structures

Many times, a heritage structure is small in its form and size but holds very strong and significant associated cultural values while the grand and magnificent monuments are loud and express the higher importance in the society. The size, shape, form & design, embellishments & carvings are the aspects through which one can understand and assess the rooted socio-cultural values which are significant to the identified heritage structures. Moreover, over a period of time historical structures have undergone through changing scenarios and have survived in the same. The degree of alteration affects the integrity of the historic structures. The insensitive layers of modifications damage the value those structures hold.

The grading has been done in below mentioned categories:

# Varaha

The Varaha is the third incarnation of Vishnu which was believed to be incarnated to save the mother earth from the clutches of a demon king Hiranyaksha. In Varaha incarnation, Lord Vishnu took a form of a huge boar and dive deep into the water to save the earth from sinking.

The sculptures of Varaha are popular in the central region of India where Varaha is represented in two different forms with a female figure which represents mother earth.

The two forms of Varaha are first, anthropomorphically where the deity is shown with a human body and boar head and second, zoomorphically where the full body of deity is shown in the form of a wild boar.
