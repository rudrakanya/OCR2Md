# Gaps and conflicts for the editor

The outline is the author's intent; the KB is the evidence. Where they diverge it is recorded here rather than written around.

### 1. "1070 CE accession" is one of four reign spans in the KB

| span | source | chunk |
|---|---|---|
| 1070–1093 | INTACH 2022 | `intach-2022-udaypur-00075` |
| c. 1059–93 | INTACH 2022, same volume | `intach-2022-udaypur-00127` |
| 1058–1087 | Patil 1952 | `patil-1952-00095` |
| 1059–1086 | Ganguly | `ganguly-paramara-00568` |

The claims register (K2) records why: a Udaypur inscription of V.S. 1116 shows Udayāditya ruling in 1059, while the Panhera inscription of the same year is issued under Jayasiṃha as sovereign. **Both readings have primary support**, and INTACH contradicts itself between two pages of one volume. Fixing 1070 as a bare anchor would assert as settled what the KB shows to be contested.

**Recommended wording:** "ruled Mālava c. 1070–93; already active at Udaypur by 1059" — true on every reading. *Editor's decision required before the Stance section is drafted.*

### 2. "Qanungo Baoli, 1645 CE trilingual slab" — NOT IN THE KB

- "Qanungo" appears twice, both as a revenue-officer title in the Vidisha gazetteer, never as a baoli.
- "1645" appears **zero** times in the corpus.
- "Trilingual" appears once, about a modern three-language publication.

The KB does hold stepwell inscriptions: one on the retaining wall of the Ghud Dod Baoli, one in the Setho ki Baniya Baoli, and a slab recovered from a house in the Chatua Darwaza locality, now in the Gwalior museum (Tiwari). **This point cannot be drafted as given.** Options: (a) cut it; (b) substitute a stepwell inscription the KB holds; (c) supply the source and it will be ingested. Not invented, not silently dropped.

### 3. "Khaljī conquest & epigraphic survival (1310 CE)" — supported, but not as framed

The KB's 1310 is about Paramāra **survival**, not Khaljī conquest: an inscription "indicates that the Paramara dynasty survived until 1310 CE, at least in the northeastern part of Malwa", with the Delhi Sultanate holding the area **by 1338**. The chunk is `inference`, so the sentence must attribute. No chunk names a Khaljī conquest of Udaypur.

### 4. "Tughluq mosque records 1336–1339" — the KB says 1336–38

Two independent works give A.H. 737–739 = **A.D. 1336–38**. No source gives 1339. Minor, but it is a date the book will be quoted on.

### 5. "Shāhī Mahal and Masjid, 1616–1632" — half supported

**1632 is solid.** **1616 is not in the KB at all.** The mosque was begun under Jahāngīr (r. 1605–27) by Qazi Aulia and finished at Shāh Jahān's accession. Small internal conflict: INTACH gives A.H. 1041, Patil A.H. 1042, both converted to 1632. **Recommended:** "begun under Jahāngīr and completed in 1632, at Shāh Jahān's accession".

### 6. The Maratha regime rests on one object

The only Scindia-era anchor in the KB is the liṅga's brass face plate of 1775, given by Khande Rao Appaji, a general of Mahadji Scindia. No chunk describes eighteenth-century Scindia *repairs*. The regime is representable, but by a donation rather than a building campaign.

### 7. 1.3's "not an ancient site" meets a counter-claim inside the KB

INTACH states that "Udaypur's history reaches back to at least the ninth century" (`intach-2022-udaypur-00127`, factual). This does not contradict the scriptural negative — a ninth-century antecedent is not a Purāṇic naming — but the chapter cannot claim the site was empty before 1059 without engaging it. **Recommended:** make the claim precisely, about *naming and foundation*, not about occupation.

### 8. Corroboration counts read low

99% of C1's bucket shows no detected corroboration. That is the KB's semantic detector being switched off (see `RETRIEVAL_VALIDATION.md`), not evidence of weakness. Single-source claims are attributed accordingly.

### 9. Quotability

87 of the 1,998 bucketed chunks (4%) are AI-reworded or failed an OCR gate. **None of the chunks selected for this chapter are among them** — every source above is `is_quotable: true`, so Tiwari may be quoted directly in 1.1.

### 10. Section budgets vs. evidence density

1.1 and 1.4 are comfortably supported. 1.2 is supported but its five regimes are unevenly evidenced (Paramāra and Mughal rich; Maratha thin; "Khaljī" not as framed). 1.3's scriptural half is thin **by design** — the finding is an absence — so the section's 900 words must be carried by the method and the argument, not by scriptural quotation.
