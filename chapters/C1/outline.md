# Outline — C1 Udaypur: A Small Town with a Long Memory

*The author's outline, annotated against the KB. Chunk ids are what the drafter may cite; ⚠ marks a point where the evidence and the outline diverge. Full detail in `research.md` → "Gaps and conflicts for the editor".*

**Framing question:** a village of about 6,000 people carries a historical record out of all proportion to its size, and that gap is what the book sets out to explain. **Supported:** the 2011 census gives 6,383 people in 1,247 houses across 1,966 hectares [`intach-2022-udaypur-00162`, `intach-geoheritage-00133`].

---

## 1.1 Arrival (~900 w) — Tiwari + INTACH

**Intent:** reach the monument *through* the village; present-day texture before any history.

The spine of this section is Tiwari's approach: the desolate three-way junction, the lane six or seven feet wide running three hundred metres uphill, the decades-old stone shops selling cloth, groceries and sweets, built from Udaypur's own stone [`tiwari-jhk-en-00023`]; the huge old stone doorway at the lane's end, with the temple behind it and its main east door long shut [`tiwari-jhk-en-00024`]; his own judgement, a diamond on a heap of garbage, and the indifference of everyone from shopkeepers to the panchayat [`tiwari-jhk-en-00025`]; the lanes beside the temple leading into a settlement of Paramāra-era houses built from massive carved stone, narrowed over centuries by encroachment [`tiwari-jhk-en-00029`]; a first-time visitor's reaction to the temple seen from a distance [`tiwari-jhk-en-00046`]. Census figures anchor the scale [`intach-2022-udaypur-00162`, `intach-geoheritage-00133`].

- `tiwari-jhk-en-00024` is **observation** → attribute to Tiwari, name him as the walker.
- Tiwari writes "Udaipur"; quotations keep his spelling, the book's voice uses Udaypur.
- All five Tiwari chunks are quotable, so direct quotation is available here.
- **Note for the editor:** the outline says "how a visitor actually sees Udaypur in 2025". Tiwari's walk dates to about 2021; the KB has nothing later. The draft will not date the scene to 2025.

## 1.2 The inventory (~900 w) — five regimes within one square kilometre

**Intent:** Udaypur as a micro-archive where five political eras stand side by side.

| regime | what the draft can say | evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Paramāra | Temple founded by Udayāditya, begun 1059 (V.S. 1116), flagstaff 1080 (V.S. 1137); the Udaysagar tank of 1059; an older tank, Bhujariya, already there | `patil-composite-inscriptions-00084`, `intach-geoheritage-00092` |
| ⚠ Khaljī / Tughluq | An inscription **indicates the Paramāras survived to 1310** in north-eastern Mālava; the Delhi Sultanate held the area **by 1338** | `intach-2022-udaypur-00126` (**inference** → attribute) |
| Tughluq | Muḥammad Tughluq damaged the attendant shrines **1336–38** (A.H. 737–39) and built a mosque behind the temple from their stone; Persian inscriptions record it | `pande-udayesvara-00391`, `patil-1952-00387` |
| ⚠ Mughal | The Shāhī Masjid begun under Jahāngīr by Qazi Aulia, left half-built when both died, completed **1632** at Shāh Jahān's accession; the Mahal beside it; Sher Khan's Mosque of 1488 | `intach-2022-udaypur-00110`, `intach-2022-udaypur-00293`, `patil-1952-00388`, `patil-composite-inscriptions-00085` |
| ⚠ Maratha | A brass face plate given to the liṅga in **1775** by Khande Rao Appaji, general of Mahadji Scindia | `patil-composite-inscriptions-00084` |
| Modern | ASI protection of the temple, alongside the hill's status as reserved forest in revenue records and the Forest Department's working plan | `intach-geoheritage-00089`, `intach-geoheritage-00090`, `intach-2022-udaypur-00017` |

⚠ **Three divergences, all recorded in `research.md`:**
1. **1310 is Paramāra survival, not Khaljī conquest.** No chunk names a Khaljī conquest of Udaypur.
2. **"1336–1339" → the KB says 1336–38.** Two independent works; no source gives 1339.
3. **"1616–1632" → 1616 is not in the KB.** 1632 is solid.

⚠ **"Qanungo Baoli, 1645 trilingual slab" is not in the KB at all** and cannot be drafted. 1645 appears zero times in the corpus. The Maratha regime will rest on the 1775 plate unless a source is supplied.

## 1.3 What "long memory" means (~900 w) — the honest negative

**Intent:** the Purāṇic register is regional, not local; Udaypur was a deliberate eleventh-century foundation, not an ancient Purāṇic site.

**This is the chapter's strongest evidential move and it is fully supported — as an absence.** All 713 Skanda-Purāṇa chunks were searched for *Udayapura*, *Udaypur*, *Udayeśvara* and उदयपुर: **zero matches**. What the text does name is the region's sacred water — Daśārṇa among holy rivers [`skanda-xiii-00207`], Vetravatī in confluence with Gaṅgā [`skanda-xiii-00301`]. The draft states the method as well as the finding.

⚠ **One counter-claim inside the KB:** INTACH says "Udaypur's history reaches back to at least the ninth century" [`intach-2022-udaypur-00127`]. A ninth-century antecedent is not a Purāṇic naming, so the two are compatible — but the chapter must claim precisely: **about naming and foundation, not about occupation.** Do not write that the site was empty.

- Skanda-Purāṇa chunks are `primary_scriptural`, tagged `scriptural_register`: evidence of what the text says, never of what stood here.

## 1.4 The name (~600 w) — the royal naming act

**Intent:** town (**Udayapura**), temple (**Udayeśvara**), lake (**Udayasamudra**) named together as one act of place-making.

The three limbs are separately evidenced: the city named after the king [`intach-2022-udaypur-00127`]; the temple founded by him [`patil-composite-inscriptions-00084`]; the tank built by him in 1059 [`intach-geoheritage-00092`]. Tiwari puts the naming plainly, but his chunk is **inference** → attribute [`tiwari-jhk-en-00078`].

⚠ **What the KB does not supply:** a single source stating that the three were named *together as one act*. Each limb is attested; the unification is the book's argument. The draft will make that visible — presenting the pattern and naming it as the author's reading, not as a sourced claim.

## Stance / methodological promise (~800 w)

**Intent:** state how the book treats evidence — architecture and epigraphy kept apart from folklore, inscriptions read as royal panegyric (*praśasti*) rather than neutral record — and fix the anchor dates.

| anchor | status in the KB |
|---|---|
| **1059 foundation** | Solid: three independent works and the temple's own inscription [`patil-composite-inscriptions-00084`, `ganguly-paramara-00562`, `intach-geoheritage-00092`] |
| **1080 flagstaff** | Solid: V.S. 1137 [`patil-composite-inscriptions-00084`] |
| ⚠ **1070 accession** | **Contested — four different spans in the KB**, including two in one INTACH volume: 1070–93 [`intach-2022-udaypur-00075`], c. 1059–93 [`intach-2022-udaypur-00127`], 1058–87 [`patil-1952-00095`], 1059–86 [`ganguly-paramara-00568`] |

⚠ **Decision required before this section is drafted.** The outline asks for 1070 as a fixed anchor the rest of the manuscript depends on; the KB shows it contested, with primary support on both sides (claims register K2). Recommended: **"ruled Mālava c. 1070–93; already active at Udaypur by 1059"** — true on every reading, and it lets the rest of the manuscript rest on it safely. The alternative is to assert 1070 flat and footnote the dispute; the chapter's own methodological promise argues against that.

The section's demonstration of method can use the material this chapter has already surfaced: the Aurangzeb attribution that Patil names and rejects in favour of the Tughluq inscriptions [`patil-1952-00387`], and a praśasti read as a claim rather than a report.
