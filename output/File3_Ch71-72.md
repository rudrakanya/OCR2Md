[FLAG: The text has parenthetical corrections indicated — अथ(प्रपञ्जयते?) and रा(चि)त्रं — reproduced exactly as printed.]

**English Translation:**

Now the favourite one is being illustrated by us the systematisation of art of painting (Citrakarma) painting (Citraṃ) verily is the foremost (mukhaṃ) of all the arts or crafts a favourite one of the Universe. ²

> **Footnote 2:** Dr. (Mrs.) Rajani Patki in her article entitled– Bhoja's painting technology in Samarāṅgana Sūtradhāra, (Dr. K.V. Sharma Felicitation volume, p. 345-350 published by Śrī Śāradā Education Society Research Centre, Chennai) says painting is an art which has flourished in India for centuries. The Ajanta caves are world famous for wall paintings. Similar paintings are to be seen in the caves of Bagha situated on the banks of Dhārā river, which is famous because of king Bhoja who was an all rounder, an able king, a soldier, a scholar, and one who was loved by the people of his country.

# Chapter 72 — Bhūmibandha — Seasoning of the wall of painting

### Verse 72.1

**Sanskrit (Devanagari):**

इदानीं वर्तिकालक्ष्म भूमिबध्ध कथ्यते।
गुल्मान्तरे शुभे क्षेत्रे पच्मिन्यां सरितस्तटे॥१॥

**IAST Transliteration:**

idānīṃ vartikālakṣma bhūmibaddha kathyate ।
gulmāntare śubhe kṣetre pacminyāṃ saritastaṭe ॥1॥

**English Translation:**

Now the definition of a Varttikā³ (brush- painting brush) and the Bhūmibandha⁴ is being illustrated. From the agulma (a thicket or a cluster of trees) in an auspicious field, or a lotus lake or on the bank of a river.

> **Footnote 2:** Actual Bhūmibandha i.e., seasoning of the wall for painting is to be commenced under a sacred constellation on an auspicious day with the offering of fragrant flowers of different colours, dhūpa etc. for the tutelary deity. Nine parts of the cotton fibre and the clay are then mixed with an equal quantity of water. The paste then should be spread on the wall and pressed evenly with a hair brush, water being sprinkled on it as needed. The plaster would then be crystal clear white and small.

> **Footnote 3:** V.S. Apte, p. 494 - a paint brush.

> **Footnote 4:** Clay is to be collected for plaster from the banks of a river, the roots of litus creepers, streams flowing from the hills and the roots of the trees. The clay so collected in the form of lumps red or glossy white, does not develop cracks after it is used for plastering. The collected clay is mixed with rice grains with husk (śali) the preparation being a seventh part of the clay in summer, a fifth part of it in winter, a sixth part in Autumn, and a fourth part during the rainy season.

**English Translation:**

Having cleansed the winnowed Śālitaṇḍulas i.e. the rice grains one may bake them in a pot having ground them into a pulp.
