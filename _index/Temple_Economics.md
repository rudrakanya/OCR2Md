# Index: Temple_Economics.md

**Title/Author:** *Temple Economics (Arthvyavastha of Mandir)* by Sandeep Singh.

**Overall (2-3 sentences):** A contemporary, ideologically-framed book arguing that Hindu temples (mandirs) function as the nucleus of a self-sustaining economic system ("Mandir based Arthavyavastha") spanning rituals, prasad, crafts, dance/music, pilgrimage, fairs, and monastic institutions. It chronicles how this economy allegedly thrived historically, was disrupted during Islamic, Christian/colonial, and modern "secular" periods, and makes a case for its revival. The work is polemical and present-day in orientation, using regional case studies (Tirumala, Meenakshi, Kumbh Mela, Jagannath, etc.) rather than scholarly architectural or art history.

**Relevance to a monograph on the Udayeśvara temple of Udaypur:** Essentially none — this source can be excluded.

- No mention of the Udayeśvara temple, Udaypur, the Paramāra dynasty, or King Bhoja in any relevant sense.
- The six keyword hits for "Malwa" are unrelated: they reference the 14th-16th c. "Islamist Malwa" sultanate (line ~4187) and a 1680 Mughal demolition record citing "Someshwar mandir in West Malwa" (line ~4443) — not Paramāra Malwa or 11th-century temple-building.
- The "Antiquity and History of Mandir based Arthavyavastha" chapter (ch. 4, line ~1246) and a list of "Mandirs older than 1000 years" (line ~1466) are the closest thematic touchpoints, but they treat temples as economic engines, not as architectural/historical subjects, and do not cover the Udayeśvara or Paramāra context.
- The Madhya Pradesh craft section (line ~2484) names Bhedaghat, Khajuraho, Tikamgarh, and Budni — none near Udaypur or relevant to the monograph.

In short, the book concerns the modern/contemporary economics and management of temples, not medieval temple architecture or the economics of medieval temple-building, and offers nothing usable for an Udayeśvara monograph.
