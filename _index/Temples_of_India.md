# Index: Temples_of_India.md

**Title/Author:** *Temples of North India* (series: India—The Land and People) by Krishna Deva, National Book Trust, India, 1969.

**Overall (2-4 sentences):** A concise, authoritative survey of North Indian (Nagara) temple architecture from the Maurya/Gupta origins through the medieval regional schools, written by a leading scholar of the subject for an educated general reader. It is organized chronologically and regionally (Gupta, Early Chalukyan, Pratihara, Rajasthan, Gujarat, Central India, Deccani, Orissa, Hoysala), tracing the evolution of plan, elevation and the curvilinear shikhara, and naming representative monuments in each style. Architectural terms are given in popularized form alongside Sanskrit shilpa-text vocabulary.

**Relevance to a monograph on the Udayeśvara temple of Udaypur:** Supporting (locally strong). Most of the book is broad survey material, but the Deccani/Bhumija chapter contains a direct, substantive discussion of the Udayeśvara temple itself, its dating, patronage (Paramāra Udayāditya), and a comparative listing of nearly every other Bhūmija temple in Malwa, Rajasthan, Gujarat, the Deccan and Chhattisgarh — exactly the comparative architectural context a monograph needs. Krishna Deva credits his own research with identifying Malwa as the origin of the Bhūmija mode, so this is a primary-voice source on that claim.

## Useful sections

- Chapter VIII "Deccani Style" / Bhūmija discussion (approx. lines 918-972, pp. 65-67). Core section. Defines the Bhūmija shikhara (four spines with chaitya-dormer mesh on central rathas; quadrants filled with rows of miniature shrine-models; prominent sukanasa). States the style was favored by the Paramāras and (per the author's research) originated in 10th-c. Malwa.
- Udayeśvara temple proper (approx. lines 930-934). Direct description: Nilakaṇṭheśvara/Udayeśvara at Udaipur (Dist. Vidisha), built by Udayāditya, started 1059, completed 1080; stellate plan, sapta-ratha sanctum, vestibule, hall with three porches, 7 subsidiary shrines, platform-terrace, shikhara with 7 vertical and 5 horizontal rows of miniature shikharas per quadrant; called "the grandest specimen of Paramara architecture."
- Comparative Bhūmija temples (approx. lines 936-972). One-line characterizations of: Un group (Chaubara Dera, dated by Udayāditya inscription); Onkar Mandhata / Amareshwara (later one dated 1063, plan comparable to Udayeśvara); Jamli (only non-stellate Malwa Bhumija, ~decade later); Nemawar Siddheshwara (degenerate, 12th c.); Sewari, Menal Mahanaleshwara (contemporary with Udayeśvara), Ramgarh Kota, Bijolia, Jhalrapatan, Ranakpur, Adbhutnath, Limkheda, Sarnel Galateshwara, Arang Bhand Dewal. Useful for situating Udayeśvara within the Bhumija family.
- Udaipur (Rajasthan)-region temples, Chapter V (approx. lines 588-614, pp. 53-57). Unwas Durga (959), Ekalingaji Lakulisha (972), Nagda Sas-bahu, Ahar Mira/Mahavira temples — note these are the Mewar Udaipur, distinct from the Paramāra Udaypur; useful for disambiguation and regional contrast.
- General "Characteristics of the North Indian Temple" (approx. lines 246-262, pp. 2-5). Defines shikhara, ratha/offset terminology, amalasaraka, kalasha, mandapa, ambulatory — baseline vocabulary.
- Chapter VII Central India / Khajuraho (approx. lines 802-906, pp. 49-64). Chandella temples and the Kandariya Mahadeva culmination; relevant as the contrasting Khajuraho-type Nagara shikhara against which Bhumija is distinguished.

## Key terms

shikhara, Bhūmija, Nagara/North Indian style, ratha (tri-ratha, pancha-ratha, sapta-ratha), stellate (star) plan, sukanasa antefix, chaitya-dormer, bhumi-amalaka, amalasaraka, kalasha, jangha (wall), mandapa, panchayatana, makara-torana, Paramāra/Pratihara/Chandella dynastic styles.
