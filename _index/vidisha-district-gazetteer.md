# Index: vidisha-district-gazetteer.md

**Title/Author:** *Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha* — by P. N. Shrivastav & Dr. Rajendra Verma; District Gazetteers Department, Madhya Pradesh, Bhopal; First Edition 1979 (draft finalised 1971–73).

**Overall (3-5 sentences):** This is the standard government district gazetteer for Vidisha District (Madhya Pradesh), a composite district formed from the former Bhilsa District of Gwalior State, the princely states of Kurwai/Muhammadgarh/Pathari, and the Sironj sub-division (ex-Kota, Rajasthan). Across nineteen chapters it surveys geography, history, people, agriculture, industry, banking/trade, communications, administration, revenue, law, local government, education, health, and "Places of Interest," supported by extensive statistical appendices. The history and archaeology coverage is rich, tracing Vidisha/Besnagar from the Epics, the Mauryas, Sungas (Heliodoros Pillar), Nagas, Vakatakas, Guptas (Udayagiri caves), Paramaras (Udayaditya and the Udayeśvara temple), through Sultanate, Mughal, and Maratha periods. Chapter XIX gives detailed monument-by-monument descriptions, including the Udayeśvara (Nīlakaṇṭheśvara) temple of Udayapur, which is the document's most directly relevant content for the monograph.

**Authoritative on:** Administrative/political history of the Bhilsa–Vidisha region and its constituent princely states; the historical geography of the Betwa (Vetravatī) valley and Malwa plateau; the archaeology and monuments of the district (Besnagar/Heliodoros Pillar, Udayagiri caves, Gyaraspur, Badoh-Pathari, and Udayapur); local economy, demography, and administration c. 1950s–1970s.

**Relevance to a monograph on the Udayeśvara temple of Udaypur:** **Supporting (with one core section).** The bulk of the volume (economy, banking, modern administration, census tables) is light/peripheral context. However, several sections are directly load-bearing:
- **Chapter XIX, "Udayapur & The Udayeshwara Temple"** (lines ~2992–3004) is the single most relevant passage: founding by Paramāra King Udayāditya, temple construction dates (1059 A.D. launch, 1080 A.D. flagstaff), the brass-sheathed lingam (gift of a Mahadji Sindhia general, 1775), the Tughluq-era mosque at the temple's rear, the Udayasamudra reservoir, and satellite structures (Bijamandal/clockman's house, Barakhambi, Pisanari-ka-Mandir, Shahi Mosque 1632, Sherkhan ki Masjid 1488, Ghod-daud-ki-baodi).
- **Chapter II (History)** — Paramāra Bhoja and Udayāditya, and the explicit attribution of the "Nilkanthesvar temple at Udaypur" to Udayāditya (line 201).
- **Chapter I (Geography/Drainage)** — the Betwa river system and the Malwa/Vindhyachal plateau setting (lines 140–162); Bareth station is the rail access point for Udayapur.
- **Udayagiri caves** (same Ch. XIX section) — adjacent Gupta Vaiṣṇava context, including the Chandragupta II inscriptions and the Varāha relief.
- **Lateri (Chhoti Madagaon)** — a 10th-century Nīlakaṇṭheśvara temple "styled identically to the great temple of Udaipur" (line 2964), a useful comparandum.

## Section map

### Front matter (Preface, Advisory Board, Contents, Illustrations)
- Explains the district's composite formation and difficulty of sources; lists 23 illustrations, of which #1 is "Back view of Neelkantheshwar Temple, Udayapur" and several depict Udayagiri caves.
- Notes Udayapur among the district's "magnificent temples" alongside Gyaraspur and Badoh (line 14).

### Chapter I: General (Geography)
- Location, area (7,433 sq. km), tahsils (Vidisha, Basoda, Kurwai, Sironj, Lateri); origin of names Vidisha/Besnagar/Bhilsa/Bhaillasvamin.
- Topography (Vindhyachal/Malwa plateau), drainage dominated by the **Betwa (Betrawati)** flowing south-to-north; geology (Deccan Trap, Vindhyan sandstone — the building stone of the temples); climate (avg. rainfall 1,229.9 mm).

### Chapter II: History
- Prehistory through Freedom Movement. Key: Mauryas (Aśoka & Devī at Vidisha), Sungas (Agnimitra; **Heliodorus Garuda Pillar** c.140 B.C.), Guptas (**Udayagiri** rock inscriptions, Chandragupta II, 401 A.D.).
- Paramāras: "Bhoja Deva (1000–1055 A.D.)... his successor **Udayaditya built the magnificent Nilkanthesvar temple at Udaypur**" (line 201). Sultanate, Mughal (Aurangzeb's 1682 demolition of Vijay Mandir), Maratha periods; 1857 revolt; 1956 integration.

### Chapter III: People
- 1971 population 6,58,427; Hindi-majority; Hindus 89%, Muslims 9%, Jains 1.65%. Scheduled Castes (Chamars) and Tribes (Sahariyas). Taran Swami (Jain saint, b.1448) and Taranpanthi sect. Customs, Rai dance, fairs.

### Chapter IV: Agriculture and Irrigation
- Wheat-dominant rabi economy; *kans* weed reclamation; Halali/Kethan irrigation projects; lift irrigation on the Betwa; crop/seed/fertilizer tables; animal husbandry (Malwi cattle), fisheries, forestry, famine (1899-1900 *Chappan ka sal*).

### Chapter V: Industries
- No heavy industry; household crafts. Note: "red sandstone *kundies* of Udaypura" reputed locally (line 530). Historic Sironj textiles (chintz, fine muslin per Tavernier) and paper-making. Power from Chambal Project (1968-69). Small-scale units, cooperatives, labour welfare.

### Chapter VI: Banking, Trade and Commerce
- Indigenous *kothee-walas*/hundi banking at Sironj; indebtedness surveys; cooperative and joint-stock banks; Mughal/Maratha mints at Bhilsa, Ganj Basoda, Sironj; regulated mandis; historic weights/measures.

### Chapter VII: Communications
- Ancient trade routes (Paithan–Sravasthi), Sher Shah's Agra-Burhanpur road; modern roads; **Bhopal-Bina railway** (Central Railway) with Bareth and Vidisha stations; postal history.

### Chapter VIII: Miscellaneous Occupations
- Census of public administration, learned professions (teachers, medical, legal, engineers, artists/sculptors), and domestic/personal service trades.

### Chapter IX: Economic Trends
- Livelihood patterns, wholesale price series (Depression, WWII, post-Independence), wages, employment exchange, Community Development blocks (Vidisha block est. 1-4-1956).

### Chapter X: General Administration
- Historical governance of constituent states; Collector-centred modern structure; sub-divisions, tahsildars, patwari circles; district and Central offices.

### Chapter XI: Revenue Administration
- Mughal/Todarmal assessment, ijaredari/zamindari/ryotwari systems, jagir & zamindari abolition (1951 Acts), MP Land Revenue Code 1959 (bhumiswami tenure), landholding ceilings, agricultural wage tables.

### Chapter XII: Law and Order and Justice
- Historical courts of the princely states; Madhya Bharat/MP court structure; Nyaya Panchayats; crime statistics; police organization; jails; Bar Associations.

### Chapter XIII: Other Departments
- Department-by-department administrative listing (Agriculture, Veterinary, Fishery, Co-operation, Education, PWD, Public Health Engineering, etc.).

### Chapter XIV: Local Self-Government
- Municipal history (Sironj 1885 — oldest; Vidisha 1904; Kurwai 1917; Ganj Basoda 1943); village panchayats; municipal income/expenditure tables; drinking-water supply (Vidisha drawn from the Betwa, line 1790).

### Chapter XV: Education and Culture
- Educational history from ancient Sanskrit learning to modern schools/colleges (S.S.L. Jain College). Notes a Sun Temple at Vidisha built V.S. 935 (line 1867) and the region's literary heritage (Kālidāsa's *Meghadūta*).

### Chapter XVI: Medical and Public Health Services
- Early medicine, vital statistics, causes of mortality, hospitals/dispensaries, family planning.

### Chapter XVII–XVIII: Other Social Services / Public Life
- Social welfare, voluntary and social-service organizations (per contents outline; brief).

### Chapter XIX: Places of Interest (most relevant)
- **Badoh** (Gadarmal Temple, Jain complex, Dashavatara/Sola-Khambi); **Besnagar/Bes** (Heliodoros "Kham Baba" Pillar c.140 B.C., Cunningham's finds, pre-Mauryan canal, lime-mortar discovery, 1963-75 stratigraphy).
- **Gyaraspur** (Maladevi temple, Hindola Torana, Shalbhanjika); **Kurwai/Lateri/Pathari** (Pathari's Rashtrakuta *Bhimagada* Garuḍadhvaja, 861 A.D.; **Lateri Chhoti Madagaon Nīlakaṇṭheśvara temple "styled identically to... Udaipur"**).
- **Udayagiri caves** (Cave 5 Varāha relief; Chandragupta II inscription Cave 6, G.E. 82 = 401 A.D.; Samudra Manthan ceiling).
- **Udayapur & the Udayeśvara Temple** — see Key entities below. Notable quote: "The sculpture style is finer than Khajuraho" (line 2999). Also Modern Vidisha town (Bhillasvamin Sun Temple, Lohangi Rock, Bijamandal Mosque on the Vijay Mandir foundations).

### Statistical Appendices
- Rainfall, land utilization, irrigation, crop production, machinery, livestock, revenue, and liquor-offence tables (c. 1950s–1973).

## Key entities, dates & terms
- **Udayapur (Udayāpura)** — municipal village, Basoda Tahsil, 6.5 km east of Bareth Station; walled with giant uncemented stone blocks; founded by Paramāra King **Udayāditya** (late 11th c. A.D.).
- **Udayeśvara / Nīlakaṇṭheśvara Temple** — built by order of Udayāditya, **1059 A.D.** (construction launch) to **1080 A.D.** (flagstaff); Indo-Aryan (Bhūmija) style; four gateways with dvārapālas; 8 attendant shrines (6 ruined); large stone lingam sheathed in **brass given 1775 by a Maratha general of Mahadji Sindhia**; exterior carvings of Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Gaṇeśa, with Śiva/Durgā dominant; "finer than Khajuraho."
- **Sultan Muhammad Tughluq (1336–38)** — mutilated carvings and built a **mosque at the rear** of the temple (two Persian inscriptions).
- **Udayasamudra** — large reservoir lake excavated by Udayāditya beside the temple.
- **Satellite Udayapur structures** — Bijamandal/Ghadiyalan-ka-makan (clockman's house, Sun-god invocation); Barakhambi (11th-c. pillared hall); **Pisanari-ka-Mandir** (built by a poor woman from earnings grinding flour for the temple masons); Shahi Mosque (Jahangir–Shah Jahan, completed **1632 A.D.**); Sherkhan ki Masjid (**1488 A.D.**, Ghias Shah Khilji of Mandu); Ghod-daud-ki-baodi (cavalry stepwell).
- **Paramāra Bhoja Deva** (1000–1055 A.D.) — predecessor of Udayāditya.
- **Betwa (Vetravatī / Betrawati)** — main river; enters district 6 km south of Vidisha town; flows S–N; defines the central valley setting.
- **Besnagar (Vidiśā / Wessanagara)** — ancient capital of Dāśārṇa, 3 km from modern Vidisha; site of the **Heliodoros (Heliodora) Garuḍa Pillar (Kham Baba)**, c. **140 B.C.**, earliest Vāsudeva/Vaiṣṇava monument.
- **Udayagiri** — Gupta cave-temple ridge near Vidisha; Chandragupta II inscription **G.E. 82 = 401 A.D.**; monumental **Varāha** relief.
- **Bhilsa → Vidisha** — town renamed in 1956; named from the **Bhaillasvamin/Bhillasvamin Sun Temple**; Vijay Mandir destroyed by Iltutmish (1234) and by Aurangzeb (1682, who renamed the town Alamgirpur).
- **Bareth Station** — rail access point for Udayapur (Bhopal-Bina line).
- **Lateri (Chhoti Madagaon)** — 10th-c. Nīlakaṇṭheśvara temple stylistically tied to Udayapur.
