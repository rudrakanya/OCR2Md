# Index: madhya-bharat-temples-and-architecture.md

**Title/Author:** This file bundles TWO works. (1) *The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat* by Dr. D. R. Patil, M.A., LL.B., Ph.D. (Dept. of Archaeology, Govt. of Madhya Bharat, Gwalior; pub. 28 Dec 1952), comprising Parts I and II (general history, arts, architecture, and a place-by-place directory). (2) *Some Paramāra Temples From Madhya Pradesh: A Case Study of Village Un* by Swati Mondal Adhikari (Directorate of Archaeology, Archives and Museums, Bhopal), a focused monograph on the bhūmija temple cluster at Un (appended from line 627 onward).

**Overall (3-5 sentences):** Patil's book is a regional cultural-archaeological handbook: how Madhya Bharat (the 1948 union of 22 states) was formed, its geography, peoples, a long dynastic history (Haihayas → Pradyotas → Mauryas → Shungas → Satavahanas/Kshatrapas → Guptas → Pratiharas/Rashtrakutas → Paramaras/Kacchavahas → Sultanate → Mughal → Maratha), and chapters on architecture, sculpture, painting, music, and crafts, followed by a gazetteer of key sites. The Adhikari monograph is a technical Bhūmija-style analysis centered on Un but explicitly framed around the Udayeśvara/Nīlakaṇṭheśvara temple of Udaypur as the canonical type. Together they give both the broad Paramāra historical context and a granular comparative architectural vocabulary directly applicable to Udaypur.

**Authoritative on:** Madhya Bharat dynastic history; Paramāra political and cultural history (Bhoja, Udayāditya); regional temple typology, especially the **Bhūmija** order; site-level descriptions across northern Madhya Bharat and Malwa; the Un temple cluster (chronology, ṣilpis, iconography).

**Relevance to a monograph on the Udayeśvara temple of Udaypur: CORE.** The text repeatedly treats Udayeśvara as the supreme/archetypal monument. Specifically: Patil's Ch. V (Architecture, ~lines 354-356) names the Udayeshwar temple as standing "supreme," praises its śikhara and carved medallions, and recounts that it allegedly escaped destruction; the Part II Udaypur entry (line 582) gives builder (Paramāra King Udayāditya, 1059-1080 A.D.), pink sandstone, and the Tughlaq mosque addition (1336-1338). The Adhikari monograph is even more directly load-bearing: Kramrisch (1946) "utiliz[ed] the Nilkantheshvara temple of Udaypur as the classic archetype" of Bhūmija (line 636); the entire "Technical Analysis of the Bhūmija Architecture" section (lines 656-668) supplies the vocabulary (latā, kūṭastambha, śukanāsā, etc.) for describing Udayeśvara; and the Un chronology dates the Nīlakaṇṭheśvara at Un to "the reign of King Udayaditya" (line 742).

## Section map

### The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat — Preface & Contents (lines 6-114)
- Authorship, acknowledgments (draws on M. B. Garde's *Archaeology in Gwalior* for Udaypur etc.); note (line 25) flags shared source material with two companion files; full table of contents and illustration list (incl. "Nilkanthesvar Temple, Udaypur," plate 17).

### Ch. I — How Madhya Bharat Was Formed (lines 120-139)
- 1948 union of 22 states (Gwalior largest) under Sardar Patel; covenant 22 Apr 1948; area 46,785 sq mi.

### Ch. II — Geographical Setting (lines 143-173)
- Five natural divisions; ancient names (Avanti, Dasharna, Anupadesha); Bhilsa region monuments "second to none."

### Ch. III — Peoples and Languages (lines 177-215)
- Demographics (~8 million), religious/caste breakdown, tribes (Bhils, Gonds), dialects (Malwi, Bundeli, Rajasthani).

### Ch. IV — Historical Pre-View (lines 219-329)
- Long dynastic narrative. **Paramāra section (lines 267-275):** Siyaka II, Vakpati/Munja (974-995), **Bhoja (1010-1050)**, Udayaditya (1058-1087), Arjunavarman (last able prince, 1211-1215); Paramāra art at "Udaypur, Nemawar, Jamli, Badnawar, Un." Sultanate/Mughal/Maratha succession follows.

### Ch. V — Arts and Architecture (lines 333-378)
- (A) Sacred architecture evolution (Gupta one-room shrine → 10th-12th c. full plan with garbhagṛha, śikhara, Sabhāmaṇḍapa, toraṇa). Key Udaypur passages at lines 354-356. Muslim and secular/civil architecture follow.

### Ch. V (B) — Sculpture and Painting (lines 382-425)
- Sculpture from 3rd c. B.C.; Besnagar Yakshini/Kubera colossi; medieval figure-sculpture at "Udaypur" (line 408). Painting: Bagh caves (tempera, Gupta), Gwalior tile mosaics.

### Ch. V (C)-(D) — Music & Crafts (lines 429-468)
- Gwalior music school (Man Singh, Tansen); stone carving, ceramics, Chanderi/Maheshwar textiles, lacquer, metalwork.

### Ch. VI — Archaeological Monuments (lines 472-513)
- 1,783 monuments, 145 of National Importance; district tables; chronological and religious breakdowns (240 Śaiva temples incl. Udaypur; Mattamayura order at Kadwaha/Terahi/Ranod).

### Part II — Directory of Sites (lines 517-625)
- Gazetteer entries. **Udaypur (line 581-582):** Udayeśvara (Nīlakaṇṭheśvara) temple, pink sandstone, Udayāditya 1059-1080 A.D., balanced śikhara with carved medallions, Tughlaq mosque addition 1336-1338. Other entries: Gwalior, Suhania (Kakanmadh), Padhavli, Bhilsa, Besnagar (Heliodoros Pillar), Udaygiri, Gyaraspur, Dhar (Bhoja Shala), Mandu, Bagh, Maheshwar.

### Un Monograph — Introduction & Historiography (lines 630-638)
- Scholarship from Cunningham (1876-77) to Krishna Deva (1975/92). Quote: Kramrisch (1946) "formally identified and classified the regional style as **Bhūmija** ('country-born')... utilizing the Nilkantheshvara temple of Udaypur as the classic archetype" (line 636).

### Un — Location, Legends, Maratha Renovations (lines 642-652)
- Un = Jain *Pāvāgiri*; "un" (one less than 100) legend; Holkar-era restorations by Ramchandra Ballala Bhuskutte (1751).

### Un — Technical Analysis of Bhūmija Architecture (lines 656-668)
- Definitional section: latā, kūṭastambha/stambhakūṭa, śukanāsā, talacchanda (caturaśra/vṛtta), mandovara zones (vedībandha, jaṅghā, baraṇḍikā), crowning members. Core comparative apparatus for Udayeśvara.

### Un — Detailed Temple Directory (lines 672-715)
- Eight Śaiva + two Jain temples; per-temple plan, iconography, inscriptions (Bhoja sarpabandha record at Chaubara Dera No. 1).

### Un — Ṣilpis, Marks, Quarries (lines 719-731)
- Masons' signatures per temple; Chirakhan quarry; coarse low-durability sandstone explains decay (contrast with pink sandstone).

### Un — Chronology (lines 735-744)
- Sequence c.1000-1206 A.D.; **Nīlakaṇṭheśvara & Omkāreśvara c.1075-1100 under King Udayaditya** (line 742); Goaleśvara dated 1206 (V.S. 1263).

### Glossary (lines 748-783)
- ~40 IAST architectural terms defined (āmalaka, bhadra, bhūmija, śukanāsā, vedībandha, vṛtta, etc.).

## Key entities, dates & terms
- **Udayāditya** — Paramāra king; builder of Udayeśvara/Nīlakaṇṭheśvara, Udaypur; reign given as 1058/59-1080/1087 A.D.
- **Bhoja** — greatest Paramāra (1010-1050); Bhoja Shala/Dhar; inscription at Chaubara Dera No. 1, Un.
- **Udayeśvara / Nīlakaṇṭheśvara temple, Udaypur** — pink sandstone Bhūmija archetype; Tughlaq mosque addition 1336-1338 A.D.
- **Bhūmija** — "country-born" Nāgara sub-style of the Paramāras; Udaypur as canonical example.
- **Paramāras** — Malwa dynasty; capital Dhāra; fell to Alauddin Khilji's general 1305 A.D. (Mahlakadeva).
- **Stella Kramrisch (1946, *The Hindu Temple*)** — classified Bhūmija using Udaypur.
- **D. R. Patil (1952)** — author of main work; also his *Descriptive and Classified List of Ancient Monuments*.
- **Un (Pāvāgiri)** — Nimar-district temple cluster, 21°50'N 75°27'E; ten temples c.1000-1206 A.D.
- **Key terms:** latā, kūṭastambha, śukanāsā, vedībandha, jaṅghā, garbhagṛha, śikhara, saptaratha/pañcaratha, vṛtta/caturaśra, Sapta Mātṛkās, Maru-Gurjara (Caulukyan).
- **Other dates:** Heliodoros Pillar 2nd c. B.C.; Yashodharman pillars 532-533 A.D.; Sas Bahu temple 1093 A.D.; Maratha entry into Malwa c.1728-1740.
