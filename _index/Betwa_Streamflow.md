# Index: Betwa_Streamflow.md

**Title/Author:** "Streamflow of the Betwa River under the Combined Effect of LU-LC and Climate Change" — Amit Kumar, Raghvender Pratap Singh, Swatantra Kumar Dubey, and Kumar Gaurav (IISER Bhopal, MP, India; with Seoul Nat'l Univ. of Science and Technology).

**Overall (2-3 sentences):** A technical hydrology paper modeling how land-use/land-cover change and climate change jointly affect streamflow of the (Upper) Betwa River in semi-arid Central India. Using satellite imagery, an ANN-Cellular Automata land-use model, and the SWAT hydrological model driven by CMIP6 climate scenarios, it projects substantial declines in monsoon streamflow and rainfall through 2100. The substance (modeling methods, projections, drought indices) is irrelevant to a temple monograph.

**Relevance to a monograph on the Udayeśvara temple of Udaypur:** Light. The paper is a quantitative water-resources study with no historical, architectural, or cultural content. Its only value is atmospheric/geographic: the Betwa is the river of the Udaypur region, and the "Study Area" section (Section 2.1) offers a few concrete facts about the river and its semi-arid Central Indian setting that could lend texture to an introductory "sense of place." Use sparingly and only for landscape/climate color, not as a substantive source.

**Usable facts for scene-setting:**
- The Betwa River originates in Raisen district, Madhya Pradesh, and joins the Yamuna in Hamirpur district, Uttar Pradesh.
- The river is largely intermittent in its upper reaches and becomes perennial only in its lower ~85 km before the Yamuna confluence.
- The catchment lies in a semi-arid to dry sub-humid climate of Central India.
- Strong seasonal extremes: summer (Mar-May) highs of 40-45 °C; winter (Nov-Feb) lows down to ~2 °C.
- The monsoon (June-September) delivers about 92% of the catchment's total rainfall.
- Topography ranges roughly 376-669 m above mean sea level, higher in the west, lower in the east.
- Regional trend context: Central India's mean summer monsoon rainfall has declined ~10-20% (1950-2015), with increasingly frequent dry events after 2000.
