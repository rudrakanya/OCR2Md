# Index: serpentine-scimitar-inscription-udaypur.md

**Title/Author:** "A serpentine scimitar of letters from Udaypur, district Vidisha, M.P." by Saarthak Singh.

**Overall (3-5 sentences):** A short scholarly paper presenting a newly-discovered, previously unpublished Nāgarī inscription found by the author during fieldwork at the Udayeśvara (Nīlakaṇṭheśvara) temple of Udaypur. The inscription, engraved on a reset sandstone slab in the temple's parapet wall, combines a serpent-shaped diagram (*nāgabandha/sarpabandha*) of the Sanskrit alphabet and grammatical endings with two short accompanying inscriptions, one of which names the diagram a *varṇṇanāgakṛpāṇikā* ("serpentine scimitar of letters"). The author analyses the diagram's structure, deciphers a metrical verse, and cross-references an identical composition at Ujjain's Mahākāla temple to date and interpret it. He argues the graph was not a teaching aid but a magico-political device of the Paramāra kings Udayāditya and Naravarman, serving Śaiva dynastic power and the "preservation of language and social order."

**Authoritative on:** the Udaypur *varṇṇanāgakṛpāṇikā* inscription (palaeography, content, decipherment); the corpus of Paramāra serpentine alphabet-diagrams (Udaypur, Ujjain, Dhar, Un); the Udayeśvara temple's epigraphic archive.

**Relevance to a monograph on the Udayeśvara temple of Udaypur:** Core — it is authored by a leading scholar of the temple, documents a unique unpublished inscription physically on the temple's parapet wall, situates the temple within its multi-century epigraphic archive, and reinforces the temple's foundation context (1080, named for Udayāditya).

## Section map

### Abstract & Keywords
- Summarises the discovery, the *varṇṇanāgakṛpāṇikā* designation, the Ujjain parallel describing it as a *siddhāsiputrikā* ("unique magical sword") of Udayāditya and Naravarman, and the dating to the late-11th century.
- Keywords: Udaypur, varṇṇanāgakṛpāṇikā, Paramāra, Malwa.

### Temple as epigraphic archive (intro)
- Udayeśvara built c. 1080 in *bhūmija* style (Paramāra dynasty, c. 972–1305); holds continuous inscriptions in Sanskrit, Persian, Hindi spanning eight centuries (samvat 1137 to samvat 1985).
- Corpus recorded by Gwalior State reports and ASI epigraphy branch; only partially published (Trivedi, Mittal).

### The inscription's physical setting
- Nāgarī script on pinkish sandstone slab 70cm high, set into the outer parapet wall (*prākāra*) at the south courtyard projection; slab is reset/reversed, likely during 1923–29 Gwalior or 1981–84 ASI restorations.

### Palaeography & orthography
- Inch-high rectilinear characters comparable to late-11th-century local records; orthographic features: va for ba, sa for śa, doubling of consonants after r (e.g. *varṇṇa*).

### The serpentine diagram (Fig. 3)
- "Head" = 14 vowels in short/long pairs; tubular "body" = semivowels, 5 classes of stops in a rhomboidal knot, sibilants; arrangement follows the Śivasūtras of Pāṇini's *Aṣṭādhyāyī*; base has 3 aspirates (*upadhmānīya, jihvāmūlīya, visarga*); tail = 21 noun terminations and 18 verb terminations.

### The short inscriptions & verse
- Two short records; the upper two-line verse deciphered via onsite eye-copy. Quote (l.1–2): "bhāradvāṃ jīvihitv=ēmāṃ varṇna-nāga-kṛpāṇikāṃ ... pālaḥ pālayatu gām || 1 ||".
- Read as a causative imperative: "may the protector cause this serpentine sword of letters to protect..."; imagery maps snake (hood/body/tail) onto sword (blade/hilt/strap).

### The Ujjain parallel & interpretation
- Identical diagram at Mahākāla temple, Ujjain (Sastri 1960): *udayāditya-nāmāṅka-varṇna-nāga-kṛpaṇikā*, composed by "the friend of talented poets" (Naravarman); lines 24–26 call it a *siddhāsiputrikā* for *varṇnasthityai* (preservation of language/social order).
- Argues against the "educational tool" view (Trivedi, Pollock); frames it as a Śaiva dynastic device; copies placed at leading Paramāra Śiva temples.

### References & Figures
- Key scholarship: Deva 1975, Trivedi 1978/1989, Mittal 1979, Salomon 1982/1998, Sastri 1960, Pollock 2006, Singh 2019. Figures 1–4 locate and detail the slab, graph, and short inscriptions.

## Key entities, dates & terms
- **Udayeśvara / Nīlakaṇṭheśvara temple** — Śiva temple, Udaypur, Vidisha dist., M.P.; built c. 1080, *bhūmija* style.
- **varṇṇanāgakṛpāṇikā** — "serpentine scimitar of letters"; the diagram's designation.
- **siddhāsiputrikā** — "unique magical sword" (Ujjain verse).
- **nāgabandha / sarpabandha** — serpentine epigraphic graph type (Salomon 1998).
- **Udayāditya** — Paramāra king, c. 1070–93; founder/namesake of Udaypur temple and tank (1080).
- **Naravarman** — son of Udayāditya, r. 1094–1134; credited with composing the graph.
- **Paramāra dynasty** — Malwa, c. 972–1305.
- **Aṣṭādhyāyī / Śivasūtras** — Pāṇini's grammar; basis of the alphabet ordering.
- **Saarthak Singh** — author; also wrote the 2019 Udayeśvara foundation-record study (Zenodo).
- **Dates** — temple c. 1080; graph late-11th c. (after Udayāditya's death 1093); inscriptions span samvat 1137–1985.
- **Related sites** — Ujjain (Mahākāla), Dhar, Un (other Paramāra serpentine inscriptions).
