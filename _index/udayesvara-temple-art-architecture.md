# Index: udayesvara-temple-art-architecture.md

**Title/Author:** *The Udayeśvara Temple: Art, Architecture and Philosophy of the Śaiva Siddhānta* — Anupa Pande (ASI Bhopal Circle Library, Acc. No. 10789, Class No. 721/PAN).

**Overall (3-5 sentences):** A monograph on the Udayeśvara temple at Udayapur (Malwa, Vidisha district, Madhya Pradesh), a Śaiva temple built in the second half of the 11th century CE by Udayāditya Paramāra. The book studies the temple holistically through primary Śilpaśāstra and Śaivāgama sources, integrating architecture, sculpture, iconography, and the metaphysics of Śaiva Siddhānta. The extracted portion covers the front matter and Chapter I (architecture and sculpture) in detail: the Bhūmija style, ground plan, mouldings, śikhara, śukanāsa, śūrasenakas, exterior/interior iconography, the vedī, and the temple's later history. Later chapters (II–III) and appended Śilpaśāstra translations are listed in the contents but not transcribed here.

**Authoritative on:** Udayeśvara temple architecture and sculpture; Bhūmija temple style; Paramāra-period Malwa temple iconography; śukanāsa and śūrasenaka sculptural programs; directional/Dikpāla iconographic placement; later (Sultanate, Aurangzeb, Maratha) modifications of the complex.

**Relevance to a monograph on the Udayeśvara temple of Udaypur:** core — this is a dedicated, detailed primary study of the temple itself.

### I. Architecture and Sculpture — Introduction
- Temple at Udayapur, Malwa (modern Vidisha); Śaiva, built 2nd half 11th c. CE by Udayāditya Paramāra, who ascended in 1070 CE and founded the city of Udayapur.
- *Udayapur Praśasti* quoted: "...another sun, as it were, destroying the dense darkness in the form of his magnanimous foes..."

### The Bhūmija Style of Architecture
- Earliest Śilpaśāstra reference is the *Samarāṅgaṇasūtradhāra* of Bhoja Paramāra; style matured under the Paramāras; concentrated in Malwa, Rajasthan, N. Maharashtra.
- Etymology of *Bhūmija*: earth-born/regional; terrestrial (vs. divine origin); born of *bhūmis* (storeys). Features: śikhara with four latās of gavākṣa motifs, kūṭastambhas in quadrants, śūrasenaka medallions, eastern śukanāsa, always nirandhara.

### Layout and Ground Plan
- Red sandstone, east-facing; stellate-cum-circular, *saptaratha* plan, *saptabhūmi* elevation; plan via rotated square (*parivartanā*).
- Components: garbhagṛha, antarāla, gūḍhamaṇḍapa (3 mukhamaṇḍapas), vedī, jagati with eight subsidiary shrines (*aṣṭatāyatana*); original eastern stairway with Śaiva pratīhāras.

### Architectural Mouldings and Ornamentation
- Pīṭha: Bhiṭṭa 1 & 2, jādyakumbha, karṇaka, grāsapaṭṭi.
- Maṇḍovara: vedibandha (khura, kumbha, kalaśa) and jaṅghā (pillar-like buttresses with khaṭṭakas); buttresses continue upward into kūṭastambhas.

### Key Structural & Sculptural Compositions
- Śikhara: kūṭastambhas, crowning āmalaka; NW face has a flag-bearer figure climbing the spire (dhvaja).
- Śukanāsa (east): top kīrtimukha with makaras; upper Naṭeśa Śiva (tāṇḍava, ūrdhvajānu karaṇa); central Hariharārkāpitāmaha (Śiva+Viṣṇu+Sūrya+Brahmā); flanking dancing devi and a rare **Dancing Sarasvatī** (linked to Bhoja's patronage of learning, Bhojaśālā of Dhara).
- Celestial mansions: North devis (Mahālakṣmī, Sarvamaṅgalā, Rāmbhā, Maheśvarī, Gaurī; Nandī below); South includes Śreyā/Śrī on a preta holding a godhā.
- Three śūrasenakas: West (Naṭeśa + six-armed Mṛtyuñjaya Śiva, invoked against death in battle); South and North (Śiva dancing with seated Śiva below).

### Iconography of the Outer Walls (Maṇḍovara)
- Dikpāla-based directional placement (tabulated). Deities include Mahābhairava, Sūrya (with Daṇḍī & Piṅgala), Agni, Indra (Airāvata), Ardhanārīśvara, Harihara, Umā-Maheśvara, Sarasvatī, Gāyatrī-Sāvitrī-Sarasvatī, Yama, Nirṛti, Tripurāntaka, Varuṇa, Vāyu, Īśāna, Kārttikeya/Skanda, Brahmā, Mahiṣāsuramardinī, Cāmuṇḍā.
- Garbhagṛha facets: Gaṇeśa/Gaṇeśānī & Virabhadra (south); Virabhadra strangling Dakṣa, Nandīśvara, Bhṛṅgī (west); Umā-Maheśvara, Lakṣmī-Nārāyaṇa, Brahmā-Brahmāṇī (north).

### Internal Architecture and the Sanctum
- Doorways: patraśākhā, gandharvaśākhā, stambhaśākhā; Gaṅgā & Yamunā at jambs; Navagraha lintels (Rāhu, Ketu, Saturn on south porch); samvaraṇā bell-roof.
- Interior: four central pillars, circular dome with kīrtimukhas and padmaśīla pendants.
- Garbhagṛha: Śivalinga with brass mukha cover gifted by Khanderao Appaji, a general of Mahadji Scindia, in 1775 CE.

### The Vedī (Sacrificial Hall)
- Low flat-roofed square building (~23 ft square), four pillars, stone balustrade (vedikā) with gavākṣa jālā. Per Cunningham and tradition, a public hall for Veda preaching and yajñas.

### Historical Changes and Later Modifications
- 1325 CE order (Delhi Sultanate; Muhammad Tughluq/Alauddin Khalji) to dismantle/repurpose; mosque conversion of rear jagati/vedī with a miḥrāb and two Persian inscriptions; Aurangzeb-era defacement (per legend); 18th-c. Maratha Scindia restoration and brass mukhaliṅga installation.
