# Index: madhya-bharat-cultural-heritage.md

**Title/Author:** *The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat* by Dr. D. R. Patil, M.A., LL.B., Ph.D. (Department of Archaeology, Govt. of Madhya Bharat, Gwalior). Published 28 December 1952, drawing substantially on M. B. Garde's *Archaeology in Gwalior*.

**Overall (3-5 sentences):** A general handbook surveying the land, peoples, history, arts, and archaeological monuments of the former Madhya Bharat state (formed 1948 from 22 princely states). Part I gives thematic chapters (formation, geography, peoples/languages, a chronological history from the Haihayas to the British, and arts/architecture); Part II is a region-by-region gazetteer of important archaeological sites. The work is strongly archaeology- and temple-centred, treating Hindu, Buddhist, Jain, and Muslim monuments across five natural divisions. It repeatedly singles out the Udayeśvara temple of Udaypur as the supreme achievement of Madhya Bharat temple architecture. It has companion volumes on temples/architecture and on inscriptions/shrines.

**Authoritative on:** Madhya Bharat archaeology and monument distribution (1,783 catalogued monuments); medieval North-Indian (Indo-Aryan/Nāgara) temple architecture; Paramāra Malwa history and art; Bhilsa/Vidiśā region antiquities; Śaiva, Vaiṣṇava, Jain and Buddhist sacred sites; the Gwalior school of music.

**Relevance to a monograph on the Udayeśvara temple of Udaypur:** **Core.** The book devotes a dedicated Udaypur section (lines 970–996) describing the Udayeśvara/Nīlkaṇṭheśvara temple's foundation by Paramāra king Udayāditya, its dated inscriptions (commenced V.S. 1116/A.D. 1059; flagstaff V.S. 1137/A.D. 1080), plan, sculpture, and later mutilation. Multiple other sections flag it: Ch. IV covers Paramāra dynasty history (Sīyaka II → Mūñja → Bhoja → Udayāditya → Arjunavarman) and Malwa temple art (lines 289–302); Ch. V repeatedly names Udayeśvara as the supreme/best-preserved temple and recounts the legend of its survival (lines 392, 394, 450); Ch. VI's Śaivism section lists Udaypur among major 10th–12th c. Śaiva temples (line 650). Nemawar's Siddheśvara śikhara is explicitly compared to Udaypur's (line 1172). Strong supporting material on Vidiśā, Besnagar, Udaygiri, Pathari, and the broader Bhilsa district context surrounds it.

## Section map

### Preface & Contents (lines 7–120)
- Author's aims, acknowledgements, and note on three companion files sharing Patil's 1952 source. Full table of contents (Parts I & II) and list of 28 illustrations (Illus. 17 = Nilkanthesvar Temple, Udaypur).

### Ch. I: How Madhya Bharat Was Formed (lines 126–148)
- Post-1947 integration of 22 states (Sardar Patel); covenant signed 22 Apr 1948, "United State of Gwalior, Indore and Malwa," inaugurated 28 May 1948. Area 46,785 sq mi; pop. 79,50,494; Gwalior winter / Indore summer capital.

### Ch. II: Geographical Setting (lines 152–183)
- Five natural divisions tied to the Chambal/Betwa/Narmada and Vindhya/Satpura systems. Ancient names: Dasharna (Bhilsa region), Avanti (Malwa plateau), Anupadesha (Nimar). Bhilsa/Vidiśā called the focal point of the NE plateau.

### Ch. III: Peoples and Languages (lines 187–226)
- Population ~8 million (90% Hindu, 6% Muslim, ~1% Jain). Adivasi tribes (Bhils, Bhilalas, Gonds, Korkus, Meenas); Rajput clans. Languages: Western Hindi (Brij, Bundeli), Malwi, Rajasthani, Nimadi (Grierson's classification).

### Ch. IV: Historical Pre-View (lines 230–363) — key historical chapter
- Sweeps from Haihayas/Yadavas and Kartivirya Arjuna (Mahishmati) through Pradyotas, Mauryas (Ashoka viceroy at Ujjain), Shungas (Heliodoros pillar, Bhagabhadra), Satavahanas, Shakas (Rudradaman), Nagas of Padmavati/Vidisha, Guptas, Hunas, Yashodharman (Mandsor pillars, A.D. 532–33), Pratiharas, Rashtrakutas.
- **Paramāras & Kacchavāhas (10th–13th c., lines 289–306):** Sīyaka II, Vākpati/Mūñja (974–995), **Bhoja (1010–1050)**, **Udayāditya (1058–1087)**, Arjunavarman (1211–1215, last able prince). "In the long array of these sacred structures, the Udayeshwar temple stands supreme" (line 392, in Ch. V). Then Muslim invasions, Mandu Sultans, Mughal/Maratha periods to 1818.

### Ch. V: Arts and Architecture (lines 367–567)
- (A) Architecture: evolution from Kasrawad stupas and Gupta one-cell temples to full 10th–12th c. Nāgara temples (garbhagṛha, sabhāmaṇḍapa, śikhara, toraṇa). Udayeśvara named supreme; legend that it survived the iconoclasts (lines 392–394). Then Muslim, civil/secular architecture (Man Mandir, Gujari Mahal).
- (B) Sculpture & Painting: Besnagar Yakshinis/Kubera; Gupta art; Bagh caves vs Ajanta (tempera, Gupta-period). (C) Music: Gwalior school, Raja Man Singh, Tansen. (D) Industrial Arts: stone-carving, ceramics, textiles (Chanderi, Maheshwar), lacquer, metalwork.

### Ch. VI: Archaeological Monuments — overview (lines 571–758)
- 1,783 monuments at 467 sites across 16 districts (Bhilsa = richest in antiquity/importance; table at lines 581–598). Chronological tiers; religious history by sect.
- **Śaivism (lines 643–654):** ~240 of ~750 temples Śaiva; Udaypur listed among finest 10th–12th c. Śaiva temples (line 650); Mattamayura ("mad peacock") monastic order at Kadwaha/Terahi/Ranod.
- Vaiṣṇavism (~87 shrines; Heliodoros pillar earliest), Jainism (89 shrines), Islam (~113 mosques). Forts/gadhis, sati stones, memorial pillars, chhattris, wells/tanks, sarais/trade routes (Vidisha–Ujjain–Mahishmati).

### Part II-A: Northern Madhya Bharat (lines 764–932)
- Gazetteer: Gwalior Fort (Sas Bahu, Teli-ka-Mandir, Man Mandir, Jain colossi), Naresar, Pawaya (Padmavati/Nagas), Ater, Suhania (Kakanmadh), Padhavli-Mitaoli, Narwar (Nalapura), Surwaya, Kadwaha, Ranod (Khokhai Math), Terahi, Mahua, Chanderi.

### Part II-B: NE Malwa Plateau / Bhilsa District (lines 936–1034) — core region
- Bhilsa (anc. Vidiśā; Bijamandal mosque), Besnagar (Heliodoros/Khamb Baba pillar), Udaygiri (20 caves; Varaha cave No. 5), **Udaypur (Udayeśvara temple, lines 970–996)**, Gyaraspur (Hindola Torana, Maladevi), Badoh-Pathari (Gadarmal temple; Pathari Garuḍadhvaja A.D. 861).
- **Udayeśvara key facts (lines 980–996):** Founded by Paramāra Udayāditya (town Udayapura, temple Udayeśvara, tank Udayasamudra); built A.D. 1059–1080; pink sandstone; square courtyard with eight attendant shrines; garbhagṛha + sabhāmaṇḍapa + three mukhamaṇḍapas; Śiva liṅga with brass face donated by Khande Rao Appaji V.S. 1832 (A.D. 1775). Mosque at rear built with temple materials by Muhammad Tughlaq A.H. 737–39 (A.D. 1336–38), per Persian inscriptions; "main building... escaped without serious damage... a fine example of the Indo-Aryan style." Other monuments: Bijamandal/Ghadiyalan-ka-makan, Bara-khambi, Pisnari-ka-mandir, Shahi Masjid (A.D. 1632), Sher Khan's mosque (A.D. 1488), rock sculptures (Raon-tor Śiva, Sapta Mātṛkās).

### Part II-C: Malwa Plateau (lines 1038–1112)
- Ujjain (Mahakal Jyotirlinga, Kaliadeh, Jantar Mantar), Dhar (Bhojaśālā/Kamal Maula mosque, Lat Masjid), Mandu (Jahaz Mahal, Hindola Mahal, Jami Masjid, Hoshang's Tomb, Rupamati's Pavilions).

### Part II-E: Narmada Valley / Nimar (lines 1115–1175)
- Bagh (Buddhist caves & paintings), Maheshwar (Mahishmati; Ahilyabai Holkar ghats/chhattri), Un (Paramāra-style temples "matching that of Udaypur," line 1150), Nemawar (Siddheśvara temple, śikhara compared to Udaypur's, line 1172).

## Key entities, dates & terms
- **Udayāditya** — Paramāra king (1058–1087); founder of Udayapura, Udayeśvara temple, Udayasamudra tank.
- **Udayeśvara / Nīlkaṇṭheśvara Mahādeva** — Śiva temple at Udaypur, A.D. 1059–1080; pink sandstone; Indo-Aryan/Nāgara apogee.
- **Bhoja (1010–1050)** — greatest Paramāra; Dhar capital, Bhojaśālā.
- **Paramāras** — Malwa dynasty (Sīyaka II, Mūñja, Bhoja, Udayāditya, Arjunavarman); ended 1305 (Mahlakadeva, Ain-ul-Mulk/Alauddin Khilji).
- **Vidiśā / Bhilsa / Besnagar** — ancient cultural focus of NE plateau; Heliodoros (Garuḍadhvaja) pillar honoring Vāsudeva, court of King Bhagabhadra, ambassador of Antialcidas of Taxila (1st–2nd c. B.C.).
- **Udaygiri caves** — 20 rock-cut caves; Cave 5 Varaha (Gupta, 4th–5th c. A.D.).
- **Mattamayura** — Śaiva ("mad peacock") monastic order; monasteries at Kadwaha, Terahi, Ranod, Surwaya.
- **Muhammad Tughlaq** — built mosque at rear of Udayeśvara, A.H. 737–39 (A.D. 1336–38).
- **Khande Rao Appaji** — general of Mahadji Scindia; brass face on Udaypur liṅga, V.S. 1832 (A.D. 1775).
- **M. B. Garde** — Patil's predecessor; *Archaeology in Gwalior*, main source for Part II.
- **Architectural terms** — garbhagṛha, sabhāmaṇḍapa, mukhamaṇḍapa, śikhara, toraṇa, dvārapāla, Vastuśāstra, kīrtimukha.
- **Comparanda to Udaypur** — Siddheśvara temple, Nemawar; Chaubara Dera & temples at Un (all Paramāra 11th–12th c.).
- **Monument totals** — 1,783 catalogued; ~750 religious; ~240 Śaiva, ~87 Vaiṣṇava, 89 Jain, ~113 mosques; Bhilsa district = richest in antiquity.
