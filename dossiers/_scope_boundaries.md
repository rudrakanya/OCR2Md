# Cross-chapter scope boundaries

DRAFT — for your review at Checkpoint 2. Not yet consumed by `gen_dossier.py`
in an automated way; each dossier's "Scope note" section currently just
quotes the relevant row below by hand. Edit freely — this is meant to be
argued with, not treated as settled.

Built from each chapter's `title_annotation` (your parenthetical scope
commentary) plus its existing `scope`/`keywords` in `chapters.yaml`, grouped
into the clusters where two or more chapters visibly compete for the same
evidence.

**Note on C05 and C08 appearing in two clusters each — confirmed intentional,
not a slip.** A chapter in a chronological or nested sequence has an
upstream and a downstream neighbor, which are two different boundary
relationships, not one duplicated: C05 hands off FROM C04's prehistory
(Cluster 3) and hands off TO C16's decline narrative (Cluster 4); C08 is
bounded by C06/C07's founding narrative on one side (Cluster 1) and by
C09/C10's iconography/inscriptions on the same building on the other
(Cluster 2). Each membership constrains a different neighbor.

## Cluster 1 — the temple's creation (C06 / C07 / C08)

The audit's own example. All three chapters will retrieve inscriptional
evidence mentioning Udayāditya, the temple, and the tank, because that
evidence is the load-bearing fact for all three narratives at once.

| Chapter | Owns | Explicitly excludes |
|---|---|---|
| **C06** The Paramāras and Bhoja *(How Udaypur was formed)* | Paramāra dynastic succession, court culture, Bhoja's reign and patronage of learning **as a political/dynastic narrative** — the world Udayāditya inherited and the pattern of royal temple-building he continued. | The specific founding act itself (date, the three-named-works formula, the Udaypur Prashasti's content) — that's C07's. Does not describe the temple's architecture. |
| **C07** Udayāditya's Foundation | The founding EVENT: who, when, why, and the inscriptional record of it (the Udaypur Prashasti's "Udayapura / Udayeśvara / Udayasamudra" naming, VS 1116/1059 CE start, VS 1137/1080 CE flag-staff). Narrative of the act of founding. | Does not analyze the temple's architectural style/form in technical depth (C08). Does not cover Bhoja-era or pre-Udayāditya dynastic politics (C06). |
| **C08** Udayeśvara in Stone *(Neelkantheshwar Temple Architecture)* | The building itself once founded: plan, courtyard, gateways, Bhūmija/Nāgara śikhara, architectural typology and style. Assumes the founding (C07) as settled fact, cited briefly if needed, not re-argued. | Does not retell the founding narrative. Does not cover sculpture/iconographic content on the walls (C09) or the inscriptions' epigraphic/paleographic study as texts (C10). |

## Cluster 2 — the temple's surface and words (C08 / C09 / C10)

| Chapter | Owns | Hands off to |
|---|---|---|
| **C08** | Structural/architectural form. | — |
| **C09** Gods on the Walls *(Deities and Symbolism)* | Sculptural and iconographic content: which deities, what attributes, what the imagery means. "What's carved and what it depicts," not "how the building is shaped." | C08 for structural context; C12 for the deeper ritual/philosophical meaning behind a depicted deity or symbol. |
| **C10** Words in Stone | Epigraphy as a discipline: script, paleography, the serpentine scimitar inscription, translation and dating cruxes. Owns the DEEP study of inscriptions as texts. | C06/C07 may cite an inscription's content briefly for a historical claim (e.g. a date) without re-doing C10's epigraphic analysis — C10 is where the inscription itself is the subject, not evidence for something else. |

## Cluster 4 — decline and modern custodianship (C05 / C16 / C17 / C18 / AW)

A chronological chain, not a real overlap risk if kept in order: ancient
political evolution → medieval decline → modern institutional protection →
present daily life → forward-looking challenges.

| Chapter | Period / register |
|---|---|
| C05 | Ancient–early medieval dynastic evolution. |
| C16 Conquests and Regimes | Post-Paramāra decline: invasions, Malwa Sultanate, medieval disruption. |
| C17 *(Udaypur in Independent India)* | Institutional/legal history: ASI, heritage law, INTACH surveys, post-Independence protection. |
| C18 Present Tense *(based on Jagta Hua Kasba)* | The town's present-day lived experience — local, oral, contemporary register. |
| AW Afterword | Forward-looking: present conservation struggles and what still needs doing — recommendation register, not narrative history. |
