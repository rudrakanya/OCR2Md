# Outline: C17 — Archaeologists, Laws and Repairs: Making Udaypur a "Monument"; Udaypur in Independent India

**Scope note:** Owns institutional/legal history of site protection: ASI, heritage law, INTACH surveys, post-Independence. Receives from C16. Also owns conservation status specifically for structures C11 catalogs by existence.

**Thesis:** Udaypur's transformation from a scattered ruin-field into a legally recognized "monument" was a slow, uneven institutional process — beginning with colonial-era antiquarian surveys, formalized through Indian heritage law and the ASI's protection regime, stalled by post-Independence neglect and bureaucratic failure, and only recently revived through INTACH's systematic documentation and conservation planning.

## Colonial Origins of Archaeological Attention
- British antiquarian Alexander Cunningham's tireless surveying of sites like Sanchi, Vidisha, Sarnath and Bharhut, and his petitioning of Viceroy Lord Canning, laid the institutional groundwork for what became the Archaeological Survey of India. **[THIN — single source]** (Udaypur: Jagta Hua Kasba (Udaypur, A Town Awake))
- Later local archaeologists such as Narayan Vyas continued this tradition of fieldwork and documentation in the region, connecting colonial-era exploration to modern heritage recognition (e.g. of Rani-ki-Vav and Sanchi). **[THIN — single source]** (Udaypur: Jagta Hua Kasba (Udaypur, A Town Awake))

## Legal and Institutional Framework for Protection
- Under the Ancient Monuments Preservation (Declaration of National Monuments) Act, Madhya Bharat's archaeology department listed 1,783 monuments, of which only 145 were declared of national importance, reflecting how selective and incomplete such legal 'monument' status was from the outset. **[THIN — single source]** (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 66-67)
- Regional gazetteer records catalog Udaypur among the historic places of Bhilsa district within a district-wide count of 142 monuments and 26 of national importance, situating Udaypur within this broader legal-administrative framework. **[THIN — single source]** (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 66-69)
- The village's Neelkanthéshwar Temple is specifically noted as an ASI-protected monument, marking its formal legal status as distinct from the many unprotected structures nearby. **[THIN — single source]** (Geo-Heritage of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh: The Third Eye, pp. 7-9)

## Post-Independence Neglect and Institutional Failure
- A polemical critique argues that Independent India's government failed to activate a coordinated Department of History, Culture and Archaeology from 1947 onward, leaving heritage sites to decay through official neglect. **[THIN — single source]** (Udaypur: Jagta Hua Kasba (Udaypur, A Town Awake))
- A 2013 CAG performance audit found that the ASI lacked a reliable database of protected monuments, had never conducted a comprehensive review of which sites merited national-importance status, and had no updated Conservation Policy, leading to arbitrary and inconsistent conservation decisions nationwide. (Singh, Sandeep, Temple Economics (Arthavyavasthā of Mandirs), Volume I, pp. 161-162; Singh, Sandeep, Temple Economics (Arthavyavasthā of Mandirs), Volume I, pp. 161-163; Singh, Sandeep, Temple Economics (Arthavyavasthā of Mandirs), Volume I, pp. 161-164)
- Critics allege that ASI custodianship of temples can function as a mechanism of neglect and alienation from worshippers, citing examples such as the Lingaraj Temple cracks and the dilapidated Devuni Gutta temple, as illustrations of a broader pattern said to also affect Hindu religious sites under ASI control. (Singh, Sandeep, Temple Economics (Arthavyavasthā of Mandirs), Volume I, pp. 161-162; Singh, Sandeep, Temple Economics (Arthavyavasthā of Mandirs), Volume I, p. 161; Singh, Sandeep, Temple Economics (Arthavyavasthā of Mandirs), Volume I, pp. 166-167)

## INTACH's Modern Documentation Effort
- INTACH's Bhopal chapter, led by convener Madan Mohan Upadhyay, undertook the first comprehensive field survey of Udaypur, identifying roughly 50 heritage sites and over 100 inscriptions across 42 monuments never previously catalogued. (INTACH, Heritage Documentation of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh, pp. 1-5; Udaypur: Jagta Hua Kasba (Udaypur, A Town Awake); Geo-Heritage of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh: The Third Eye, p. 18)
- This documentation effort produced multiple publications, including 'Udaypur: The Architectural Splendor' and the 'Geo-Heritage of Udaypur' study, using a methodology combining literature review, direct site observation, drone photography, and interviews with local residents. (Geo-Heritage of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh: The Third Eye, p. 45; INTACH, Heritage Documentation of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh, pp. 6-11)
- INTACH's survey extended beyond built heritage into the site's geo-heritage — the hill fortification wall, caves, and rock formations — treating natural and archaeological features together for the first time. (Geo-Heritage of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh: The Third Eye, pp. 153-155; Geo-Heritage of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh: The Third Eye, pp. 1-4)

## Conservation Status and Physical Condition
- The fortification wall's condition has been documented in detail: bastions, posterns, loopholes and eyelets remain intact in places, but illegal stone quarrying and lichen growth have accelerated deterioration of the wall over the past decade. (Geo-Heritage of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh: The Third Eye, pp. 90-98; Geo-Heritage of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh: The Third Eye, pp. 99-102)
- Water seepage and dampness pose an acute structural threat to the fortification wall, which was originally built using dry construction unsuited to modern moisture conditions, and INTACH recommends borings to assess bedrock water penetration alongside minimally invasive stabilization. **[THIN — single source]** (Geo-Heritage of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh: The Third Eye, pp. 103-106)
- Detailed condition assessment of the site notes collapsed structures, temple fragments, and later lime-mortar/cement-plaster repairs along the hill pathway, indicating a long history of ad hoc maintenance predating systematic conservation. **[THIN — single source]** (Geo-Heritage of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh: The Third Eye, pp. 108-111)
- A survey table grading individual temples (e.g. Nav Durga Mandir, Tulsi Mahadev Mandir) records most as 'Unprotected' with 'Poor' to 'Moderate' condition, showing that formal protected status has not been extended to most structures despite their age and continued use. **[THIN — single source]** (Geo-Heritage of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh: The Third Eye, pp. 153-169)

## Toward a Coordinated Conservation Future
- INTACH's proposed geo-heritage park would require coordination among multiple agencies — the Forest Department, the Madhya Pradesh Eco-Tourism Development Board, the district collector, and the local panchayat — with INTACH itself piloting conceptualization and implementation. **[THIN — single source]** (Geo-Heritage of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh: The Third Eye, pp. 129-143)
- Recommendations include improved approach roads, signage, an interpretation board, and use of the thousand-year-old hillside gateway as the entrance to a planned Geo Heritage Reserve, aimed at balancing tourism development with preservation. **[THIN — single source]** (Geo-Heritage of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh: The Third Eye, pp. 150-152)
- Lack of awareness among local and national authorities about geo-heritage conservation is identified as a structural barrier, requiring sustained sensitization of panchayats and municipal bodies not accustomed to including such conservation in their mandates. **[THIN — single source]** (Geo-Heritage of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh: The Third Eye, pp. 146-147)
