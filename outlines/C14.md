## Caste Hierarchy and Landed Society
- Brahmins made up about 7-8 per cent of the population, divided into sub-groups, while Rajputs and Thakurs, drawn from families such as Tornars, Bhadorias, Kacchawahas, Jadhos, Shikaras, Khichis, Chandellas, Bundelas, Ponwars, Gehlots, Chauhans, Shisodias and Solankis, historically dominated political life and remained the landed gentry. **[THIN — single source]** (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 21-25)
- Gujars, Jats and Ahirs in the north claimed status level with the Rajputs and achieved social eminence for various historical reasons. **[THIN — single source]** (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 21-25)
- Census data for Vidisha District in 1941 recorded the leading castes as Teli, Chamar, Rajput, Kachhi, Brahman, Lodhi, Ahir, Koli, Kurmi, Mina, Rawat, Nai, Kirar, Saharia, Kumbhar, Kayastha, Dhobi and Luhar. **[THIN — single source]** (Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, pp. 76-77)

## Scheduled Castes: Composition and Livelihoods
- Scheduled Castes formed about 20.92 per cent of Vidisha District's population in 1961, rising to roughly 23 per cent by 1971, with a formally enumerated list including Bagri or Bagdi, Balai, Chamar and many others. **[THIN — single source]** (Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, pp. 76-77)
- Chamars, the largest Scheduled Caste group with 76,177 persons, were mainly cultivators and agricultural labourers in rural areas but showed greater occupational diversity and higher literacy in towns. **[THIN — single source]** (Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, pp. 78-79)
- Koris and Chidars, along with Barhar or Basod, formed smaller but significant shares of the Scheduled Caste population, mostly working as cultivators or agricultural labourers with modest and uneven literacy rates. **[THIN — single source]** (Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, pp. 78-79)

## Aboriginal Tribes and Scheduled Tribes
- The aboriginal tribes of the Vindhyas and Satpuras, including the Bhilalas, Bhils, Korkus, Mundas, Gonds and Meenas, did not form a single population group and differed widely from each other and from Hindus in customs, religion and racial origin. (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 21-23; Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 21-24)
- The Bhils were physically distinct-short-statured, dark-complexioned, broad-nosed and curly-haired-and had lost any original dialect, now speaking a mixed Gujarati-Malwi tongue with Marathi and Munda borrowings. **[THIN — single source]** (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 21-24)
- The Gonds once held political sway around Bhilsa and Bhopal, a region once called Gond Wada, while the Meenas occupied the north-western hill tracts along the Chambal in Shivapuri District. **[THIN — single source]** (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 21-24)
- Scheduled Tribes formed about 4.34 per cent of Vidisha District's population in 1961, rising to about 5.4 per cent in 1971, concentrated almost entirely in rural areas and overwhelmingly Hindu by religion. **[THIN — single source]** (Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, p. 79)
- Saharias, comprising about 86 per cent of Vidisha's Scheduled Tribe population, lived in appalling economic conditions, subsisted on lesser millets, and were frequently bound into virtual servitude through the Hari Baholi and Hali Banta labour systems. **[THIN — single source]** (Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, p. 79)

## Languages and Dialects of the Plateau
- The region belonged overwhelmingly to the Indo-Aryan language family, classified under Grierson's linguistic survey as falling within the Central Group of Western Hindi and Rajasthan, with only traces of Munda or Dravidian words surviving in southern aboriginal dialects. **[THIN — single source]** (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 21-25)
- Northern Madhya Bharat fell within the Western Hindi sphere, featuring the dialects of Brij-Bhasha in the north and Bundeli in the central northern parts. **[THIN — single source]** (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 21-25)
- In Vidisha District, Hindi was the mother tongue of over 90 per cent of the population in 1961, with Urdu the largest minority tongue, especially in Kurwai, Sironj and Lateri tahsils, alongside small numbers speaking Marathi, Marwari and Sindhi. **[THIN — single source]** (Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, pp. 73-76)

## Everyday Customs and Religious Minorities
- Common village customs cut across communities, such as dressing young boys as girls to ward off the evil eye and applying kajal to children's eyes and foreheads for protection. **[THIN — single source]** (Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, pp. 80-81)
- Korkus traditionally buried their dead facing south and raised memorials called gatha, observing the Pitarmiloni and Sidoli rites to settle the soul of the deceased among the ancestors. **[THIN — single source]** (Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, pp. 80-81)
- Gonds also traditionally practiced burial, including burying children beneath a Mahua tree, though acculturation with caste Hindus was leading some toward cremation. **[THIN — single source]** (Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, pp. 80-81)
- Muslim and Jain minorities were culturally close to their Hindu neighbours, with Jains-just over one per cent of the population-playing an outsized role in industry, trade and commerce, and a prosperous Bohra community concentrated around Ujjain. **[THIN — single source]** (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 21-23)
