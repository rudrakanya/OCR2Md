# Outline: C06 — The Paramāras and Bhoja: Kingship, Learning and Temple Building

**Scope note:** Owns Paramara dynastic succession, court culture, and Bhoja's reign as a POLITICAL/DYNASTIC narrative -- the world Udayaditya inherited. Does NOT own the specific founding act (C07's) or the temple's architecture (C08's).

**Thesis:** The Paramāra dynasty rose from Rāṣṭrakūṭa feudatory origins to build, under Muñja and above all Bhoja, an empire that fused military ambition with unmatched royal patronage of learning and temple-building, before a cycle of succession crises and external invasions eroded that inheritance and set the stage for Udayāditya's restoration.

## Origins: From Rāṣṭrakūṭa Feudatories to Independent Kings
- The Paramāras began as a subordinate branch installed by the Rāṣṭrakūṭa Govinda III to govern Mālava, with Upendra as the first ruler of the line, probably enthroned in the early ninth century. (Ganguly, D. C., History of the Paramāra Dynasty, pp. 15-34; Ganguly, D. C., History of the Paramāra Dynasty, pp. 15-32)
- The Ā'in-i-Akbarī and later tradition trace the founder's arrival from the Deccan, establishing the family's sovereignty in Mālava. **[THIN — single source]** (Ganguly, D. C., History of the Paramāra Dynasty, pp. 15-25)
- Siyaka II broke decisively from Rāṣṭrakūṭa overlordship, defeating Khoṭṭiga and sacking Mānyakheta in 971 A.D., assuming the imperial title Mahārājādhirājapati and becoming the true founder of an independent Paramāra kingdom. (Some Paramāra Temples, pp. 8-9; Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 34-35)

## Vākpati-Muñja and Sindhurāja: Consolidation and Its Limits
- Vākpati-Muñja extended Paramāra power through victories over the Guhilas, Cāhamānas, Gurjaras and Caulukyas, while also patronizing poets such as Dhanika and Padmagupta, but ultimately lost his life fighting the Cālukya Tailapa. (Some Paramāra Temples, pp. 8-9; Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 34-35; Ganguly, D. C., History of the Paramāra Dynasty, pp. 59-63)
- Sindhurāja, Muñja's brother and successor, extended Paramāra influence toward Berar but suffered a serious military reverse against Cāmuṇḍarāja of the Caulukyas that damaged Paramāra prestige in the north. (Some Paramāra Temples, pp. 8-9; Ganguly, D. C., History of the Paramāra Dynasty, pp. 93-95)

## Bhoja's Accession and the Legend of a King Marked for Greatness
- Later tradition, preserved by Merutuṅga and echoed in the Ā'in-i-Akbarī, tells that a horoscope cast at Bhoja's birth predicted a fifty-five-year reign over Dakṣiṇāpatha and Gauḍa, prompting his father Vākpati (Muñja) to order his execution, an order rescinded when Bhoja's composure and verse moved his would-be executioners and, through them, the king. **[THIN — single source]** (Ganguly, D. C., History of the Paramāra Dynasty, pp. 96-97)
- Bhoja's reign is conventionally dated from around 1000 to 1055 A.D., succeeding Sindhurāja as the greatest monarch of the dynasty. (Some Paramāra Temples, pp. 8-9; Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 34-35)

## Bhoja's Political and Military Reign
- Epigraphic and literary evidence, including Bhoja's conquest of Konkan (1019-20) and Al Bīrūnī's report that Bhilsa marked the eastern limit of Malwa in 1030, allow reconstruction of a Paramāra dominion stretching from Banswara and Dungarpur in the north to the Godāvari, Khāndesh and Konkan in the south. **[THIN — single source]** (Ganguly, D. C., History of the Paramāra Dynasty, pp. 96-103)
- Bhoja rebuilt Dhārā as a new capital, transferring the seat of government there from Ujjain, which had been the chief Paramāra city since Siyaka II's accession. **[THIN — single source]** (Ganguly, D. C., History of the Paramāra Dynasty, pp. 96-103)
- Bhoja fought a protracted war with the Caulukyas of Gujarat under Bhīma I, including the general Kulacandra's sack of Anhilwar while Bhīma campaigned in Sind, an episode so notorious it entered proverb. **[THIN — single source]** (Ganguly, D. C., History of the Paramāra Dynasty, pp. 128-131)
- Court poetic tradition, echoed for centuries afterward, cast Bhoja as a king before whom rival monarchs of Chola, Andhra, Karnataka, Gurjara, Chedi, Kanyakubja, Konkan, Lāta, Kalinga and Kośala all cowered, reflecting the political mythology built around his martial reputation. **[THIN — single source]** (Raja Bhoj aur Parmarkālīn Malwa (Raja Bhoja and Paramāra-period Malwa), pp. 1-8)

## Bhoja the Scholar-King and His Court
- Bhoja was renowned as a patron and author across an extraordinary range of subjects — poetics, Ayurveda, astrology, statecraft, and Śaiva philosophy — with works such as the Sarasvatī-kaṇṭhābharaṇa, Śṛṅgāra-prakāśa, Yukti-kalpataru, Rāja-mārtaṇḍa, and Tattva-prakāśa attributed to him, and commentaries beginning within a century of his death. (Raja Bhoj aur Parmarkālīn Malwa (Raja Bhoja and Paramāra-period Malwa), pp. 143-145; Raja Bhoj aur Parmarkālīn Malwa (Raja Bhoja and Paramāra-period Malwa), pp. 1-13; Raja Bhoj aur Parmarkālīn Malwa (Raja Bhoja and Paramāra-period Malwa), pp. 1-14)
- **[→ see C09]** Note: the content and authorship claims of Bhoja's individual treatises belong to the chapter on Bhoja's intellectual works, not to this dynastic/political chapter.
- Bhoja built halls of learning at Dhārā and Ujjayinī, called Śāradā-sadana and Bhāratī-bhavana or Sarasvatī-kaṇṭhābharaṇa, where poetry and grammar were inscribed on slabs and pillars and dramatic performances were staged, and he supported poets such as Ubbata, Dhanapāla, Chhittapa, Amitagati, Keśava and Nichula. **[THIN — single source]** (Raja Bhoj aur Parmarkālīn Malwa (Raja Bhoja and Paramāra-period Malwa), pp. 1-9)
- Educational institutions flourished in Malwa under the Paramāras, exemplified by the building now known as the Kamal Maula mosque at Dhārā, believed to have been a school erected under Bhoja and bearing carved alphabets and grammar rules. **[THIN — single source]** (Ganguly, D. C., History of the Paramāra Dynasty, pp. 285-292)
- Bhoja's reputation as patron of learning became proverbial well beyond his lifetime, invoked in Rajasthani sayings, the Vijayanagara court's praise of Krishnaraya, and the description of the later Paramāra Bhoja II as 'Bhojo Bhoja ivaparah'. **[THIN — single source]** (Raja Bhoj aur Parmarkālīn Malwa (Raja Bhoja and Paramāra-period Malwa), pp. 154-165)

## After Bhoja: Succession, Fragmentation and the Coming Crisis
- Later Paramāra kings, including Bhoja II, continued the dynasty's tradition of poetic patronage even as political power waned. **[THIN — single source]** (Ganguly, D. C., History of the Paramāra Dynasty, pp. 285-292)
- Following Bhoja, the dynasty faced instability: Jayasimha I was defeated and killed fighting the Cālukyas and Caulukyas, and Malwa itself was conquered and annexed by the Caulukya Kumārapāla, plunging the imperial line into obscurity for a generation. (Ganguly, D. C., History of the Paramāra Dynasty, pp. 10-14; Ganguly, D. C., History of the Paramāra Dynasty, pp. 186-188)
- This dynastic crisis — the loss of the throne of Dhārā itself — forms the immediate political background against which Udayāditya's reconquest and re-establishment of Paramāra sovereignty must be understood. **[THIN — single source]** (Ganguly, D. C., History of the Paramāra Dynasty, pp. 10-14)
- **[→ see C07]** The precise circumstances and chronology of Udayāditya's own reconquest and reassertion of Paramāra rule are treated as a distinct founding act in the following chapter.
