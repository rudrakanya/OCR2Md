# Outline: C07 — Udayāditya's Foundation: Town, Temple and Tank

**Scope note:** Owns the founding EVENT itself: who, when, why, and the inscriptional record of it (the Udaypur Prashasti's naming and dates). Does not analyze the temple's architectural style in technical depth (C08's) or re-litigate dynastic politics (C06's).

**Thesis:** Udayāditya's founding of Udayapura, its Udayeshvara temple, and the Udayasamudra tank was a single, deliberate act of royal self-naming and consolidation, undertaken as he restored the shattered Paramāra kingdom after Bhoja's death, and the inscriptional record left on the temple itself remains our primary evidence for when, why, and by whom this triple foundation was made.

## The context: a kingdom in crisis and a new king
- Udayāditya inherited the Paramāra throne of Malwa amid severe adverse circumstances following the reverses of Bhoja's later reign, and his first task was restoring peace and order to the kingdom. (Ganguly, D. C., History of the Paramāra Dynasty, pp. 144-152; Ganguly, D. C., History of the Paramāra Dynasty, pp. 144-154)
- **[→ see C06]** Udayāditya's precise dynastic relationship to Bhoja and his path to power is a matter of ongoing scholarly debate that belongs to this chapter only insofar as it explains the moment of the foundation; the fuller dynastic politics are treated elsewhere.

## The founding act: town, temple, and tank named for the king
- An inscription on the Udayeshvara temple records that the Paramāra king Udayaditya of Malwa founded a town, built a temple of Shiva, and excavated a tank, naming all three after himself: Udayapura, Udayeshvara, and Udayasamudra. **[THIN — single source]** (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 114-115)
- The ruins of the tank Udayasamudra survive a short distance to the north-east of the town, and the temple referred to in the inscription is the still-standing Udayeshvara monument. **[THIN — single source]** (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 114-115)
- The Udayapur prasasti itself, engraved on a stone slab now lying before the temple, is the chapter's central primary document, furnishing much of what is known of Udayāditya's reign and the founding. **[THIN — single source]** (Ganguly, D. C., History of the Paramāra Dynasty, pp. 144-150)

## Dating the foundation from the temple's own inscriptions
- Two further inscriptions on the temple indicate that construction began in V.S. 1116 (A.D. 1059) and that the flagstaff was erected in V.S. 1137 (A.D. 1080), giving a working span for the temple's building. **[THIN — single source]** (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 114-115)
- A separate Udaipur temple inscription bearing the date Vikram Samvat 1116 / Shak Samvat 981 (1059 CE) is a long, error-ridden Sanskrit verse text that nonetheless casts light on the relationship between Bhoj and Udayaditya, describing Udayaditya's lineage back through Gandal (Gondal) and Pata (Jnata). **[THIN — single source]** (Udaypur: Jagta Hua Kasba (Udaypur, A Town Awake))
- Some scholars caution that the 1059 A.D. date may reflect Udayāditya ruling only his paternal territory from Udayapur, before he later assumed full sovereignty over Malwa, since other records place Jayasimha as ruling Malwa at that time. **[THIN — single source]** (Ganguly, D. C., History of the Paramāra Dynasty, pp. 144-146)

## Why here: the absence of earlier Paramāra inscriptions at the site
- No inscription of any earlier Paramāra king has been found at Udaipur, indicating the town itself came into existence only in Udayāditya's time, and its very name commemorates him. **[THIN — single source]** (Udaypur: Jagta Hua Kasba (Udaypur, A Town Awake))
- **[→ see C06]** A separate, badly worn inscription describes some of Udayāditya's military victories, including the defeat and killing of the Dahaladhish (the Chedi ruler of Dahalamandala), material that belongs to the chapter on dynastic and military politics rather than to the founding narrative itself.

## The prasasti as document: authorship and formal record of dedication
- The prasasti of Udayāditya recording the construction and consecration of the Nilakantheshvara temple was composed by Pandita Mahipala, son of Pandita Sringavasa, and follows the customary form of dated royal foundation inscriptions, opening with 'svasti' and closing with 'mangalam, mahasri'. **[THIN — single source]** (Some Paramāra Temples, pp. 44-45)
- A comparable Paramara-era foundation inscription at Paratheda, dated the same year V.S. 1116 (A.D. 1059), shows this was the broader convention for recording temple foundations, endowments, and builders in the period. **[THIN — single source]** (Some Paramāra Temples, pp. 44-45)
- A fragmentary land-donation inscription inside the temple, mentioning a gift of 4 biswa of land and the institution of the panchakul, shows that the temple's foundation was accompanied by ongoing administrative and endowment activity, though its precise purpose remains uncertain. (Udaypur: Jagta Hua Kasba (Udaypur, A Town Awake); Udaypur: Jagta Hua Kasba (Udaypur, A Town Awake))

## The tank and town within the wider landscape of Udayapura
- The temple was built on the highest point of a mound overlooking the settlement, with the tank and the flow of water shaping the layout of the town below it, consistent with the historic relationship between water bodies and settlement in the Udaypur region. **[THIN — single source]** (Geo-Heritage of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh: The Third Eye, pp. 73-74)
- Later inscriptions from the successor Naravarman's reign record the construction of another tank near Udayapur in V.S. 1151 (1094 A.D.), showing that hydraulic works remained an ongoing concern at the site after Udayāditya, though this is a subsequent development rather than part of the founding act itself. **[THIN — single source]** (Ganguly, D. C., History of the Paramāra Dynasty, pp. 168-173)
