# Outline: C01 — Udaypur: A Small Town with a Long Memory

**Scope note:** Owns the town's own textual/scriptural memory -- Puranic mentions, place-name etymology. Written/literary tradition specifically about this place. Hands off to C15 for oral/folk (non-scriptural) tradition about the town.

**Thesis:** Udaypur's own literary memory begins not in myth but in stone: an eleventh-century Sanskrit inscription on the Udayeshvara temple names the town, its temple and its lake after King Udayaditya, anchoring the settlement's identity in a datable act of royal textual self-commemoration rather than in the deep Puranic antiquity claimed by larger neighbors like Vidisha.

## The Foundational Inscription: Udayaditya's Self-Naming Act
- An old Sanskrit inscription on the Udayeshvara temple records that the Paramara king Udayaditya of Malwa founded a town, built a temple of Shiva, and excavated a tank, naming all three after himself as Udayapura, Udayeshvara and Udayasamudra. **[THIN — single source]** (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 114-115)
- Two further inscriptions on the same temple date construction to between V.S. 1116 (A.D. 1059) and the erection of the flagstaff in V.S. 1137 (A.D. 1080), giving the town's textual self-record a firm chronological frame. **[THIN — single source]** (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 114-115)
- A separate stone inscription, the Udaypur Prashasti, now kept in the Gwalior Museum in two parts, independently confirms 1059 CE as the year in which the town, the Udayeshwar temple, and Udaysagar Lake were all founded together. **[THIN — single source]** (Geo-Heritage of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh: The Third Eye, pp. 74-75)

## Udaypur in the Wider Epigraphic Record of the Paramaras
- Later Paramara inscriptions such as the Shergadh stone inscription of Udayadityadeva situate Udayaditya's reign and conquests within a documented dynastic and geographic framework, of which Udaypur was one royal foundation among several place-names recorded in stone across Malwa. (Trivedi, Harihar Vithal (ed.), Corpus Inscriptionum Indicarum, Vol. VII, Part 2: Inscriptions of the Paramāras, pp. 125-131; Trivedi, Harihar Vithal (ed.), Corpus Inscriptionum Indicarum, Vol. VII, Part 2: Inscriptions of the Paramāras, pp. 125-132)
- The scholarly corpus of Paramara inscriptions (Corpus Inscriptionum Indicarum) treats the 'Udaipur prasasti' as a recognized textual source used by historians to identify Udayaditya's title as 'Dahaladhisa' and to reconstruct his campaigns, showing that Udaypur's own inscription was read and cross-referenced by later epigraphists as part of the region's written record. **[THIN — single source]** (Trivedi, Harihar Vithal (ed.), Corpus Inscriptionum Indicarum, Vol. VII, Part 2: Inscriptions of the Paramāras, pp. 175-179)

## Puranic and Epic Memory of the Surrounding Region
- The Ramayana states that Shatrughna's son Shatrughati (or Subahu, per the Raghuvamsam) was placed in charge of Vidisha, the larger region within which Udaypur later arose, giving the district an epic-era textual pedigree that predates any specific mention of Udaypur itself. (Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, pp. 13-14; Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, pp. 33-34)
- The Skanda Purana names Vidisha as a tirth to be visited after Someshwar, and the Brihata Samhita likewise mentions Vidisha, showing that the region's sanctity was recorded in Puranic and Sanskrit scientific literature centuries before Udaypur's own founding inscription. (Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, pp. 13-14; Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, pp. 253-254)
- The Skanda-Purana's Avantya-khanda catalogues eighty-four Shiva lingas of the Mahakalavana/Avanti sacred landscape, with legends explaining each shrine's name and history, illustrating the genre of scriptural place-naming into which Udaypur's own temple-naming inscription implicitly participates, though no passage in this khanda names Udaypur, Udayeshvara, or Udayasamudra specifically. (Tagare, G. V. (trans.), The Skanda-Purāṇa, Part XIII: Āvantya-khaṇḍa, pp. 1-7; Tagare, G. V. (trans.), The Skanda-Purāṇa, Part XIII: Āvantya-khaṇḍa, pp. 7-8)
- **[GAP — UNSUPPORTED]** No source in the evidence establishes a direct Puranic (Skanda-Purana or other classical scripture) verse that names Udaypur, Udayeshvara, or Udayasamudra by name; the town's scriptural memory rests on its own dynastic inscription rather than on inclusion in an older Purana.

## Place-Name Etymology as Textual Practice
- Neighboring Vidisha's own name-histories, such as its transformation from Besnagar to Bhailaswamin/Bhillaswamin and then Bhilsa, and its many literary names (Vessanagar, Vaisyanagar, Vaidisa, Visvanagar), demonstrate the regional pattern by which towns acquired successive Sanskrit and vernacular names recorded in inscriptions and literature -- the same pattern by which Udaypur derives its name directly from Udayaditya via the temple inscription rather than through gradual toponymic drift. (Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, pp. 13-14; Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, pp. 33-34)
- **[→ see C15]** Local tradition recorded in modern heritage surveys asserts the town was earlier called Bhrangarajpur before Udayaditya's conquest and renaming, but this account is presented as oral/local legend rather than as a scriptural or inscriptional source, and its detailed treatment belongs to the chapter on oral tradition.

## The Limits of Udaypur's Textual Memory
- Unlike Vidisha, which can claim mention across Ramayana, Mahabharata, Skanda Purana, Bana's Kadambari, and Kalidasa's Raghuvamsam, Udaypur's entire scriptural/textual memory rests on a single class of source -- the temple and prashasti inscriptions of Udayaditya -- underscoring that the town's 'long memory' is inscriptional rather than Puranic in the classical sense. (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 114-115; Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, pp. 33-34; Geo-Heritage of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh: The Third Eye, pp. 74-75)
- Modern secondary compilations (district gazetteers, geo-heritage surveys) repeat and rely on the same eleventh-century inscriptional evidence rather than adding any newly discovered ancient scriptural reference to Udaypur by name, confirming that the written record about this specific place remains anchored to its Paramara-era founding text. (Geo-Heritage of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh: The Third Eye, pp. 7-9; Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, pp. 338-346)
