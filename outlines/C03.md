## The Sandstone Tableland: Mesas, Buttes, and Bedding
- Udaypur sits on a sandstone tableland whose caprock-over-weaker-rock structure produces a classic sequence of escarpment retreat, residual mesas, buttes, and eventually isolated pinnacles or boulder scatters. **[THIN — single source]** (Geo-Heritage of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh: The Third Eye, pp. 27-30)
- The specific pathway of mesa decay at Udaypur depends on lithology and structure — caprock thickness, jointing patterns, and weaker intercalations — meaning the hill's forms are not uniform but reflect local rock character. **[THIN — single source]** (Geo-Heritage of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh: The Third Eye, pp. 27-30)
- Sedimentary structures such as planar and cross bedding, visible throughout the hill, preserve evidence of the ancient depositional environment and help identify the stratigraphic order of the rock. **[THIN — single source]** (Geo-Heritage of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh: The Third Eye, pp. 129-133)

## Forest Cover and Vegetation of the District
- The teak forests of the district vary in quality and density depending on soil richness and slope, ranging from good stands in valleys to open, stunted growth on exposed, heavily grazed ground. **[THIN — single source]** (Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, pp. 25-27)
- Forest area figures for the district shifted considerably across the mid-20th century, though much of the apparent increase reflects reclassification of land rather than genuine forest growth. **[THIN — single source]** (Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, pp. 92-93)

## Fauna of the Hill and District
- The hill supports monkeys, shrews, squirrels, and other species adapted to its mixed forest and scrubland habitats, each with distinct feeding habits documented in the survey. **[THIN — single source]** (Geo-Heritage of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh: The Third Eye, pp. 38-43)
- The hill's caves, protected by their inaccessibility and steep terrain, host large bat colonies whose guano deposits and associated snake predation form a self-contained cave ecosystem. **[THIN — single source]** (Geo-Heritage of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh: The Third Eye, p. 37)
