# Outline: C04 — Before Udaypur: Prehistory and Early Historic Vidisha

**Scope note:** Owns material, archaeological prehistory of the broader Vidisha region (palaeolithic/chalcolithic record, Besnagar) -- evidence BEFORE and INDEPENDENT of any textual tradition. Hands off to C05 once organized dynastic polities appear.

**Thesis:** Long before Udaypur or any organized dynastic polity existed, the Vidisha region had already accumulated a deep archaeological record -- from palaeolithic tool scatters through chalcolithic settlement to the early-historic urban and religious center of Besnagar -- laying the physical and locational foundations on which later textual and dynastic history would be built.

## The Deep Prehistoric Record of the Vidisha Region
- Palaeolithic tools -- choppers, handaxes, cleavers, scrapers, bifaces, and discs -- found in the valleys of the Betwa, Bina, Dhasan and Keothan rivers attest to a palaeolithic culture across the district. **[THIN — single source]** (Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, p. 33)
- Chalcolithic material similar to that of Eran and Nagda, including black-and-red and red wares dated roughly 1000-700 B.C. along with microlithic blades of crystalline quartz, was recovered from an ancient mound in the district, with painted pottery just above signaling later Aryan influence. **[THIN — single source]** (Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, p. 33)

## Geomorphology and the Landscape Base of Settlement
- The Udaypur hill area itself is a sandstone tableland whose mesa-butte-pinnacle landform evolution, shaped by caprock thickness, jointing, and differential erosion, provided the physical terrain later settlements would occupy. (Geo-Heritage of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh: The Third Eye, pp. 30-31; Geo-Heritage of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh: The Third Eye, pp. 27-30)
- Across the broader region, settlement organization consistently paired a habitation mound (tekro) with adjacent water bodies (talav/talavadi), a pattern whose origins predate any specific textual or dynastic identification and instead reflect basic ecological adaptation to a semi-arid landscape. **[THIN — single source]** (Geo-Heritage of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh: The Third Eye, pp. 73-74)

## Besnagar Before Textual History: Stratigraphy and Early Habitation
- Excavations at Besnagar revealed multiple habitation strata, with the earliest traces -- charcoal, ash, and tile layers along with a handful of burnt-earthenware ring wells -- belonging to the Maurya period, beneath later Sunga and Andhra stone-and-brick strata. **[THIN — single source]** (Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, pp. 323-333)
- A 1963-65 excavation at the confluence of the Bes and Betwa rivers exposed ruins spanning six periods of habitation, confirming a long, continuous occupation sequence at the site independent of any single dynasty's records. **[THIN — single source]** (Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, pp. 323-333)
- Systematic exploration by the Archaeological Survey of India's Central Circle during 1963-65 established a regular cultural sequence at Vidisha running from the Chalcolithic through the early historical period into the early medieval era. **[THIN — single source]** (Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, pp. 323-334)
- Minor antiquities recovered from the site -- 58 objects including terracotta figurines, pottery, stone and metal items, beads and amulets -- along with 66 coins (56 of them Karshapanas of the Western Kshatrapa and Naga period) from the Kham Baba and Besnagar sites, document the material culture of the settlement's successive occupants. **[THIN — single source]** (Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, pp. 323-333)

## Besnagar's Emergence as an Early Historic Urban and Sacred Center
- The site of Bes/Besnagar is generally identified as the ancient city of Wessanagara (Vaishyanagari) or Vidisha, said to have covered roughly two kilometers by one kilometer with suburbs extending its total circuit to about eight kilometers. **[THIN — single source]** (Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, pp. 323-326)
- Excavated railings surrounding the Kham Baba mound and column, datable to 165 B.C. and 140 B.C. respectively, together with a mound believed to mark the site of a Vasudeva temple, show Besnagar functioning as an organized religious center in the pre- and early-Shunga period. **[THIN — single source]** (Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, pp. 323-331)
- Cunningham's 19th-century excavations recovered a coping-stone, railing pillar, and rail bars inscribed in characters of the Ashokan age (3rd century B.C.), decorated with processions of elephants, horsemen, and stupas -- physical evidence of Buddhist activity at the site independent of any later textual tradition. **[THIN — single source]** (Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, pp. 323-327)
- Later digging at the site also yielded sacrificial pits (kundas) alongside coins of the Mahakshatrapa Ishvaradatta and of Naga kings, dating this phase of ritual activity to the third or early fourth century A.D. **[THIN — single source]** (Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, pp. 323-332)

## Toward the Textual and Dynastic Record
- Positive archaeological evidence for the art of sculpture in the wider Malwa/Madhya Bharat region is traceable only from the 3rd century B.C. onward, with Besnagar yielding some of the earliest known free-standing colossal statues, whose dating remains debated between pre-Mauryan and Shunga times. **[THIN — single source]** (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 51-52)
