**Thesis:** Udaypur's fort and heritage landscape face ongoing physical decay driven by illegal quarrying, water damage, and neglect, mirroring a national pattern of institutional apathy toward temple and monument conservation; reversing this requires coordinated, locally-rooted action among government agencies, INTACH, and the villagers themselves.

## The Present State of Decay
- Decades of continuous sandstone mining, illegal quarrying along the walls, and natural weathering from lichen have dramatically accelerated deterioration of the fortification wall over the past decade. **[THIN — single source]** (Geo-Heritage of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh: The Third Eye, pp. 99-102)
- Water seepage and dampness remain acute problems because the fortification was originally built with dry construction methods unsuited to modern water infiltration, inducing bulges and structural loss. **[THIN — single source]** (Geo-Heritage of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh: The Third Eye, pp. 103-106)
- Even after repair work, new cracks have reappeared, such as at the southwest corner of the wall, showing that underlying causes rather than symptoms must be addressed. **[THIN — single source]** (Geo-Heritage of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh: The Third Eye, pp. 103-106)

## A National Pattern of Institutional Neglect
- Udaypur's struggles echo a broader national pattern: a CAG performance audit found the ASI lacked a reliable database of protected monuments and had never comprehensively surveyed which sites merited national importance. **[THIN — single source]** (Singh, Sandeep, Temple Economics (Arthavyavasthā of Mandirs), Volume I, pp. 161-162)
- Cases like the Jain shrine ruins in Wayanad and cracks left unrepaired at the Lingaraj mandir in Odisha show how apathy and bureaucratic inertia at the ASI level allow heritage sites to decay even when local communities raise alarms. (Singh, Sandeep, Temple Economics (Arthavyavasthā of Mandirs), Volume I, p. 161; Singh, Sandeep, Temple Economics (Arthavyavasthā of Mandirs), Volume I, pp. 161-162)

## Immediate Conservation Priorities
- INTACH's assessment stresses general stability as the primary objective, arguing preventative measures are far cheaper than rebuilding after failure and that minimal intervention with maximum public safety should guide all work. **[THIN — single source]** (Geo-Heritage of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh: The Third Eye, pp. 103-106)
- Applicable conservation principles for the fortification call for documentation preserving stratigraphic and structural layers, understanding the site's original operational function, and developing interpretation that connects visitors and locals to its historic and territorial significance. **[THIN — single source]** (Geo-Heritage of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh: The Third Eye, pp. 83-86)
- INTACH's broader documentation project has organized fieldwork data into a typology of structures with unique IDs, laying groundwork for site-specific conservation recommendations, though further work remains to complete this compilation. **[THIN — single source]** (INTACH, Heritage Documentation of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh, pp. 6-11)

## Stakeholders and Coordination
- Effective conservation requires coordination among multiple agencies: the Forest Department, the Madhya Pradesh Eco-Tourism Development Board, the district administration under the collector, the local panchayat, and INTACH's Bhopal chapter, with the District Collector proposed as overall team leader. **[THIN — single source]** (Geo-Heritage of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh: The Third Eye, pp. 129-143)
- INTACH's role would include training village guides, providing them literature, and defining their responsibilities, while also drawing on cultural activities in the village to shape conservation plans. **[THIN — single source]** (Geo-Heritage of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh: The Third Eye, pp. 129-143)

## Engaging the Village Community
- Local and national authorities currently lack awareness of geo-heritage conservation, and bodies like the gram panchayat need sustained sensitization and training, a slow process requiring agencies like INTACH for hand-holding. **[THIN — single source]** (Geo-Heritage of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh: The Third Eye, pp. 146-147)
- Recommended community strategies include early engagement through awareness campaigns, conducting heritage audits with community volunteers, incorporating local and indigenous knowledge into planning, and developing inclusive communication strategies. **[THIN — single source]** (Geo-Heritage of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh: The Third Eye, pp. 146-150)

## Toward Sustainable Geo-Heritage Tourism
- The trek to the hill's natural rock arch and caves, with its dramatic sandstone cliffs and panoramic views, has real potential to draw geological and historical tourism if paired with proper infrastructure and conservation. **[THIN — single source]** (Geo-Heritage of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh: The Third Eye, pp. 144-146)
- Realizing this potential requires a stepwise process beginning with identification of significant geo-heritage sites and thorough assessment of their biodiversity and visitor appeal before development proceeds. **[THIN — single source]** (Geo-Heritage of Udaypur, Vidisha District, Madhya Pradesh: The Third Eye, pp. 146-147)
