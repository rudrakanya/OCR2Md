# Outline: C16 — Conquests and Regimes: Decline of the Socio-Economic Hub of Madhya Bharat

**Scope note:** Owns post-Paramara decline: invasions, Malwa Sultanate, medieval disruption. Receives from C05; hands off to C17 for modern/institutional history.

**Thesis:** The decline of Madhya Bharat's socio-economic hub after the Paramara period was driven by successive waves of conquest—Rajput fragmentation, Muslim invasions culminating in the Delhi Sultanate's annexation of Malwa, the rise and consolidation of the Malwa Sultanate at Mandu, and finally Mughal and Maratha conquest—each disrupting the region's temple-centered economy, political stability, and monument-building traditions.

## The Paramara Zenith and the Seeds of Fragmentation
- The Paramara dynasty, having shifted from Gujarat to Malwa, reached its peak under Munja and especially Bhoja, whose patronage of art, architecture and Sanskrit learning made Malwa's fame eternal even as both rulers met violent or tragic ends in war. (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 34-35; Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 34-36)
- After Bhoja, Paramara power steadily weakened through repeated wars with the Calukyas, Caulukyas, Hoysalas and Candellas, with Malwa repeatedly conquered and reconquered by rival dynasties before its final dismemberment. (Ganguly, D. C., History of the Paramāra Dynasty, pp. 10-14; Ganguly, D. C., History of the Paramāra Dynasty, pp. 54-56)
- The Kacchavaha house simultaneously rose to prominence in northern Madhya Bharat, dividing the region into the two rival kingdoms of Malwa and Gwalior. (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 34-35; Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 34-36)

## The First Muslim Invasions and the Fall of Gwalior and Malwa
- Mahmud of Ghazni's invasions from 1019 targeted Gwalior, exacting tribute without annexation, but later Muslim conquerors—Mohammad Ghori, Qutubuddin, and Itutmish—repeatedly besieged and eventually absorbed Gwalior into the Delhi Sultanate by 1231. **[THIN — single source]** (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 37-38)
- The Paramara power in Malwa was finally crushed at Mandu by Ain-ul-Mulk, a general of Alauddin Khilji, in 1305, making Malwa a province of the Delhi Sultanate. (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, p. 130; Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 128-129)
- Devapala's Paramara kingdom faced Moslem incursions from the north-eastern frontier as early as the 1190s-1220s, including Iyal-Timish's capture of the fort of Mando (Mandasor) in 1226. **[THIN — single source]** (Ganguly, D. C., History of the Paramāra Dynasty, pp. 224-230)

## The Rise of the Independent Malwa Sultanate
- Following the chaos of Timur's invasion of Delhi, Dilawar Khan Ghori declared independence in Malwa in 1401 with Dhar as capital, and his son Hoshang Shah shifted the capital to Mandu in 1405, fortifying the hill fort. (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, p. 130; Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 128-129)
- The Khilji dynasty of Mandu, beginning with Mahmud Shah after the poisoning of Gazni Khan in 1436, built Malwa into a powerful and prosperous kingdom over a 33-year reign, though later rulers proved progressively weaker. (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 37-39; Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, p. 130)
- Under the weak Mahmud II, domestic feuds and the overweening influence of the Rajput chief Medini Ray destabilized Malwa, prompting Mahmud to seek Gujarat's aid to oust him. **[THIN — single source]** (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 37-40)

## Annexation by Gujarat and the Interregnum of Qadir Shah
- In 1526 Bahadur Shah of Gujarat invaded Malwa, captured Mandu, imprisoned Mahmud II, and annexed the Malwa Sultanate to Gujarat, ending indigenous rule. (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 37-40; Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, p. 46)
- During his 1531 campaign Bahadur Shah sacked Bhilsa, destroying temples and building mosques, illustrating the disruptive religious and physical impact of these conquests on the region's older monuments. **[THIN — single source]** (Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, p. 46)
- After Bahadur Shah's death, Mallu Khan seized power as Qadir Shah in 1536-37, consolidating local Rajput and Muslim support, but Malwa's political control remained contested and unstable until Sher Shah's invasion in 1542 ended his rule. (Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, p. 47; Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, pp. 47-48)

## Sher Shah, the Afghans, and Continued Rajput Resistance
- Sher Shah invaded and conquered Malwa in 1542, appointing Shuja Khan as governor, but Rajput resistance under Puranmal at Raisen, Bhilsa and Chanderi continued to contest Afghan control for years afterward. (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 40-41; Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, pp. 47-48)
- Shuja Khan's death in 1554 triggered a domestic feud among his sons, from which Baz Bahadur emerged as an independent ruler more devoted to music and his consort Rupmati than to governance or defense. **[THIN — single source]** (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 40-41)

## Mughal Conquest and the End of Independent Malwa
- Babar's 1528 capture of Gwalior brought northern Madhya Bharat permanently under Mughal control, while Humayun's 1534 conquest of Mandu temporarily ended Gujarat's hold on Malwa before Mallu Khan's resurgence. **[THIN — single source]** (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 40-41)
- In 1561 Akbar's general Adham Khan defeated Baz Bahadur at Sarangpur, definitively annexing Malwa to the Mughal empire, though Baz Bahadur briefly recovered it before Pir Muhammad Khan's forces prevailed. (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 130-131; Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, pp. 49-50)
- Mandu and the wider region then remained a settled part of the Mughal empire, visited by emperors such as Akbar and Jehangir, until the Marathas captured the fort in 1732. **[THIN — single source]** (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 130-131)

## Maratha Ascendancy and the Transition Out of Sultanate Rule
- Maratha cavalry under Ranoji Scindia and Malharrao Holkar, and the Peshwa Bajirao I's decisive 1736 victory near the imperial capital, secured Maratha sovereignty over the Chambal-Narbada territory that constituted Madhya Bharat, carving out the principalities that would later form Gwalior and Indore. **[THIN — single source]** (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 40-44)
