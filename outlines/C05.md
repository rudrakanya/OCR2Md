# Outline: C05 — From Avanti to Malwa: Dynasties, Trade Routes and Sacred Cities

**Scope note:** Owns regional political evolution (Avanti to Malwa, pre-Paramara dynasties, trade routes, sacred cities) -- the centuries between C04's prehistory and C06's Paramaras specifically. Receives from C04; hands off to C06 once the Paramaras themselves are the subject, and to C16 for the post-Paramara decline that follows.

**Thesis:** Between the prehistoric horizon and the rise of the Paramaras, the region later called Malwa was shaped by successive dynasties—Haihayas, Mauryas, Shungas, Satavahanas, Kshatrapas, Guptas, Kalachuris and Pratiharas—whose control of sacred cities like Ujjain and Vidisha and the great north-south trade corridor through them gradually built the political and religious infrastructure that the Paramaras would inherit.

## Legendary Origins: Haihayas, Yadavas, and the Naming of Avanti
- Ancient tradition traces the region's rule to King Yayati's son Yadu, whose descendants split into the Yadavas and Haihayas, with the Haihayas dominating the Madhya Bharat region and later giving the territory the name Avanti after one of Kartavirya Arjuna's five sons. **[THIN — single source]** (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 26-28)
- The Haihayas, associated with the founding of Mahishmati, were considered the ancestral Kshatriya stock of the Avantis, and Ujjayini's name itself was linked in later Puranic legend to Shiva's mythic victory over the demon Tripura. **[THIN — single source]** (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 125-126)

## Vidisha and Ujjain as Sacred and Political Capitals
- Vidisha, identified with ancient Besnagar, appears in the Ramayana and Mahabharata and is called a tirth in the Skanda Purana, marking it early as both a political center and a place of religious sanctity. **[THIN — single source]** (Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, pp. 13-14)
- Under the Shungas, Vidisha served as a viceregal capital—Agnimitra governed there under his father Pushyamitra—and the Besnagar Heliodorus pillar records a Greek ambassador's devotion to Vasudeva, showing the city's role as a center of Bhagavata worship amid Buddhism's spread. **[THIN — single source]** (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 28-29)
- Ujjain grew from a capital of the Pradyotas in the Buddha's time into a great trading center, later a Mauryan viceroyalty under prince Ashoka, and remained a seat of viceroyalty under the Guptas. **[THIN — single source]** (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 125-126)

## Successive Empires Contend for Malwa (c. 2nd century BC–7th century AD)
- After the Mauryan and Shunga periods, the Satavahana ruler Simuka extirpated the last Shunga and Kanva rulers and brought the Vidisha region under Satavahana influence, before Kanishka's Kushan empire absorbed northern Madhya Bharat. **[THIN — single source]** (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 29-31)
- From roughly the first century BC to the second century AD, the Western Kshatrapas (Ksaharata and Kardamaka houses) and the Satavahanas fought repeatedly over Malwa, with Vidisha changing hands between them and Nahapana's son-in-law Ushavadata claiming mastery of the whole region. **[THIN — single source]** (Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, pp. 38-39)
- Broader trade and cultural networks linking the Kushana and Satavahana realms to Roman and Mediterranean commerce, and the classical flourishing of the Gupta era, set the stage for later medieval developments in the region. **[THIN — single source]** (Hardy, Adam, The Temple Architecture of India, pp. 26-28)
- In the later sixth and early seventh centuries, the early Kalachuri kings Krishnaraja, Sankaragana and Buddharaja, claiming Haihaya descent, ruled from Vidisha until the Chalukya king Mangalesha defeated Buddharaja. **[THIN — single source]** (Government of Madhya Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh District Gazetteers: Vidisha, pp. 41-42)

## The Trade Routes That Bound the Region Together
- Vidisha (Bhilsa), Ujjain and Mahishmati formed the key links joining the western coast, the Gangetic plains and the Deccan, a role the region retained even as political power shifted from Magadha to Delhi in medieval times. **[THIN — single source]** (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 66-81)
- The prosperity of the Bhilsa tract, ancient Dasharna, depended on its position between the fertile Malwa plateau and the Gangetic plains, a geographic advantage reflected in the richness of its surviving monuments. **[THIN — single source]** (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 17-20)

## The Gurjara-Pratihara and Rashtrakuta Struggle for Malwa
- By the early eighth century the Pratiharas held the throne of Malwa, and through the later eighth and ninth centuries they fought the Palas and the Rashtrakutas for supremacy, eventually surrendering Malwa to Rashtrakuta control under a governor named Upendra. **[THIN — single source]** (Ganguly, D. C., History of the Paramāra Dynasty, pp. 6-7)
- Rashtrakuta king Govinda III accepted the submission of the Malwa king Nagabhata and later, per the Sanjan plates of 871 A.D., placed the territory under an officer's administration, establishing Rashtrakuta rule over Malwa after 808 A.D. **[THIN — single source]** (Ganguly, D. C., History of the Paramāra Dynasty, pp. 15-32)
- Pratihara king Mahipala reasserted authority over the Deccan disorder following Govinda IV's usurpation, and by 946 A.D. the Partabgarh inscription shows a Pratihara feudatory, Madhava, governing Ujjain and worshipping at the Mahakala temple, confirming renewed Pratihara supremacy over Malwa before its later collapse. (Ganguly, D. C., History of the Paramāra Dynasty, pp. 45-48; Ganguly, D. C., History of the Paramāra Dynasty, pp. 45-50)

## The Threshold of Paramara Rule
- The Paramara family began as a viceregal house of the Rashtrakutas of Manyakheta under Upendra in the late eighth/early ninth century, and gained independent control of Malwa as Pratihara and Rashtrakuta power both collapsed around the mid-tenth century, with Siyaka II emerging as the first independent Paramara ruler. (Patil, D. R., The Cultural Heritage of Madhya Bharat, pp. 34-35; Some Paramāra Temples, p. 8)
- **[→ see C06]** The detailed political history of the Paramara dynasty itself—Siyaka II, Munja, Bhoja, Udayaditya and their successors, their wars, temple patronage, and eventual struggles with the Chandellas, Chaulukyas and Chahamanas—belongs to the next chapter on the Paramaras.
- **[→ see C16]** The eventual decline and extinguishing of Paramara rule at Dhara under Gujarat and Delhi Sultanate pressure, including events like Iltutmish's 1234 sack of Bhilsa, belongs to the chapter on post-Paramara decline.
